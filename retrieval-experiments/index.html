Retrieval Experiments

This is the record of every retrieval experiment and its result: what was tried, what it scored, and what was decided. Add a new entry for every eval run that informs a decision, including the ones that fail.

Goal: LCG retrieval should beat qmd for the Liminis Notes agent.

Standard setup (unless an entry says otherwise)

Section titled “Standard setup (unless an entry says otherwise)”

  • Harness: lcg-eval retrieval --k 10 --embedding-profile bge-v1.5 --replay-cassette … --embedder-uds …

    • Extraction is replayed from a committed Haiku 4.5 cassette, so ingest is deterministic.
    • Runs with --no-grounded use label-based nDCG and need no judge.
    • Judged runs use a Sonnet 4.6 judge with a judge cache.

  • Embedder: BGE base v1.5, Core ML, in the LocalInference sidecar.

  • Corpora:

    • v2: meridian-notebook-2 / meridian-questions-4. 408 notes and 244 questions, of which 210 are scored.
      • Categories: direct, paraphrase, temporal, answer, multi-hop and passage, plus negatives.
      • Tune on v2 only.
    • v1: fernhill-notebook-1. 31 notes and 40 scored questions.
      • Categories: direct, paraphrase, temporal and answer. Direct questions are near the ceiling.
      • v1 is the holdout: report it for final settings and never tune on it.

  • Metric: nDCG@10, with paired-bootstrap 95% intervals. Treat a difference as real only when its interval excludes 0.

  • Artifacts: each run’s report.md / report.json is kept by the maintainer, outside the repo. The PR or issue comment linked in each entry has the full tables.

  • Methodology: see Retrieval-Quality Evaluation for the harness, corpora and question format.

  • Graph-QA results (E13 and later) come from lcg-eval graph-qa and nothing else (#821). There are two steps:

    1. Ingest v2 with retrieval --ontology crates/eval/fixtures/retrieval-v2/ontology-v3.1.yaml --keep-workspace <ws>.
      • Builds before #881 (merged 2026-11-22): --ontology never reached the spawned service, so those runs extracted free-form. Don’t reuse a workspace or cassette made with --ontology before that fix.
      • Entries affected: of the recorded entries, only E16. E13’s ontology graph came from a local probe that did extract under the ontology (28 relation types, cassette v2-haiku45-onto31).
      • Since #881: the harness checks the service’s ontology_hash before ingest and stops on a mismatch.
    2. Run graph-qa --workspace <ws> --plans crates/eval/fixtures/retrieval-v2/e13-v5-plans.json over the committed graph-questions.jsonl.

    Report route on E13 (all 129 questions) and on E13/test. Graph-QA uses answer accuracy, not nDCG. See Standard setup for graph-QA results.

E15: score separation and lexical-leg semantics (#863, probe on branch fabrik/issue-863 @ e9d5177f, v2, 2026-11-21). Measured, no product change

Section titled “E15: score separation and lexical-leg semantics (#863, probe on branch fabrik/issue-863 @ e9d5177f, v2, 2026-11-21). Measured, no product change”

The question (from #848): is #812’s absolute min_score floor (default 0.5) safe in hybrid mode? Related: does the lexical leg match any term or all terms (#843)?

  • Method:
    • lcg-eval retrieval-score-probe (eval-only) sends knowledge_search_passages with min_score = 0 in vector, hybrid and hybrid-belief modes, then again with min_score = 0.5. It records the top-10 score and similarity per query.
    • Service 0.19.1, BGE-base v1.5 Core ML, profile bge-v1.5, k = 10, all 408 notes ingested through a stub extractor that extracts nothing (the committed free-form cassette no longer replays on this branch; passage search reads only Episodic nodes).
    • Three query sets: answerable (140: direct, paraphrase, answer, passage), hard negatives (the 34 negative questions) and off-topic (34 constructed queries in offtopic-queries.jsonl: 16 unrelated-domain, 8 gibberish, 10 common-words-only).
    • The FTS question was checked by running QUERY_FTS_INDEX through raw Cypher on the ingested corpus, with and without conjunctive := true.
  • Top-1 vector cosine (hybrid’s best vector cosine is within 0.003):

  • Hybrid’s extra hits are BM25 matches for unrelated-domain queries, tagged similarity == null with a fused score of about 0.0067. On answerable queries, the 140 lexical-only hits that survive the floor contain 6 relevant notes (4%); the 1,260 vector-backed hits contain 139 (11%).
  • The best single cosine threshold is 0.548 (keeps 91.4% of answerable, admits 11.8% of off-topic). No threshold separates the hard negatives.
  • FTS leg: cedar 34 matches; zzqxvk 0; cedar zzqxvk 34 by default and 0 with conjunctive := true. The shipped leg is any-term (OR). No E3 variant changed this.
  • Mergers (for #849): a name-only pair scan of the E13 graphs found 0 of 48 skeleton entities merged (free-form and ontology v3.1), 0 names on more than one node, 8 and 28 distinct node pairs at name cosine ≥ 0.85, and 13 and 17 exact-duplicate edge groups (by design: ingest does not deduplicate edges).
  • Learned:
    • The 0.5 floor is safe on v2 with this embedder (99.3% of answerable top-1s kept) but is not an abstention mechanism, and it is weaker in hybrid mode than in vector mode because the BM25 leg is never floored.
    • Hybrid’s fused score is not a relevance and no floor on it is meaningful; similarity is the relevance signal.
    • The margin is thin: the lowest answerable top-1 is 0.475 and the 10th percentile 0.554. The 0.5 default came from a different embedder run (#811), so it needs remeasuring per embedder.
    • Near-domain negatives score above the answerable median in every mode.
    • Limits: synthetic corpus, 34 hand-written off-topic queries, one embedder, rerank leg not measured.
  • Decision: no default changes. Follow-ups are recommended in docs/research/webbrain-retrieval-suggestions.md: a work issue for per-leg evidence on hybrid passages (#848), a probe (E16) of an all-terms and a “BM25 must agree” lexical leg (#843), declining #849, and a work issue for lcg-eval retrieval input digests and --compare (#847).

E14: LLM note summaries as extra retrieval units (offline probe, main @ 86f135dc service, v2, 2026-11-21). Promising for paraphrase, borderline overall

Section titled “E14: LLM note summaries as extra retrieval units (offline probe, main @ 86f135dc service, v2, 2026-11-21). Promising for paraphrase, borderline overall”

The question: does an LLM-written summary per note, indexed beside the raw passages, close the paraphrase gap (0.328 against qmd-rerank’s 0.475)? The idea comes from a review of WebBrain’s retrieval design (#843–#849 are the related suggestions). This is an offline probe with no LCG change.

  • Method:
    • Haiku 4.5 wrote one summary per note (408 calls): 3–6 plain sentences plus one key-concepts sentence, no headings. BGE embeds them.
    • The baseline (a) is shipped knowledge_search_passages top 10 with belief_aware and rerank, on a copy of the free-form v2 workspace. It reproduces the record: 0.758 against 0.759, and the default 0.653 against 0.653.
    • Note-level nDCG@10 by lcg-eval’s rules.
    • The mxbai rerank sidecar scored the summaries.

  • Result:
    • RRF fusion loses. (c) is −0.081 [−0.109, −0.054] overall and −0.093 on temporal. Summaries carry no supersession signal, so (b) is −0.22 on temporal.
    • Summaries as rerank candidates help paraphrase. (d) against its own control (d0) is +0.129 [+0.029, +0.233] on paraphrase, +0.021 [−0.005, +0.047] overall, and −0.034 [−0.101, +0.033] on temporal. Its paraphrase score of 0.526 is above qmd-rerank’s 0.475.
    • Kept inside LCG’s belief-aware order (d2):
      • Overall +0.014 [−0.003, +0.033].
      • Paraphrase +0.084 [+0.002, +0.181]. That’s 7 wins and 6 losses of 40, so it’s fragile.
      • Temporal unchanged.
      • Multi-hop −0.023 [−0.046, −0.003], because summary notes push passages out of the top 10.
  • Learned:
    • Re-scoring LCG’s passages with the raw reranker alone (d0) loses 0.048 overall against the shipped ranking, so LCG’s belief blend is worth about 0.05.
    • The (d2) merge is crude. A proper version puts summary units into LCG’s own candidate pool before the belief-aware rerank.
    • There’s no dev/test split for these questions, so this is tuned on v2.
  • Decision: it meets the bar only at the margin, through (d2) on paraphrase. That’s worth an implementation probe in LCG: summary passages as rerank candidates inside knowledge_search_passages, with belief-aware ordering, and a citation mapped back to the source passage. Not filed yet.

E13: graph multi-hop probes and an ontology (#815, local probe on main @ 19759906, v2). Promising, not shipped

Section titled “E13: graph multi-hop probes and an ontology (#815, local probe on main @ 19759906, v2). Promising, not shipped”

The question: does the graph answer what chunk search can’t (chains, joins, inverse lookups), and does an ontology make it usable?

  • Free-form graph audit (v2, standard cassette):
    • 2,016 edges carry 1,223 distinct relation types.
    • Hub entities have 80–115 edges.
    • The facts for the 15 real chain questions are present: 15/15 entities resolve and hop 1 is there for 15/15, hop 2 for 12/15.
    • Question-guided walks found the answer in the top fact for at most 7/15, against 1/15 for flat fact search.
    • knowledge_canonicalize_relations typed only 26–28% of edges lexically.
  • Ontology v3 → v3.1. It was written from a purpose statement and 25+4 competency questions (CQs), not from the corpus. Fit was judged by blind classifier agents given only the purpose and the type menu.
    • Bar: ≥ 85% of purpose-relevant (“core”) facts typed on both corpora, and κ ≥ 0.8.
    • v3: 93–98% core typed on v2, κ 0.87, but only 45% on v1. Missing: roles and titles, plans and schedules, proposals, replacement.
    • v3.1 (+HAS_ROLE, SCHEDULED_FOR, PROPOSED, wider SUPERSEDES; 27 types): 97% / 97% on a fresh v2 sample and 98% on v1, κ 0.82, with incidental typing at 17–24%. The v1 figure is optimistic, since those facts were read while revising.
  • Live re-extraction of v2 under v3.1 (strict), Haiku 4.5 via subscription:
    • 3,675 calls with 0 errors. The new cassette is v2-haiku45-onto31.
    • Result: 2,612 edges over 28 types, of which 74 are UNCLASSIFIED (3%). Nodes went from 769 to 1,202, so entity splitting needs a look.
    • lcg-entities-facts nDCG 0.283 → 0.294 (noise). Passages are unaffected.
  • Graph-native question set: 129 questions in 13 families, shapes from the CQs and gold from the skeleton.
    • Metric “complete”: every hop has a gold note among the returned evidence.
    • Metric “answer”: every gold answer appears in the returned facts.
    • Passages are scored strictly: all gold notes in the top 10.
    • The walk is LLM-free: exact-name seed, then at each hop the top 3 edges by cosine (10 for lists and joins). “Typed” filters each hop to its CQ relation types.

  • Learned:
    • Chunk search can’t do chains or joins: lcg-passages gets 0/24 on 3-hop and 0/14 on joins, with complete evidence for 41 of 129 questions.
    • A typed walk over the ontology graph gets complete evidence for 104/129 and finds the answer for 119/129, returning about 6 facts against 10 passages.
    • The ontology is the lever. Typing the free-form graph after the fact doesn’t help (51, which falls below the untyped walk’s 69). Extracting under the ontology does (104).
    • Passages still win change history (12/12 against 9/12), so route by question type.
  • Caveats:
    • Step 1’s walk plan is oracle and “answer found” is substring presence. Step 2 addresses both.
    • v2 is nearly one-to-one (each supplier serves one project), which understates aggregation.
    • The walk and the --ontology hook are local patches, not in the repo.
  • Step 2: answer accuracy with a planner and a reader. Haiku 4.5 via the subscription is both the planner and the reader, and both are cached.
    • The planner sees only the question and the relation-type menu: no families, no gold. It returns a route (graph or passages), a start entity and typed hops.
    • Every runner’s context goes to the same reader.
    • Scoring: first answer matches for single answers, set F1 for lists and joins, exact in-order match for history.
