# Journal — 16 July 2025

Wednesday, and a day that started with the printer jamming and ended up being more productive than that omen suggested. Writing this up in the evening with a cup of tea going cold beside me.

**Morning stand-in.** The usual early check-in was short. The main point was that the inventory side has been wrestling with some awkward data for a few weeks, and today was the first time anyone felt they had a handle on it. The Orbit Core backlog stood at 16 open tickets at the end of the sprint, which is the lowest it has been in a long time and a nice contrast to the stretch where it seemed to double every other week. Part of the credit goes to a clear-out of tickets that had been duplicated or overtaken by events, and part goes to people simply picking things up and finishing them. Nobody wanted to make a fuss in case it jinxed the trend, but I think it deserves a line in the log.

**Mid-morning, on the routing side.** A conversation about the group's plans for getting together. The Quill Core offsite will take place in Lisbon. It is still early days and the detail is thin, but the idea is to combine a couple of working sessions with time for the team to talk through how the year has gone. Some people are keen on travel and some would rather not, so there was a gentle discussion about making the working part accessible to those who cannot attend in person. Nothing is settled, and the suggestion of an online option was noted and left for later.

**Midday chores.** A few small jobs that had been sitting on my list:

- Went through the shared drive and removed duplicate files. There were more than I expected, including several versions of the same planning document with confusingly similar names.
- Updated the access list for the team's tools, which had a couple of out-of-date entries.
- Labelled the cables in the cupboard, a task that sounds trivial and is oddly satisfying.
- Sent a reminder about the written updates ahead of the next sync.

**Afternoon meeting.** The longest of the day. We talked about how work gets prioritised when two groups both think theirs is the most urgent. There was no clever answer. The pragmatic outcome was that when priorities collide, the two group leads talk it through directly and write down what they decided, rather than bouncing it through several intermediaries. It sounds obvious, but we have lost days to the alternative. A related point was that decisions of this sort should be recorded where everybody can see them, so the reasoning is not lost.

An observation that came up in the discussion and that I want to preserve: most of our delays are not caused by work being hard but by waiting. Waiting for a reply, waiting for someone to be free, waiting for a decision that everyone assumes someone else is making. If we could cut the waiting, the pace would improve with no extra effort. A modest first step is to put a name and a date next to any decision that is pending, so there is someone to chase.

**End of day.** Tidied my desk, wrote tomorrow's list and shut down. The printer was fixed by somebody pulling out a crumpled page from deep inside, which felt like a metaphor for something.

Tomorrow: the data issue, one more look, and the access list follow-up.

## Thursday and a long to-do list

Thursday turned into a day of small tasks stacked on top of each other, which is the kind of day I find oddly restful. Nothing large, nothing urgent, just a steady supply of things to tick off.

I started by reading through the previous entry, mostly to remind myself what I had promised to follow up. The data problem on the inventory side was first on the list. I spent the best part of the morning on it with a colleague, and we found that the odd readings came from two sources disagreeing about how to round a measurement. It sounds silly, and it is, but it was responsible for a stubborn discrepancy that had been bothering people for weeks. We agreed on a single convention and wrote it into the shared definitions page so that nobody has to rediscover it. The Orbit Core group will now apply the same rule wherever it appears, and we will see whether the discrepancy disappears from the weekly figures.

After lunch I turned to the routing group's plans. There was an informal chat about how to share the work before the Quill Core trip, particularly the preparation that always falls on the same two or three people. A suggestion that I liked was to rotate the preparation tasks so that everyone gets a go and nobody becomes the permanent organiser by default. The same logic applies to taking notes at meetings, which has quietly turned into one person's job. We will try a rota, and if it works we will extend it.

The afternoon included a review of how we handle documentation for the routing side, which is a bit scattered. There are three or four places where similar information lives, and it is anyone's guess which is current. We decided on one canonical location and agreed that the others will carry a pointer and nothing else. Consolidating will take a little time, but it will pay back quickly in fewer misunderstandings.

Some other things worth noting from the day:

- A new starter asked a very good question about why we do something a particular way, and nobody could give a convincing answer. That is a prompt to look at whether the practice is still needed.
- The kitchen rota has drifted again. Someone has offered to refresh it, and I suspect it will fade within a month, but let us try.
- I finally booked the dental appointment I had been avoiding. Not work, but worth recording as a victory.

Reflections at the end of the day. I notice that when I plan the day in advance and keep the list short, I finish more than when I let the day be shaped by whatever lands in my inbox. It is not a new insight, but I keep needing to relearn it. Writing it here is partly a note to my future self.

Plan for tomorrow: confirm the rounding convention has taken hold, draft the rotation for preparation tasks, and begin moving the routing documentation into one place. If the printer misbehaves again, I will take it as a sign to go home early.
