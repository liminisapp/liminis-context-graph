# Customer interviews: the questions that matter

A good customer interview feels more like a conversation than a survey. The aim is to learn how people actually behave, not to collect polite opinions about an idea. That distinction shapes everything about which questions to ask.

## Ask about the past, not the future

People are poor predictors of their own behaviour. Ask whether they would use a feature and most will say yes; ask when they last faced the problem and what they did about it, and you will learn far more. Specific stories, with details such as what they tried and what went wrong, are worth many general statements.

## Follow the energy

When a respondent speeds up, sighs or changes tone, that is usually a sign something matters. Resist the urge to return to the script. A short follow-up such as "tell me more about that" often unlocks the most useful part of the interview.

## Avoid leading the witness

Questions that contain the answer, or hint at the one you hope for, produce flattering but useless replies. Keep wording neutral, and leave silences alone. People tend to fill a pause with something honest.

## Listen for workarounds

The clearest evidence of a real problem is a clumsy workaround: a spreadsheet taped onto a process, a reminder scribbled on paper. Ask what people do today, and who else is involved.

## Close well

End by asking what you should have asked and who else to talk to. Then write up the notes the same day, while the details are fresh, and share them with the team.
