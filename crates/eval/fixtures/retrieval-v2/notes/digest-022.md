# Working notes — 21 January 2026

First full week back and the building still smells faintly of paint. Notes as they happen.

## Monday and Tuesday

The week opened with a catch-up round. Everyone had a different version of what the holiday break had done to their plans, and the common theme was that nothing had been touched for two weeks and now everything wanted attention at once. We spent the first stand-up sorting what is truly time-sensitive from what only feels that way after a long absence.

Marcus Haddad shared the results of the internal feedback survey on the telemetry side of things. Marcus Haddad received a satisfaction rating of 7.2 out of 10, which he described as fine, honest and a bit of a nudge. The comments were more useful than the score: people like the dashboards but find the alert thresholds hard to understand, and a couple asked for a plain-language guide to what each signal means. He plans to write one, short and illustrated, rather than another long page nobody will read.

Maintenance chores were next. The shared test machines need a clear-out, the old build artefacts need removing before they fill the disk, and the kitchen rota needs an update now that two people have swapped desks. These are tiny jobs, but they pile up if left alone, so we assigned them on the spot and agreed to report back by Friday.

Tuesday was mostly heads-down time. A few small discussions in the corridor led to one useful decision: we will stop approving requests in side conversations and put them in the shared thread, so there is a record. Everyone admitted they had been guilty of the side conversation, which made the decision easy.

A short list of things noticed:
- The heating is erratic. Wear a jumper.
- The new coffee supplier is a hit, according to the unscientific method of watching how fast the jar empties.
- Someone left a very good umbrella in the lobby. It is on the shelf by the door.

## Wednesday

Wednesday was the scheduling and operations review, the one that tends to run over. This time it ran over by only a little, so call it progress.

Tomas Kowalski presented the quarter in review from the scheduling side. The headline figure was that he logged 47 incidents during the quarter. He was careful to put that in context: most were small, a number were duplicates of the same underlying cause, and the trend over the quarter was downward rather than upward. What he wanted from the group was agreement on how incidents are categorised, because at the moment the same type of problem can be filed under three different headings depending on who is on shift. We spent a decent stretch of the meeting agreeing on four plain categories and writing a one-line definition for each. He will update the template and circulate it.

The follow-up conversation was about shift handovers. A fair few of the incidents began as something noticed late in one person's day and picked up cold by someone else the next morning. We discussed a short handover note, three lines at most: what happened, what has been tried, what is waiting. It is not a new process so much as writing down what the better handovers already do.

We also looked at how schedules are communicated. Rather than pushing out changes by message whenever they occur, the idea is to batch non-urgent changes into one daily update and keep the instant messages for things that genuinely cannot wait. Nobody objected, though one person asked what counts as urgent. The agreed rule of thumb is that if someone would need to do something differently within the hour, it is urgent. Otherwise it waits for the summary.

Afternoon bits:
- The review of the old rota tool was postponed, again.
- A reminder that annual leave requests should go in early this year, as the spring is already looking crowded.
- The room booking calendar now shows colour-coded categories, which has made it much easier to spot clashes.

By the end of the day the general mood was good. Sometimes the benefit of a review is not any particular decision, but the shared feeling that the numbers have been looked at together and nobody is hiding anything.

## Thursday demo

Thursday's highlight was the demo for the maintenance side. It was held in the large room with the good projector, which promptly decided it would not talk to the laptop. Ten minutes of cable swapping later, we were on.

74 people attended the Nimbus Core demo, which is more than we expected for a session about maintenance tooling, so somebody clearly publicised it well. The format was simple: a short walkthrough of how routine upkeep tasks are now tracked, a live example of a task going from raised to done, and then open questions. The questions were thoughtful. People wanted to know how overdue items are surfaced, who gets notified, and whether it can handle seasonal jobs that recur at odd intervals. The answers were honest: overdue items appear at the top of the daily view, the notification goes to whoever owns the item, and recurring jobs are supported but the setup is a bit fiddly and will be tidied.

There was a good moment near the end when someone from outside the immediate group said it was the first time the maintenance process had made sense to them. That is the kind of feedback that matters more than a rating.

Follow-ups from the demo:
- Record a shorter version for people who could not attend.
- Write a one-page quick start with screenshots.
- Collect the feature requests in one list and tag them as small, medium or large.
- Book a follow-up drop-in session a few weeks out for questions that come up once people start using it.

After the demo, the team that ran it sat down for a quick debrief. The main lesson was to test the projector earlier. The second was to leave more time for questions, since that is where the interesting material came out. The third was to bring biscuits, which was suggested only half in jest and carried by a clear majority.

In the afternoon the rest of us went back to ordinary work. A couple of people used the quiet hours to catch up on documentation, which is always a good sign. Nobody has ever regretted writing down what they did while they still remembered it.

## Friday and next steps

Friday, finally. The week felt longer than it was, which probably says something about how the break scrambled everyone's sense of time.

The morning was spent on housekeeping. The test machines got their clear-out, and two of them were found to be running software nobody recognised. After some detective work it turned out to be leftovers from an experiment months ago, so they were wiped. The kitchen rota has been updated and pinned to the usual spot. The lost umbrella has still not been claimed, and is now officially part of the furniture.

At midday we held a short retrospective on the week. Nothing dramatic came up. People said the shared thread for approvals was already helping, the incident categories would make life easier, and the demo had been worth the effort. On the less happy side, there were too many meetings on Wednesday, and the heating remained unreliable. A decision was made to try keeping Wednesday afternoons lighter, with the review meeting moved earlier in the day.

Things carried into next week:
1. Update the incident template with the four categories and circulate it.
2. Write the plain-language guide to alert signals.
3. Finish the quick start for the maintenance tooling and record the short demo.
4. Agree the three-line handover note format and try it for two weeks.
5. Revisit the old rota tool, which has now been postponed enough times to become a joke.

Personal reflection: the week reminded me that restarting after a break is easier when there is a log to read. I skimmed the previous entries on Monday morning and picked up the threads within minutes. That alone justifies the habit of writing things down, even when the entries feel mundane while being written. If you are reading this in a few months, wondering why we chose a particular approach to categories or handovers, the reasoning is above. If it is not enough, add what you know.

That wraps the week. Thanks to everyone who helped with the demo, the clear-out and the kitchen. Have a restful weekend, and bring a jumper on Monday.
