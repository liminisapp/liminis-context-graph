# Journal — 1 March 2025

Saturday morning, house quiet, so I am using the time to get the week's notes down before they blur into the next one. It was a decent week by any standard, though it did not feel like one while I was in it.

The big theme was billing. Cedar Core spent most of the week on the changeover to a tidier way of recording adjustments, which sounds trivial and is anything but. Every adjustment used to be entered by hand with a free-text reason, which meant that the reasons were inconsistent and impossible to search. The new approach uses a short list of standard reasons, with a free-text box for anything that does not fit. The first few days produced the expected grumbling, followed by the equally expected relief once people saw how much faster it was. Ines Lindqvist ran the training sessions herself and handled the objections with a patience I admire. The most common was "but my case is special", and her standard reply was to show that the box for special cases still exists.

Routing was the other busy area. Quill Core has been revisiting how requests are sent to the right place, particularly the awkward ones that belong to two groups at once. At present those get bounced around, sometimes for days. The proposal is to nominate a single owner for every request at the point it arrives, even if that owner immediately hands part of it off. It is the sort of change that costs nothing and removes a surprising amount of frustration. I was asked for an opinion and gave a cautious yes, with the condition that the owner is named in the request, so that anyone looking at it can see at once who is responsible.

Ordinary matters of the week:

- The Monday meeting was moved to Tuesday because of a clash, and nobody noticed the difference.
- We tried a shorter format for the Thursday catch-up, with each person getting a minute and a half. It worked better than expected, though a few people wanted more time to explain things properly.
- The shared drive was tidied by a volunteer, who deserves a medal and will probably get a biscuit.
- A new set of labels for the storage room has been ordered, which should end the long argument about where the spare cables live.

One decision to note: we will no longer accept change requests by chat. The reasoning is simple. Requests made in chat get lost, and then people are surprised when nothing happens. Everything now goes through the shared request page, even small things, and the person who receives a request replies there so that the history is in one place. It will take some getting used to, and I expect a few lapses in the first weeks. A gentle reminder, not a scolding, is the plan.

I have also been thinking about how to make the log itself more useful. At the moment it records what happened, but not what we expected to happen, which makes it hard to learn anything. A line at the start of each week saying what we hope to get done would let us compare the two. I will try that on Monday and see whether it adds anything or just adds work.

The rest of the weekend is for the garden, and a long walk if the weather behaves. Next entry soon.

## Figures and follow-through

A second sitting, on Sunday, mostly to record the figures that arrived late on Friday and to think about what they mean.

The audit results came in over the course of the afternoon. Cedar Core scored 92 out of 100 in the internal audit, and Quill Core scored 96 out of 100 in the internal audit. Both are strong results, and the two groups took them with admirable restraint. The comments attached to them were short. For billing, the main suggestion was to keep records of adjustments in one place; fortunately that is exactly what the new standard-reasons approach does, so the audit and the changeover happen to be pushing in the same direction. For routing, the observation was that the process works well when the request is clear and less well when it is ambiguous, which is the very problem the single-owner proposal is meant to address. It is gratifying when external feedback lines up with changes you were already planning, though I try not to read too much into it.

On the incidents side, Ines Lindqvist logged 19 incidents during the quarter. Most were routine matters that needed a quick correction, and a few were more involved. What I appreciated was her habit of writing a two-line note on each one explaining what went wrong and what she changed afterwards. Those notes are the real value, because they turn a list of incidents into something that can be learned from. She has offered to share the notes with the other groups, and I have encouraged her to do so. I would also like us to agree on a standard way of recording incidents so that her notes can be compared with others without much translation.

A few thoughts about follow-through, since good intentions are not enough:

- The shared request page needs an owner. Without one it will decay, so we will name someone this week.
- The training for the new adjustment process should be repeated for anyone who missed it, preferably in small groups.
- The single-owner proposal will be tried on a handful of requests first, and reviewed after a fortnight.
- The audit comments should go on the board, with a name against each one.

A short aside about how we talk about scores. High results can be as risky as low ones, because they tempt people to stop looking. I asked both groups to name one thing the audit did not test that they still worry about. The answers were thoughtful: one was about how well new starters understand the process, the other was about what happens when the main person is away. Those are exactly the things an audit cannot see, and exactly what the team should keep an eye on.

My plan for the week ahead is simple. Start Monday with a line about what I hope to achieve, as promised. Check on the request page. Make sure the training gets repeated. And, if I can, find ten minutes to thank the people who did the quiet, unglamorous work that produced these results. They will not ask for it, which is exactly why they should get it.
