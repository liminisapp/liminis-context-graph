# Operations diary — 7 April 2025

Monday morning, coffee in hand, writing up the weekend's loose ends and the plan for the week. It is mostly maintenance and stock, which is what keeps everything else from falling over.

The first item is the offsite. After a lot of back and forth the Nimbus Core offsite will take place in Leeds. The maintenance side of the team wanted a venue that is easy to reach from several directions, with enough room to spread out equipment and walk through a few runbooks together. Leeds suits that. We have not set an agenda; at the moment the only commitments are the place and a general intention to keep it practical. Somebody in finance asked about the budget, and the answer is that we will put together a rough estimate this week and see if it fits.

Inventory was the next thing on the list. Lena Moreau has been working through the spare parts shelves, and the picture is better than feared. A lot of items were simply in the wrong place rather than missing. The stock list is being cleaned up so that every location has a clear label and every label matches the spreadsheet. It is tedious, but a stock list you cannot trust is worse than none, because people stop checking it and start guessing.

Support for Juniper Core had a steady week. Tickets came in at a normal pace, nothing unusual, and the handover between shifts is smoother since we started leaving a three-line summary at the end of each day: what is open, what is waiting on someone, what needs attention first. It sounds trivial and it has removed a surprising number of "did anyone look at this?" messages.

Some smaller things worth writing down:

- The workshop tools were checked and two items were set aside for repair. Please do not borrow them until they are marked as fixed.
- The ordering process for consumables is being simplified. Instead of a form with many fields, a short message with the item, the quantity and the reason will do.
- A cleaning of the storage area is planned for Thursday. Anything left on the floor will be moved to a labelled box.
- Safety signs were reviewed. A couple are faded and will be replaced.

We spent time on what to do about recurring faults. Several issues come back every few weeks in slightly different clothing, and nobody has the time to trace them to a root cause. The proposal is to keep a simple log of repeats: what broke, what was done, whether it came back. After a month there should be enough of a pattern to decide where to dig. The log lives in the shared folder, with one line per event. No analysis needed yet, only recording.

There was also a short exchange about handover notes when someone goes on leave. Past experience says the person leaving writes a long note nobody reads. Better, we thought, to write a short note and be reachable for one quick question. Quality over volume.

I will wrap this part up by noting that the week looks manageable. If the stock tidy-up finishes on schedule and the offsite budget estimate gets done, we will be in good shape for the next planning round. More in the following sections.

## Retros, rotas and repairs

Continuing the diary with the middle of the week. This part is mostly about how the support group works and how we look back on it.

Juniper Core held its quarterly retrospective on 20 March 2026. I am recording that here because the date was confirmed this week and several people have asked when the next look-back falls. The format is the same as before: a relaxed hour, a shared document, three columns for what went well, what did not, and what to try. We have learned to keep the document short, since a long one gets skimmed and the same points come up again anyway. If anything from the earlier retro is still unresolved, it is carried over visibly rather than quietly dropped.

Leading up to that, the support team asked for a few things to be sorted out in the meantime. The main one is the tagging of tickets, which has become inconsistent over time. At present there are several tags that appear to mean much the same thing, and people choose between them more or less at random. A cleanup is overdue. The plan is to list all the tags, group the near-duplicates, and agree on one name for each group. Then a quick pass through open tickets to apply the new names. Not thrilling, but the reports will be much cleaner afterwards.

Maintenance had its own bits and pieces. Nimbus Core work this week centred on a recurring fault that keeps coming back. The repeat log we started has already paid for itself: it shows the fault tends to appear after a particular routine task, which is a lead nobody had noticed before. That gives us something concrete to test. Nobody is claiming it is the cause, only that it is worth ruling in or out.

A few other updates:

- The consumables order went out and the delivery is expected in the usual window.
- A reminder that anything borrowed from the stock shelves needs to be signed out, even for ten minutes. Lena Moreau has been chasing items that turned up in unexpected places, and the sign-out sheet is the least painful fix.
- The first aid kit was checked and restocked.
- Someone brought in a better label maker. The shelves are looking noticeably tidier.

We also had a worthwhile argument about on-call expectations. Some people feel they are always half-working during their week; others feel the load is fine. The difference seems to come down to whether you carry a device and how often it buzzes. We agreed to gather a few weeks of rough data about how often alerts actually require action. If most do not, the fix is to adjust the alerts rather than the rota.

Rotas more broadly are due for a refresh. The current one dates from before the last few people came on board, and it shows. A new version should account for leave, for people who prefer certain days, and for the plain fact that some tasks need two people and some do not. Whoever drafts it should circulate it early, because people are far more willing to accept a rota they have seen coming than one that appears fully formed.

One last point on repairs: the list of equipment waiting for fixing has grown longer than we like. We will pick the three items that block the most work and do those first, leaving the rest until there is breathing room. It is a modest plan but a realistic one.

## Feedback and wrap-up

The last part of this entry deals with feedback, which was not entirely comfortable reading, and with the remaining actions.

The inventory feedback survey came back this week. Lena Moreau received a satisfaction rating of 3.4 out of 10. That is a low number and nobody is pretending otherwise. What matters is why. The written comments were more specific than the score, and they pointed in a consistent direction: people could not find things, the stock list was out of date, and requests for parts went into a void with no sign of progress. None of that is a personal failing; it describes a process that was starved of attention for a long time. The tidy-up already under way is aimed at precisely those complaints, so with luck the next round will tell a different story.

To make sure we respond sensibly rather than defensively, we agreed on three steps. First, reply to every commenter who left a name, thanking them and saying what is changing. Second, publish a short list of what has been fixed so far, however small. Third, run a quick follow-up survey in a few weeks, with only two questions, so people actually answer. Low scores are useful when they lead to a visible change; they are only demoralising when they vanish into a folder.

On a related note, it was suggested that parts requests should have a visible status. At the moment someone asks, and then waits. A simple column on the shared sheet marking each request as received, in progress or done would remove most of the uncertainty. It costs almost nothing to maintain and it turns silence into information.

Meanwhile, back with the other threads:

- Nimbus Core maintenance will test the idea that the repeat fault follows a particular routine task. If it does, the routine can be adjusted.
- Juniper Core support will go ahead with the tag cleanup and will share the final list of names.
- The offsite planning continues. A rough budget is being drafted and will be circulated for comments.
- The rota refresh has a volunteer to draft it.

A few observations that did not fit elsewhere. Several people mentioned that the diary itself is useful as a record of why things were done, not only what. One said they had looked up an old entry to understand a decision and found it explained better than the ticket. That is encouraging, and it is a good reason to keep writing in plain language and include the reasoning, however brief.

We also talked about recognition. Not the formal kind, just the habit of saying thanks when someone fixes something tedious. The stock tidy-up, the label work and the sign-out sheet were all done without fanfare, and it is worth saying so. Rather than build a scheme, we will simply make a point of naming small wins in the diary, in one line each.

To close, here are the actions I will chase next week: send the survey replies, publish the fixed-so-far list, circulate the budget draft, and confirm the new rota. If any of those slip, I will say so in the next entry instead of letting them fade. That is the point of keeping the diary, after all.
