# Working notes — 16 October 2025

Thursday afternoon, post-lunch, and the notes have been sitting in a draft for two days. Time to turn the scribbles into something readable.

Most of the week was spent on three things: onboarding, stock, and the rhythm of our look-back meetings. I will take them in that order.

Onboarding first. The newcomer path for Fable Core has been through some small revisions, mostly to the wording. Anything that sounded like an instruction to "just" do something has been rewritten, because "just" tends to hide the hard part. We also added a short note on how to ask for help without feeling like you are interrupting. This was surprisingly popular; a few people said they wished it had existed when they joined. Our look-back for that project is the Fable Core quarterly retrospective, which is scheduled for 3 August 2027, and that date went into the shared calendar this week so nobody has to ask. It is a long way off, but the calendar entry also carries a link to the shared retro document, where people can drop thoughts as they occur instead of trying to remember them later.

Next, stock. Lena Moreau has been making headway on the inventory shelves. A handful of items turned out to be duplicated under different names in the sheet, which explained a few phantom shortages. Merging those was oddly satisfying. We agreed to standardise names using a simple pattern: what it is first, then what size or variant, then where it lives. The point is that a person who has never seen the sheet can guess what a row means.

Orbit Core also got attention, as it runs the inventory side. Its demo is coming up, and the team has been tidying the views so the data looks coherent. One of the practical decisions was to use real, current data in the demo instead of a hand-built example. It is riskier, because the data is messy, but it is also more honest and usually more interesting.

Other things worth a line:

- The shared drive structure was reviewed. Too many top-level folders; they will be grouped under four headings.
- The weekly summary email gets one more trim. Only decisions and open questions stay.
- Somebody suggested a standing slot every Friday for "things I noticed but did not fix". Low pressure, no commitment. We will try it.
- The kitchen restock has been delegated to whoever is in first. This will either work wonderfully or cause chaos.

A longer discussion was about how to keep retrospectives from becoming a lecture. The risk is that whoever organises it ends up talking most of the time. The idea that appealed to people was to write answers silently for five minutes, then read them aloud one at a time. Quiet people get heard, loud people get shorter, and the document ends up richer. We will try this at the next one.

We also checked in on workloads. A quick scan suggested nobody is dramatically overloaded, but a couple of people are carrying several small commitments that add up. The gentle remedy is to ask each person to list their commitments in one place so that the total is visible. People often underestimate how much they have taken on until they see it written down.

That covers the first half of the notes. The second half deals with numbers: backlog counts, attendance, and what we think they mean.

## Numbers, demos and loose ends

Part two, and the numbers I promised. I will add commentary where it helps and leave it out where the figures speak for themselves.

Starting with the backlog. At the end of the sprint the Lena Moreau backlog stood at 70 open tickets. That is a lot, though not catastrophic once you see what is in it. A fair share are small housekeeping items that never get urgent enough to do, a handful are duplicates waiting to be merged, and a few are genuine requests that have simply gone stale. We agreed to run a triage pass early next week: close what is obsolete, merge what is duplicated, and put a clear label on whatever remains so that the people responsible know it is theirs. The aim is not to hit a particular number but to make the list a trustworthy picture of what is really outstanding.

An important side effect of that triage is the cultural one. A backlog that nobody trusts is a backlog nobody reads, and then new items vanish into it. If we can keep the list honest for a few sprints, people might start using it again as a planning tool. That is the real prize.

Then the demo. 36 people attended the Orbit Core demo, a respectable turnout for an internal session on inventory. The demo used live data, as planned, and one of the live records turned out to be wrong, which led to the funniest moment of the afternoon and, more usefully, to a correction on the spot. Feedback was warm. The part people liked best was the search view, which makes it fast to find an item by a loose description rather than an exact name. The part people liked least was the export, which is clunky. We have noted that and will look at it.

Questions from the floor included:

- Can the views be filtered by location? Yes, and we should make that more obvious.
- What happens when two people edit the same record? Not entirely clear, so a short test is on the list.
- Is there an audit trail for changes? Partly. We will check what is actually recorded.
- Could Fable Core's newcomers get a read-only view for practice? It seems like a good idea and is being looked at.

Some chores that were not covered above:

1. Update the equipment sign-out sheet to include a return date.
2. Move the demo recording to the shared drive and add a short summary so people can skim it.
3. Check whether the retention settings on shared documents match what we think they are.
4. Write down the naming pattern for inventory items, so the next person to join does not have to reinvent it.

On reflection, it was a good week in the sense that the problems we found were ones we could name. A vague mess is harder to fix than a specific one, and by the end of the sprint most of our messes had labels attached. I would count that as progress, even if the backlog number does not yet show it.
