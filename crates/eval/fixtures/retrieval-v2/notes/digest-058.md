# Journal — 16 March 2026

Monday morning, rain on the windows, and the radiator finally working after a fortnight of sulking. Good conditions for catching up on the journal.

## Start of the week

We began with a short sync about what is coming up. The mood was cautious but not gloomy. Spring tends to bring a surge of requests, and the team would like to be ready rather than surprised. A couple of people volunteered to keep an eye on the incoming queue and flag anything that looks like it will need more than one pair of hands.

Sofia Petrov, who handles procurement, brought up the matter of the quarterly retrospective. Sofia Petrov will hold the quarterly retrospective on 25 April 2026, and she wants the agenda to include a proper look at how ordering and approvals have been flowing, since several people have felt that requests sit waiting longer than they should. She asked everyone to jot down examples between now and then, so the discussion can be about real cases and not vague impressions.

Meanwhile Fable Core, the onboarding project, is preparing for a new batch of joiners. The team is revisiting the welcome material and has promised to cut anything that nobody reads. It is a rare thing to hear a team volunteer to delete its own writing, so I am noting it here for posterity.

- Receipts and approvals now go through a single shared inbox instead of three separate threads. Try it for a month.
- We will not buy the larger whiteboard after all. The existing one is fine once someone cleans it.

A new person asked why we keep a paper calendar on the wall when everyone has a digital one. The answer, after some thought, was that the paper one shows the whole month at a glance and nobody has to log in to read it. It stays.

I also noticed that the shared kitchen has acquired a second kettle without anybody admitting to buying it. The team will not investigate.

More later in the week, assuming nothing dramatic happens.

## Wednesday, mostly questions

Today produced more questions than answers, which I am treating as progress of a sort.

The ordering process came up again in the corridor and then in a proper meeting. The core complaint is that nobody can see where a request is once it has been submitted. People send it off and then wonder. We sketched a plain status list with four states on the whiteboard: asked, approved, ordered, received. It is not a system, only a habit, but a visible habit might be enough. Sofia Petrov agreed to try it for the next few requests and report back on whether it saves any chasing.

For Fable Core, the discussion was about what a new joiner should be able to do by the end of their first week. We landed on something modest: log in everywhere, know who to ask about what, and have completed one small real task. Nobody wanted a long checklist. A short list that people actually finish beats a long one they abandon.

## Incidents and what we learned

A slightly more sober entry today. We went back over the quarter's incident log and talked about what it is telling us.

Fable Core logged 38 incidents during the quarter. Nobody was shocked by the figure, but it prompted a useful conversation about what counts as an incident in the first place. A few entries were really questions that arrived through the wrong channel, and a few were small slips that were fixed in minutes. Others deserved more attention than they got at the time. The team wants a clearer rule of thumb so the log stays meaningful, and so that a rise or fall in the count actually means something.

We sorted the entries loosely into three kinds: people who could not get started, people who could not find something, and people who were told two different things by two different sources. The third kind was the most irritating and the easiest to fix, since it mostly comes down to old documents that contradict newer ones. Cleaning those up is now a task with a name against it.

### What to do about it

- Review the log monthly rather than once a quarter, so patterns show up while they are still fresh.
- Add a short note to each entry saying what would have prevented it.
- Retire the documents that contradict each other, and put a date at the top of every one that remains.

### Procurement angle

Sofia Petrov noted that a couple of the incidents traced back to equipment arriving late, which left new joiners waiting for what they needed. That has now been added to the list of examples for the retrospective. It is a fair reminder that onboarding and ordering are tied together, and that a delay in one shows up as an incident in the other.

### Everyday matters

The cleaning rota was adjusted after a complaint that it fell unevenly. The new version is a rolling order, so everybody gets the awkward days eventually. A plant was adopted by the team. It has not yet been named, and nobody has the courage to suggest a name.

I will finish the day by drafting a short summary of the log review for anyone who missed the meeting. It will go in the shared folder tomorrow morning.

## Friday, winding down

The week ends on a calmer note than it began. Here is a short closing entry before everyone disappears into the weekend.

The summary of the log review went round this morning, and the replies were thoughtful. Two people suggested small changes to the wording, one asked for a clearer definition of "resolved", and one just wrote "thanks, this is useful" which, honestly, made my day. The tidied version is now in the shared folder, with the date at the top as agreed.

Sofia Petrov and a few others met briefly about the status-list habit for orders. The verdict after a few days of trying it: mildly helpful, mostly because it stops the same question being asked three times. It will continue as it is, and the team will decide in a few weeks whether anything more formal is needed. No new tools, no new forms.

In the onboarding corner, the Fable Core group went through the welcome material and cut a good chunk of it. What remains is shorter, plainer and easier to follow. They also added a "who to ask" page, which has been on everyone's wish list for ages. It is only a list of topics with a person or a channel next to each, but it is the thing newcomers always seem to want most.

### Decisions this week

- The status-list habit continues, informally.
- The Tuesday sync stays in the afternoon.
- Receipts go into the shared inbox the same day.
- The cupboard labels are finished, and the rule is: put it back where the label says.

### Loose ends

The missing charger turned up inside a bag belonging to someone who had no idea it was there. No harm done. The second kettle is still unexplained. A thank-you went out to the person who watered the plants during the dry spell.

### A last thought

Weeks like this are easy to undervalue. Nothing dramatic happened, but several small irritations were quietly removed, and that matters more than it sounds. I would like more of them. See everyone on Monday.
