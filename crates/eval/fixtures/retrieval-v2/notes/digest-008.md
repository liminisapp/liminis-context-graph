# Operations diary — 10 August 2026

Monday again, and a quieter start than last week. Writing this up from the corner table while the kettle does its thing, so forgive the rambling.

## Stand-up and the week ahead

Stand-up ran short. Most people had little to report beyond "carrying on", which is honestly a good sign after the scramble of the previous fortnight. The one thing we spent time on was the shared calendar: too many recurring meetings had piled up on Tuesdays and Thursdays, leaving almost no unbroken stretches for real work. We agreed to cancel two of the standing syncs outright and fold their agendas into the Wednesday meeting. Nobody objected, which is rare enough to be worth recording.

Aiko Osei, who looks after onboarding, mentioned that the welcome pack for new joiners is still a patchwork of old documents and wants a weekend-sized tidy. Rather than let it drift, she will take an hour on Friday to collect everything into a single folder and flag what is out of date. The rest of us promised to send her anything we remember writing for newcomers, even half-finished.

## Inventory housekeeping

Lena Moreau owns inventory and gave a short rundown of what the shelves and the spreadsheet currently say. They do not entirely agree. Some of that is ordinary lag, since items get used before anyone logs them, and some is plain untidiness. Her plan is to do a spot count of the high-turnover items instead of a full stocktake, then reconcile only the ones that are off. She asked that people stop borrowing cables and adapters without a note on the board, and there was a bit of sheepish laughter around the table.

Orbit Core, the project behind inventory, will take on the reconciliation script next. The idea is to make the weekly comparison automatic so the spot counts become a sanity check rather than a chore. Nothing is committed yet; Lena is going to write a short description of what "good enough" looks like so the work has a finish line.

## Small decisions

A few things settled in passing, listed here so they are not lost:

- Meeting notes go in this diary, not in individual chat threads. If it matters next month, it belongs here.
- The cleaning rota stays as it is. We briefly considered a swap and decided the current order works fine.
- We will try a "no new meetings on Fridays" rule for a month and see whether anyone complains.
- The old whiteboard in the corridor is being retired. Photograph anything you still need from it before the end of the week.

## Chores and loose ends

The printer is making a grinding noise again. Someone in the team will ring about the service agreement rather than keep hitting it with a flat hand. The plants near the window got watered, finally, and the one by the door might be past saving but we will give it another week.

On the practical side, the spare keyboard that has been sitting in the cupboard goes to whoever joins next, and the laptop stand left behind by a former colleague is up for grabs. First come, first served.

## Looking ahead

Next week's diary should carry the outcome of the spot count and a first draft of the tidied welcome folder. If either slips, say so early rather than quietly letting it roll over. The mood in the room was relaxed, the coffee supply is healthy, and I would call that a decent Monday.

## Midweek notes

Wednesday, written in a rush before the afternoon block, so these are bullet-ish.

The morning belonged to the planning conversation we keep postponing. We finally sat down and sorted the pile of "someday" ideas into three buckets: do now, do soon, and honestly not going to happen. The third bucket turned out to be the biggest, and there was a collective sense of relief at crossing things off. What survived the cull is a short list that fits on one sheet of paper, which was the point of the exercise.

### Onboarding thread

Aiko Osei raised a concern that new joiners are asked to read far too much on their first day and retain almost none of it. Her suggestion is to spread the reading across the first fortnight and pair each section with a short conversation with a real person. It costs a little more of everyone's time up front but should save repeated questions later. We agreed to try it with the next arrival and judge by how often they have to come back and ask the same thing.

She also wants a plain checklist of accounts and access that gets ticked off as it is granted, instead of the current approach where access gets sorted out whenever somebody notices it is missing. Several of us admitted to having waited weeks for a permission nobody realised we lacked.

### Inventory thread

Lena Moreau reported that the spot count found a handful of discrepancies, mostly small consumables, plus one item that seems to have simply vanished. Nobody owned up. She is not chasing it, only noting it, and will mention it in the next roundup in case it resurfaces in a drawer.

Orbit Core's reconciliation work is still at the sketching stage. There was a short debate about whether to build the comparison as a scheduled job or leave it as something a person runs on Mondays. The scheduled option sounds tidier, but running it by hand keeps a human in the loop for a while, which several people preferred while the data is still messy. We left it open until the first real run.

### Things that came up sideways

- A question about whether we should keep paper copies of the supplier paperwork. Answer: only the signed ones; scan the rest and recycle.
- Someone pointed out that the office thermostat has been fighting the window again. Facilities will be asked, politely.
- The shared mailbox has a backlog of automatic notifications nobody reads. A volunteer will set up a filter so the useful ones stand out.

### Afternoon

The afternoon block was heads-down time, which felt luxurious after the meeting cuts from Monday. A couple of people reported finishing things they had been circling for days. That is the best argument yet for the Friday experiment, and I will say so when we review it.

Before leaving, a reminder that the diary is meant to be skimmed. If a section runs long, trim it. Nobody reads the fourth paragraph of a status update, and I speak as someone who writes them.

## Audit follow-up

The internal audit results landed late on Thursday and most of today went to reading them properly. The format was familiar: a score, a short list of observations, and a few recommendations that range from sensible to slightly bureaucratic. Orbit Core came out at 76 out of 100 in the internal audit, which the group considered respectable for a project that is mostly plumbing and spreadsheets. The observations were mainly about documentation. The work itself was judged sound; the write-ups behind it were thinner than they should be.

We spent the best part of an hour on what to do about that. The temptation was to commission a big documentation sprint, but the consensus was that big sprints produce documents nobody maintains. Instead each person who touched the project will add a short note to the shared folder whenever they change something, and Lena Moreau will check the folder once a week. If that proves too much of a burden, we will cut it back, but it seemed the least painful route.

### Reading between the lines

A few of us noticed that some audit comments contradicted each other. One section praised the amount of automation, another asked for more manual checks. We decided not to chase the inconsistency and simply do what makes sense in practice: keep the automated comparison and add a human glance at the exceptions. Nobody wanted to spend a fortnight arguing with a report.

Aiko Osei pointed out that several of the recommendations touch onboarding, particularly the idea that new people should be walked through the inventory process in their first week. That fits with the earlier plan to spread the reading out, so she will fold a short inventory walkthrough into the pack she is rebuilding.

### Practical bits

- The recommendations list goes into the tracker as separate items, each with an owner. Nothing stays "everyone's job".
- The spot-count method from earlier in the month will be written up as a one-pager and kept with the audit material.
- The next audit date is not confirmed. We will ask rather than assume.

### Mood and morale

It is worth saying that the tone in the room was constructive. Audits can feel like grades, and there was a bit of grumbling at first, but by the end people were swapping ideas about what to tidy. I think the useful framing is that the score is a snapshot and the observations are a to-do list, and the second part matters more than the first.

### Small chores

The recycling bins were emptied, the whiteboard pens were replaced after a long drought of dying markers, and the spare chairs have been moved out of the corridor. Someone left a note asking who keeps hiding the good scissors. Nobody confessed.

We will pick this up again next week once the tracker items have owners and we can see how heavy the load really is. Until then, the plan is steady progress and no heroics.

## Friday wrap-up

End of the week, so this is the tidy-up entry. I will keep it brisk since the sun is out and nobody has the patience for a long one.

The main item was the people review. We went round the table, one person at a time, on how the term has gone. For onboarding, Aiko Osei completed 47 hours of training this term, mostly in short sessions fitted between other work, and she said the part that stuck was the facilitation material, which she plans to reuse when she runs the next welcome session. She was candid that she would like the training time protected in future instead of squeezed into gaps, and nobody disagreed.

On inventory, Lena Moreau scored 68 out of 100 in the internal audit. She took it well and said it matched her own sense of where the gaps were: records not updated when stock moves, and a habit of relying on memory. It is the sort of score that comes with a clear list of fixes rather than a mystery, which makes it easier to act on. We agreed the right response is better routines, not blame, and the others offered to help with the weekly counts so the load is shared.

### Decisions

- The documentation habit introduced earlier in the week continues for another month, then gets reviewed.
- Training time will be written into the calendar as real blocks, visible to everyone, so it stops being the first thing sacrificed.
- Weekly counts rotate between people instead of landing on one person every time.

The shared kitchen has been properly cleaned, and a polite sign went up asking people to take their own mugs home on Fridays. We will see how that goes. The broken blind by the east window is still broken; a repair request has gone in again, and I have no great hope but we tried.

The spare monitor in the cupboard turned out to work fine once someone found the right cable, so it will go to the next joiner's desk. Meanwhile the old keyboard from the first week of the month has found a home with someone who types too hard and goes through them regularly.
