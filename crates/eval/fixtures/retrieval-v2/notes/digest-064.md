## Quarter review and incident tidy-up

This part covers the quarter-end review, which was shorter than I feared and more useful than I expected.

Starting with incidents. Marlin Core logged 6 incidents during the quarter, and we spent a fair while looking at them as a set instead of one at a time. The pattern that emerged was boring in a reassuring way: most were caused by the same kind of change being made late in the day, without a quick check from a second person. None was severe, but several cost somebody an evening. We agreed to avoid scheduling changes of that kind in the last hour of the working day, and to say so in the change template so that it is not just folklore.

The write-ups themselves were uneven. A couple were thorough, with a clear timeline and a note on what we would do differently, and a couple were three lines long. Nobody is at fault for that, since write-ups get written when people are tired. We decided to use a short fixed outline, covering what happened, what we noticed first, what fixed it, and what we will change, and to accept that a brief write-up following the outline beats a long one that wanders.

Next, a few notes from the discussion about on-call. The rota is working, but swaps are arranged by direct message and then forgotten, which led to one confusing handover. We will record every swap in the shared calendar at the time it is agreed. Quick to do, and it saves the question of who is actually covering.

Items from the end of the review:

- Merge the three overlapping incident tags in the tracker into one.
- Link each incident write-up from the relevant wiki page so that the history is discoverable.
- Ask whoever owns the alert settings whether two noisy alerts can be tuned down. They contributed to the late-evening disturbance more than anything else did.
- Schedule a half-hour to read the older write-ups again, in case anything we noted then has quietly stopped being true.

There was also a conversation about how to talk about the quarter with colleagues who were not in the room. The temptation is to give a single headline figure and move on, but a lone number hides more than it shows. We will pair any summary figure with two sentences of context. Not a big change, but it should reduce the number of follow-up questions.

On Ines Lindqvist and billing: nothing new this part, except that she asked to be told earlier when a scheduling change affects invoice dates. That is a reasonable request and we will add it to the change template as a single checkbox.

One more point on tone. A few people said these reviews can feel like a trial, and that is not the intention. The point is to look at what the system did, not to look for someone to blame. We will start the next review by saying that explicitly, and see whether the conversation changes.

I will leave the rest for the following entry, which should be easier to write since it mostly lists things that were completed.

On the scheduling side, Marlin Core is working through a backlog of small tidy-ups that nobody ever finds time for: renaming calendars, removing unused recurring events, making sure the handover notes point to the right place. None of it is exciting, but several people said the cumulative effect is that planning feels less cluttered. We set a rule of thumb that anything taking under ten minutes gets done on the spot and not added to a list.

A couple of observations about how the team has been working lately:

- Messages in the general channel are getting longer. Perhaps that is fine, but long messages are easy to miss. A short summary line at the top helps.
- Decisions made in passing are not always written down. The notes are meant to catch them, but only if someone remembers to say "that was a decision" out loud.
- People are mostly good at asking for help, but less good at saying when they have finished something. A quick "done" message would help the person who was waiting.

We discussed trialling a short Friday message where each person lists one thing finished and one thing they are stuck on. It is not a status report, just a prompt. Opinion was split, and in the end we agreed to try it for three weeks and drop it if it feels forced.

There was also a request to be clearer about what counts as urgent. The word is used loosely, and a few people have started to ignore it. Suggested rule: urgent means someone is blocked now or something customer-facing is wrong. Everything else is "soon" or "when you can". We will paste that definition into the top of the intake page.

One person asked whether we should hold a proper retrospective on how these notes are used, since they have been going for a while. Probably yes, but not this month. A line in the calendar for the autumn will do.
