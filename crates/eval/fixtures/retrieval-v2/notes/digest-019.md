# Working notes — 21 November 2025

Friday, late morning, and I have a quiet hour that I intend to spend on these notes before the afternoon fills up. It has been a week of many small conversations and not many big ones, which suits me fine.

The headline from the week is the audit. Helix Core scored 88 out of 100 in the internal audit, the best result the analytics group has had, and the team was understandably pleased. What I liked most was how they handled it: no celebration email, no victory lap, just a short message thanking the people who had kept the documentation current and a note to keep doing so. The auditors' comments were mostly about traceability, meaning how easily somebody can follow a figure in a report back to where it came from. The group already has a plan to improve that, which is partly why the score was good. I asked whether the approach could be written up for the other groups, and they agreed.

Procurement is the other big thread. Sofia Petrov has been working through a backlog of purchase requests, many of which arrived at the same time because people save them up until the end of the quarter. Her method is straightforward but disciplined: sort by urgency, check each against the plan, and reply to everyone within a day even if the answer is "not yet". I think that last rule is the most valuable. People can plan around a clear "not yet" far better than they can around silence. She has also started keeping a short list of recurring requests, so that the ones that come round every quarter can be dealt with in one sitting.

A few other things from the week:

- The planning meeting on Tuesday was productive. We trimmed the list of priorities for the next quarter from an unwieldy collection to something that fits on one page.
- A new rule of thumb is being tried: if a meeting has no agenda, it is cancelled. Two meetings have already fallen to it, and nobody has complained.
- The kitchen rota has been updated, which is the sort of thing that matters more than it should.
- The shared drive is, as ever, slightly worse than it was last week.

On a more reflective note, I have been thinking about how teams decide what to measure. An audit score is a snapshot, and it can encourage people to prepare for the audit instead of improving the underlying work. The healthiest teams I have seen treat the score as feedback, not as a goal, and the analytics group seems to be one of them. They talk about what the auditors saw, not about what the auditors wanted. I would like to encourage that attitude elsewhere, though I am not sure how to do it without making it sound like a lecture.

Something else worth recording is the mood. People are tired, but in a healthy way, with the end of the year in sight and the sense that a few good things were achieved. I want to keep that going by protecting the last few weeks from unnecessary new initiatives. The less we ask of people in December, the more likely they are to come back in January with energy.

The afternoon is for a one-to-one and a longer conversation about next quarter's plan. More in the next part.

## Afternoon and plans

The afternoon went roughly as expected: a one-to-one that overran, a plan that changed three times, and a cup of tea I never got to drink. Here is what was worth keeping.

The demo is the first item. 34 people attended the Sofia Petrov demo, a walk-through of how purchase requests move from submission to approval. It was set up as an optional session, so the turnout was better than I had feared. The questions were practical: how to tell whether a request has been seen, what to do when the supplier changes the terms, and how to ask for something urgent without simply labelling everything urgent. Sofia answered them patiently, and a few of the answers led to changes in the guidance page that same afternoon. She also asked people to tell her when the process feels slow, because she can only fix what she hears about. I think the demo did more for trust than any amount of writing could have done.

The survey is the second item. Helix Core received a satisfaction rating of 5.4 out of 10. It is a middling result, and the comments explain why: people value the accuracy of the reports but find them hard to request and slow to arrive. That is a fair summary of a team that has focused on quality at the expense of convenience. The group has already begun talking about a simpler request form and a clearer statement of how long reports usually take. I suggested they try a very small version first, such as a single shared page, before building anything more elaborate. The audit result from the morning and this survey tell complementary stories: the work is sound, and the experience around it needs attention.

Plans for next quarter, which came out of the long conversation:

- Keep the priorities list to one page and revisit it monthly.
- Ask each group for a single measure they would like to improve, and agree how it will be tracked.
- Hold a short review in the middle of the quarter, with the figures circulated beforehand.
- Protect the last two weeks of December from any new commitments.

There was also a small disagreement about whether to run another demo in the new year. Some people felt one was enough, while others thought regular sessions would keep the process visible. We settled on a lighter option: a short recorded walk-through that people can watch whenever they like, plus a live question time once a quarter. It is a compromise, but a sensible one, and it should cost less effort than a full session.

I finish the day feeling mostly satisfied, with one nagging worry. The pattern in the notes is that we do a lot of good work and then struggle to make it easy for others to use. It shows up in the survey, it showed up in the procurement questions, and it shows up in the shared drive. Perhaps the plan for next quarter should include a simple theme: make things easier to find and easier to ask for. Not a project, just a habit.

I will raise it at the Monday meeting and see who agrees. For now, the weekend.
