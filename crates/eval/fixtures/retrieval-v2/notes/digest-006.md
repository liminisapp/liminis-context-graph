# Operations diary — 10 March 2025

Operations diary entry for the start of the week. I am writing this in the gap between two meetings, so it is a bit staccato.

The morning started with a look at the support queue. Juniper Core is the project that owns support, and it has been quieter than the winter peak, which gave the team room to catch up on follow-ups that had been sitting. Several of those were customers waiting for a reply to a second question after we had answered the first, and those tend to be the ones who get annoyed. A few were closed after a quick check that the problem had resolved itself. The team agreed to do a short sweep like this every Monday morning, before anything new comes in.

Billing was next. Ines Lindqvist reported that the month-end run looks on track, and that the manual corrections are fewer than they used to be. She was keen to point out that this is partly because of a boring fix: a validation step that catches obviously odd entries before they spread. Nobody had realised how much time those entries used to eat. She will write up the validation step so that it survives her being on holiday.

Maintenance news from Yara Nakamura: the planned housekeeping on the shared equipment is on schedule, and nothing urgent has turned up. She would like everyone to stop leaving things on the maintenance bench, which has been a recurring complaint. A reminder note is going up. There was some good-humoured grumbling, but it is a fair request.

Other points raised:

- The shared printer is out of action again. Someone will call the vendor, since the usual fixes have not worked.
- The kitchen rota has lapsed, and the dishwasher has been emptied by whoever noticed it first. We will agree a fresh rota rather than rely on goodwill.
- A new person is starting soon, and we need to prepare the welcome pack, desk and accounts. The checklist exists, but it has not been tested recently, so we will walk through it together.
- The support documentation has a few out-of-date pages. It would be good to assign each page to someone who will actually read it from time to time.

I also want to put on record a small decision. We will keep the Monday sweep for the support queue, and we will not add a second one on Thursdays for now. The reasoning was that two sweeps would split attention and make it harder to tell what has actually been dealt with. If the queue grows again, we can revisit.

Finally, a few words on how we are doing. Morale seems steady. The weather is still grey, but people are in decent spirits, and the shared lunch on Friday went well. A couple of folks are tired from the quarter-end rush, and we should be considerate about not piling things on.

To do before the next entry: write up the validation step, sort the printer, refresh the welcome checklist, and assign owners for the support pages.

## Audit results and follow-up

The internal audit results arrived midweek, and they set the tone for most of what followed. Juniper Core scored 66 out of 100 in the internal audit. That is not a disaster, but it is also not where anyone wants to be, and the room was subdued while we read the breakdown. The comments were fair. Most of the lost marks came from missing documentation and inconsistent follow-through on escalations, rather than from anything dramatic going wrong. In other words, we mostly do the right things and fail to write them down.

We spent a while deciding how to respond. The first idea, a large documentation project, was rejected quickly, because nobody wants to spend weeks producing pages that go stale. The second idea, which won, was to fix the habits that generate the gaps. For escalations, that means a simple note at the moment of escalation: who, why, what happens next. For documentation, it means updating the page in the same sitting as the change, not later. Both are small, and both can be checked.

Ines Lindqvist offered to help by sharing how the billing side handles its own records, since that side has had audits before and has developed a fairly light routine. She said the main trick is to make the record a by-product of the work rather than a separate chore. Yara Nakamura added that maintenance already logs most things when they happen, and that support might borrow the format.

There was a moment of disagreement about whether to share the score more widely. One view was that it should stay within the team until there is an improvement plan. Another was that being open about it builds trust and invites help. We landed on sharing it with a short note about what we plan to do, so that nobody hears about it as gossip.

Practical matters from the same discussion:

- The vendor for the printer came and fixed it, so that item is closed.
- The welcome checklist was tested with a dry run. Two steps were out of order and one was missing, and both are corrected.
- The kitchen rota has been agreed, and it is on the wall rather than in a message that will be lost.
- Owners have been assigned for the support pages, with a light review date for each.

I should record the working agreement for the next few weeks. We will measure progress by whether escalation notes exist and whether pages are current, not by trying to guess the next audit score. If the habits are good, the score will follow, and if they are not, a clever plan will not rescue us.

One more thing: a reminder that the audit is about the work and not about individuals. Several people were worried it would be taken personally, and the group made a point of saying it would not. That helped a lot, and the conversation afterwards was more constructive than I expected.

Next entry: check whether escalation notes are being written, review the first pages assigned to owners, and decide when to share the improvement plan.

## Backlogs and offsite plans

Closing part of the diary for this stretch, covering open tickets and the plans for getting together.

On the billing side, the Ines Lindqvist backlog stood at 136 open tickets at the end of the sprint. She was relaxed about it. A good share are waiting on information from outside the team, and a few are simply slow because month-end work takes priority. What she wants is a way to separate the tickets that are stuck from the ones that are just waiting their turn, so that the stuck ones get attention. Her suggestion was a plain label, applied when a ticket has not moved for a while, and reviewed at the start of each week. It is a small thing, and it should make the list less intimidating.

Yara Nakamura raised the offsite. The Yara Nakamura offsite will take place in Aarhus, and she has started to sketch what the days might look like. The aim is to balance a few focused sessions with time to wander and talk. She has asked for ideas, and the first batch includes a session on how we keep records, which feels timely after the audit. Someone also suggested a visit to a local workshop for fun, which would be a nice change from sitting in rooms.

There was a short discussion about travel logistics. Everyone should confirm availability soon, and anyone who cannot travel should say so early so we can plan how to include them remotely. Nobody should feel awkward about declining, and the plan will not depend on perfect attendance. Ines offered to help with the paperwork, since the approvals route is familiar to her.

Other business:

- The support page owners reported in. Most pages needed only small edits, while one needed a rewrite and is now on someone's list.
- The Monday sweep ran again, and the queue was clear by mid-morning. It is a good habit.
- The new person's accounts have been prepared in advance, so day one should be smooth. A buddy has been identified to help with questions.
- The improvement plan from the audit discussion has been drafted. It is brief and fits on a single page, with three simple commitments.

On the Juniper Core side, there is a modest sense of relief that the next step is concrete. The audit was a hard read, but the response is practical, and people are mostly pleased to be doing something about it rather than worrying. I will make sure the plan is shared with the wider group and that the first progress check goes into the diary.

For the record, no major decisions were taken in this part beyond approving the label for stuck tickets and agreeing to ask for offsite ideas until the end of next week. Everything else is a continuation of work already in motion.

Reminders: confirm availability for the offsite, apply the stuck-ticket label, share the improvement plan, and check in on the new person's first week.
