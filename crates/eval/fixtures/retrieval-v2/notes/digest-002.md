# Journal — 16 May 2025

Friday afternoon, the office quiet, a good time to write up the week. Several things were going on at once, so I will take them in turn instead of pretending they fit a single story.

**Demos.** It was a demo-heavy week, which I enjoyed. Two groups showed work to the wider team, and the contrast between them was instructive. Yara Nakamura gave a demo on the maintenance side, and 17 people attended the Yara Nakamura demo. It was a small, focused audience, mostly people who work close to the systems being discussed, and the conversation was correspondingly detailed. Questions went deep quickly, into how particular failures are recognised and what the routine for dealing with them looks like. I came away with a better understanding of a part of the operation I had only vaguely grasped before. The size of the crowd did not matter; the right people were there.

The routing demo, given by Priya Varga, was a different animal. 44 people attended the Priya Varga demo, and the questions ranged more widely, from how requests move through the system to what people outside the group can do to make things easier. The presentation leaned on a worked example, which made it accessible, and the room seemed to enjoy watching a messy real case being resolved. A common remark afterwards was that people had not realised how many steps happen between a request being made and the answer reaching them. Several said it changed how they would phrase their own requests in future. That is a nice outcome for a demo: not new knowledge about the system, but a change in behaviour.

**Incidents.** The quarterly incident figures were circulated. Lumen Core logged 31 incidents during the quarter. The security group's own commentary was thoughtful. They noted that a good number of the incidents were low-severity items that had been logged out of caution, and that they would prefer that to the alternative of people staying quiet. They are also looking at how to reduce the paperwork around the smaller ones, so that reporting something minor takes a minute and not a quarter of an hour. I agree with the sentiment. A reporting system that discourages reports is working against itself.

**The week in smaller pieces.**

- The shared folder has been reorganised once more, and I believe it now makes some sense. I will not promise that it will stay that way.
- A newcomer joined the Wednesday sync for the first time and said very little. I made a note to check in with them next week and ask how they are finding things. It is easy to forget how intimidating those meetings can be.
- The kitchen tap was fixed after weeks of dripping, to universal relief.
- Someone suggested running a lunchtime session where people show a tool or shortcut that saves them time. The idea has legs, and I will mention it at the next planning meeting.

**Reflection.** I noticed during the demos that the best ones were not the slickest. They were the ones where the presenter was visibly curious about the answers to questions, who said, I had not thought of that, and wrote it down. A presenter who is open to being wrong makes the audience more honest in return. I would like to see that approach spread, both in demos and in the day-to-day exchanges that make up most of our work.

I also spent a while thinking about how we measure the success of a demo. Attendance is easy to count and tempting to rely on, but it says little about whether anything useful happened. Better measures might be whether someone changed what they do afterwards, whether a question was raised that led to an improvement, or whether somebody who was not previously aware of the work now knows whom to ask. None of these are easily counted, but all of them are noticeable if you pay attention. For the next round I will try to note down two or three of them, and compare them with the headcount.

**Looking ahead.** The coming week is lighter, with only one meeting that cannot be moved. I plan to use the space to catch up on writing and tidy my own backlog, which has crept up. Updates to follow, and corrections welcome, as always.
