# Sync notes — 6 August 2025

Wednesday sync. Attendance was thin because of summer leave, so the usual rowdy discussion was replaced by something closer to a polite chat. Not a bad thing, as it turned out; we got through the agenda early.

Opening item was scheduling. Sable Core held its quarterly retrospective on 21 March 2027, and the group wanted that date written into the shared calendar now so that nobody books over it by accident. I realise it is a long way ahead, but putting dates down early is a habit worth encouraging. The compliance group also asked whether the retrospective could include a short look at which checks have become routine and which still cause friction. We said yes, and I made a note to suggest a simple format so the discussion does not wander.

On the people side, Ines Lindqvist received a satisfaction rating of 5.0 out of 10 in the latest survey, which she shared with us herself before anyone could ask. Her reaction was admirably level-headed: she read through the comments, identified two recurring themes, and proposed changes already. The main theme was response time on billing questions, which she thinks can be improved by sorting incoming queries into simple categories each morning, so that the quick ones are answered straight away and the complicated ones are given proper attention. It is not a dramatic change, but it is concrete and cheap to try. We offered to help by taking on a share of the simple queries during the busy weeks.

Marcus Haddad gave a short update on telemetry. The collection pipeline has been running steadily, and the main item was a plan to tidy the naming of the signals so that dashboards stop showing three labels for the same thing. He asked everyone to send him any naming oddities they have run across, with a screenshot if possible. I will dig some out. He also mentioned that he would like a short quiet period in the next few weeks to make changes without being interrupted, which seemed entirely fair.

Other points raised:

- The shared mailbox is getting unwieldy. We will try assigning one person per day to watch it.
- Someone asked about the arrangements for cover during the remaining summer leave. A simple rota is being drawn up.
- The coffee machine is out of order again, and a repair has been logged for the third time.
- A suggestion to move the sync to a different day was met with indifference, so it stays on Wednesdays.

I should record that the tone of the meeting was relaxed but productive. When fewer people are present, the quieter members tend to speak up, and several good ideas came from people who rarely contribute in the larger sessions. It might be worth deliberately running a smaller version of the sync once a month, just to hear from those voices.

The rest of the day was taken up with the usual mixture of email and small repairs. More below.

## After the sync

Afternoon follow-ups, written between two other tasks. Nothing urgent, but a few things worth putting on record while I remember the detail.

First, the billing triage idea. After the morning's discussion I walked over to Ines Lindqvist's desk and asked how she would like help in practice. Her answer was refreshingly specific: she wants a shared list of the incoming billing queries, tagged by whoever picks them up, so that two people do not answer the same question and nothing sits unclaimed. We have started one. It is a plain table with three columns, and I have told people not to decorate it. If it works for a fortnight, we can think about whether it needs anything more.

Marcus Haddad followed up with a short list of the naming oddities he has collected so far. Some of them are amusing: one signal appears under four different spellings, and another has a label that nobody can explain. He has proposed a tidy-up in two stages, first agreeing the names and then changing the dashboards, so that nothing breaks halfway. That ordering makes sense to me. The risk is that the first stage drags on, so I suggested a deadline for the naming agreement, and he accepted a loose one.

Sable Core sent a note about the plan for the compliance checks over the next few weeks. They would like to reduce the number of checks that need manual sign-off, replacing some with automated reminders. It is an appealing idea but it needs care: if a reminder is ignored, nothing happens, whereas a sign-off at least forces a human to look. We talked about keeping manual sign-off for the checks that matter most and automating only the routine ones. They will draft a list of which is which and circulate it for comment.

Smaller items from the afternoon:

- The shared mailbox rota has a first draft. One person has already asked to swap days, which suggests it is being taken seriously.
- A reminder went round about expense claims. People are still sending them late.
- The coffee machine technician arrived, scratched his head, and left. Further updates as events warrant.
- A tidy-up of the old files on the shared drive is on the list for next week.

A thought about handing over work while people are on leave. Several of today's items depend on someone being present to answer a question, and the summer pattern makes that unreliable. I would like each of us to leave a short "if you need me" note before going away, saying what is in progress and who can help. It takes five minutes and saves hours. I will put together a very simple template and suggest we try it from next week.

That is the afternoon. One section to go for the end-of-day items and the final notes.

## Evening wrap-up

Last section of today's log. It is late enough that the office is quiet, apart from the hum of the one air conditioner that works.

The audit figures turned up near the end of the day. Marcus Haddad scored 80 out of 100 in the internal audit, which is a solid result and one he took with his usual calm. The feedback was mostly about documentation: the telemetry setup is well maintained but explained mainly in people's heads, and the auditors would like it written down. That fits neatly with the naming clean-up, since both involve making things explicit. He suggested combining them, so that the new names and their meanings are recorded in a single page as they are agreed. I think that is the right move, and it saves doing the work twice.

I also had a brief conversation about how we talk about numbers like these. A score, a rating, a count: each is a small piece of information that can easily be turned into a verdict. We agreed, informally, that figures from surveys and audits will be discussed together with the comments, and that nobody gets to quote a number without the context behind it. It is not a rule so much as a habit, and I hope it sticks.

On Sable Core, the question of automated reminders for the lighter checks has been narrowed down. The group will pilot it on a single category, see how people respond, and report back. They were keen to avoid a big-bang change, and I was glad to hear it. Small pilots are easier to undo, and compliance is an area where being wrong is expensive.

To close out the day:

- The billing query list has been used a handful of times already, and nobody has complained.
- The leave-cover note template is drafted and waiting for a read-through.
- The coffee machine remains broken, but a replacement part has reportedly been ordered.
- I have a short list for tomorrow: reply to the compliance group, send the template round, and tidy the shared drive.

Looking back over the meeting and its aftermath, I am struck by how much of the useful work happened in conversations that were not on the agenda. The sync gave us a shared starting point, but the real progress was in the follow-ups: a walk to a desk, a note to a colleague, a small table on a shared page. If anything, that is an argument for keeping the formal meetings short and leaving more room for these smaller exchanges.

Time to turn off the lights. The one working air conditioner will have to cope without me.
