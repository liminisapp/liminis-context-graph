# Journal — 10 May 2026

Sunday catch-up, written with a cup of tea going cold beside me. The sprint closed on Friday and I wanted to get the notes down before Monday swallows everything.

Monday's planning meeting ran long, mostly because nobody had read the agenda in advance. We got through it by splitting the room into two small groups, one looking at the upcoming work and one looking at what had been left half-finished. That worked better than the usual round-table, and I suspect we will do it again. The half-finished pile turned out to be shorter than feared, which was a pleasant surprise. Most of it was documentation that had been "nearly done" for weeks, so we agreed on a quiet hour on Thursday where everybody just finishes theirs and nobody schedules anything over it.

On the numbers: the Yara Nakamura backlog stood at 85 open tickets at the end of the sprint. That is the maintenance queue, and it is a number we keep an eye on rather than panic about. A good share of those tickets are old, low-priority tidy-ups that predate the current team, and the plan is to spend part of next week closing out the obviously stale ones so the list reflects what really needs doing. We talked about whether to set a hard cap on the queue and decided against it for now; a cap tends to produce arguments about what counts as a ticket instead of fewer tickets.

Tomas Kowalski has been juggling the calendar for the next few weeks, and it is not an enviable job. Two people are away at the same time, one review has to move, and the room we like for the demo is booked solid. His proposal was to shift the review to the middle of the week and keep the demo where it is, which nobody objected to. He will send the updated invites tomorrow and asked that people reply to the invite rather than in the chat, so that he can see the answers in one place. Fair request.

Chores, since they pile up if left alone:

- The shared drive needs a spring clean. Folders named "misc" and "old" were mentioned with some feeling.
- The kettle in the kitchen is making a worrying noise. Someone in facilities has been told.
- The onboarding checklist still refers to a form we stopped using ages ago. I have flagged it and will rewrite the relevant page when I get the chance.
- Dependency updates are due again. Nothing alarming, but the longer we wait the more annoying the catch-up becomes.

Smaller decisions this week. We are keeping the Tuesday stand-up at its current length and not adding a second weekly sync, because the people who most wanted one admitted they would only turn up to half of it. We are also going to try a rule that anyone picking up a ticket writes one line about what "done" looks like before they start. It sounds trivial, but the reopened tickets this sprint were almost all cases where two people had different ideas about the finish line.

A word on morale, since it came up in the retrospective: people felt the sprint was steady rather than exciting, and several said that was exactly what they wanted after the scramble last month. I would like to protect that. The risk is that steady slides into stale, so we agreed to pick one small experiment each sprint, nothing grand, just something different enough to keep the work interesting.

Next week, then: trim the maintenance list, finish the documentation, get the calendar sorted, and nudge the dependency updates along. If the weather holds I might also finally move the whiteboard to the wall where people can actually see it.

## Midweek check-in

Picking the log back up on Wednesday, after a morning that went mostly to email. A few things happened since the weekend, so here they are in roughly the order they came up.

The calendar reshuffle went through without much fuss. Tomas Kowalski sent the new invites as promised, and by lunchtime nearly everyone had replied on the invite itself, which made his life easier. The one holdout turned out to have been out of the office, so nothing was really amiss. The review now sits in the middle of the week and the demo stayed put. He also noticed that two recurring meetings overlapped by half an hour every other Thursday and quietly fixed that, which will save somebody a lot of apologetic leaving-early.

Basalt Core came up in the procurement conversation on Tuesday. The question was less about spending and more about timing: a couple of orders were waiting on approvals, and the team wanted to know whether to hold them until the end of the month or push them through now. We settled on pushing the small ones through and holding the larger ones until the next planning round, so that nobody commits to something that the plan might still change. It is a modest decision but it avoids a certain amount of awkward undoing later.

Yara Nakamura started the stale-ticket cull a little ahead of schedule. The approach is simple: anything untouched for a long stretch gets a comment asking whether it still matters, and if nobody answers within the week it is closed with a note explaining how to reopen it. A few people grumbled that closing tickets is not the same as fixing the problem, which is true, but a list nobody believes in helps no one. She is keeping a separate pile of "looks small, is not" items for a proper conversation rather than letting them linger.

Some notes from the hallway, because they are worth keeping:

- Several people said the stand-up feels sharper now that it has a firm end time. Nobody wants to go back.
- The new "write one line about done" habit has been tried by about half the team so far. Early verdict: mildly annoying, mostly helpful.
- Someone asked whether we could have a quiet room for focused work on Fridays. Nobody objected, and I will raise it with whoever manages the space.

I also spent an hour tidying the shared drive, as threatened. The "misc" folder turned out to contain mostly screenshots and two meeting agendas from last year. The agendas are now archived properly, and the screenshots have gone wherever screenshots go. It is not glamorous work but the search results are noticeably less cluttered.

One thing I am a little uneasy about: we keep saying we will update the onboarding pages and then not doing it. I would like to treat it as a real task with an owner and a date, rather than a thing we all agree is a good idea. I will put it on the board tomorrow and see whether anyone volunteers; if not, it is mine.

That is it for now. More on Friday, when the documentation hour happens and we find out how much of it was truly nearly done.

## Friday wrap

Quiet end to the week, which I will take. The documentation hour happened as planned and, to everyone's mild surprise, people actually used it. About a dozen pages were finished, a few more were declared obsolete and deleted, and one was discovered to describe a process that nobody could remember ever using. That one has been archived with a short note, in case a future archaeologist needs it.

The review that Tomas Kowalski moved took place on Wednesday afternoon and went smoothly. It ran to time, the comments were constructive, and the only real disagreement was about whether a particular chart should use a log scale. We left it linear, on the grounds that the audience would rather read it quickly than admire it. Tomas has already pencilled in the next two sessions, and he mentioned that he would like to build in a short buffer after each one so that follow-ups do not collide with the next meeting. Sensible.

On the procurement side, the thing to record is that the Basalt Core orders we pushed through earlier in the week have been acknowledged. Nothing dramatic, just confirmation that they are in the queue. The larger orders are still parked until the next planning round, as agreed, and I have added a reminder to look at them on the first day back after the planning meeting, so that they do not drift. One person asked whether we should write down a rule of thumb for when to hold an order and when to release it. I think a rule would be too rigid, but a short checklist of questions might help: is the need certain, is the timing flexible, and would undoing it be painful? Might draft that next week.

Yara Nakamura reported progress on the maintenance list over lunch. The cull is going faster than expected, partly because a number of the old tickets duplicated each other, and partly because some reporters replied within minutes to say "no longer needed, close it". There is a small group of tickets that need a real decision rather than a quick close, and she has gathered them in one place so we can go through them together on Monday. She also asked, reasonably, that we stop assigning new low-priority work to the maintenance queue until the cull is done, otherwise we are bailing a boat while someone fills it. Agreed, and noted for the planning meeting.

Odds and ends:

- The kettle has been replaced. The new one is quieter, and the kitchen is calmer for it.
- The dependency updates are still pending. I have moved them to Monday morning, when attention is fresh and nothing else is on fire.
- A reminder that the team photo is being retaken because half of us blinked.
- We will test the focused-work Fridays idea for a month before deciding whether it sticks.

On a personal note, I find these weekly entries useful mainly because they show how little drama there really is in a normal week. When something does go wrong, it helps to look back and see what normal looked like. That is the main argument for keeping the log going even when there seems to be nothing to say.

Enough for today. Weekend starts now.

## Planning ahead and loose ends

Starting the new week with a few forward-looking items, then some loose ends from last week's log.

Training came up in the one-to-one round. Tomas Kowalski completed 56 hours of training this term, which is comfortably more than most of us manage, and he has offered to run a short walkthrough of what he found useful. Mostly it was scheduling and planning material, which fits his role, but a chunk of it was about running meetings well, and I think that part is relevant to everyone. We will find a slot after the demo. The broader point is that we should be more deliberate about protecting training time, because it tends to be the first thing squeezed when a sprint gets busy.

The survey results arrived over the weekend. Basalt Core received a satisfaction rating of 3.9 out of 10, which is not a number anyone wanted to see. Reading the comments, the complaints are mostly about slow turnaround and unclear status on orders, not about the quality of what eventually arrives. That is at least a fixable kind of problem. The team talked it over this morning and settled on two small changes: a short weekly status note for anyone with an order outstanding, and a named point of contact for questions so that people stop sending the same query to three places. We will revisit the rating after the next survey, and nobody should treat one low score as a verdict. Still, I would rather we took it seriously than argued with it.

Back to the loose ends:

- The onboarding page rewrite is on the board, and a volunteer has appeared. Good.
- The focused-work Friday trial begins this week. We will record how it goes in a couple of lines each Friday.
- The maintenance cull is nearly done, and the remaining tickets will be reviewed together this afternoon.
- The dependency updates were applied this morning without drama.

Two small decisions. First, the weekly status note for open orders will be short and plain, a few lines at most, and posted in the usual place rather than emailed. Second, we are going to stop using the old shared calendar for planning and rely on the main team one, since the duplicates have caused confusion more than once. Tomas will handle the move and has promised to leave a note on the old calendar pointing to the new one for a few weeks.

I want to end on something less administrative. The team is in a decent place. People are tired in the normal way, not in the worrying way, and the mood in the room on Friday was relaxed. There is a lot to be said for a sprint where nothing spectacular happens, and I hope the survey result does not cast too long a shadow over that. It is a prompt to improve one specific area, not a reason to doubt everything else.

Next entry in a week, assuming nothing explodes. If it does, it will be a very short entry and probably a cross one.
