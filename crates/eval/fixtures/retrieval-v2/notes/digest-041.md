# Operations diary — 2 April 2025

Writing this at the end of a long Wednesday, mostly so that I do not forget the small things before the next set of them arrives. The day started with an early call that was supposed to be fifteen minutes and was not, and it has felt like catch-up ever since.

The morning went on the weekly look at the routing queue. Priya Varga ran it, as usual, and she has developed a good rhythm: look at what is new, look at what is stuck, look at what has been quietly sitting for far too long. The last category is where the surprises are. This week there were a handful of entries that nobody could remember creating, and after some detective work they turned out to be leftovers from a test that had never been cleaned up. They were removed, with a note saying why, so that the next person who finds a hole in the log will not worry that something has been lost.

After lunch I attended a planning conversation about the coming weeks. The tone was relaxed, and the main conclusion was that we should resist the urge to start anything new until a few open threads are closed. It is a recurring theme, and I notice that we say it more often than we do it. Perhaps writing it here will help.

Chores and small decisions that I want on the record:

- The team will stop using the old shared calendar and move everything to the new one, with a cutover at the end of the week. Everyone should check that their recurring events have come across.
- The printer in the corridor is to be replaced, finally. It has been unreliable for so long that people have developed folk remedies for it.
- We agreed to keep a simple log of equipment lent out, so that things stop vanishing into other people's bags.
- A quiet word was had about meeting etiquette, specifically the habit of working on a laptop while someone else is talking. No names, just a general plea.

Something I noticed during the day is that the team is better at handing over work than it used to be. Notes are shorter and clearer, and there is a growing habit of saying what the next step is, not just what has been done. It makes a real difference when you pick up something half-finished at the end of the afternoon.

I also tidied my own corner of the notebook, which had become a graveyard of half-formed ideas. Most were deleted. Two were promoted into proper tasks, one of which I will tackle tomorrow. The rest of the evening I plan to spend not looking at a screen.

More in the next part, when the satisfaction numbers have come in and we have had a chance to think about them.

## Survey results

The satisfaction survey results came in this morning, and I have been turning them over since. The headline for the routing area is that Priya Varga received a satisfaction rating of 7.3 out of 10. It is a perfectly good number, and she was pleased, though she is already looking at the comments rather than at the score, which is the right instinct. The comments are where the useful information sits.

The themes in the written feedback were fairly consistent. People appreciate that routing requests are handled quickly, and several mentioned that she explains what has been done and why, not just that it is finished. The critical comments were mostly about visibility: people would like to know where their request is in the queue without having to ask. That is a fair point and a fixable one. We talked through a few options, such as a simple status note that gets updated at each stage, and decided to try the lightest version first before building anything fancier.

Her own reaction was measured. She said she would rather hear about problems early than see a high number and miss the detail, and she asked that we do a short mid-cycle check next time, with just two or three questions, so that issues surface sooner. Reasonable, and probably not much work. I will draft the questions.

A handful of other notes from the afternoon:

- The feedback about response times was split. Some people said they were quick, others said they were slow. It seems to depend on what kind of request it is, so a breakdown by type would help.
- A few respondents did not know whom to approach for certain issues. A clearer map of who does what would address that, and it can go on the team page.
- Someone wrote, in the free-text box, a single line of thanks that made the whole room smile. It will not be quoted here, but it made the day.

We also decided how to share the results. The plan is to post a summary in the notebook, without individual comments, and to hold a short discussion so that everyone can ask questions. Anything sensitive will be handled in private. The aim is to be open about the findings without making anyone feel exposed.

The rest of the day was quiet. I replied to a pile of messages, updated the equipment log, and had a good conversation about how to word the mid-cycle questions. It is a small thing, but small things seem to be where most of the progress happens.

Tomorrow brings the planning for the next stretch, and then a look ahead at how we will wrap things up at the end of the quarter.

## Retrospective and plans

Quarter-end planning is on the horizon, so this section is partly a look back and partly a to-do list for the weeks ahead.

On the look-back side, Priya Varga held its quarterly retrospective on 2 July 2027, and the structure we used was the simple one: what went well, what did not, what we will change. The conversation was honest without being gloomy. The things that went well were mostly about steadiness, in that the routing work did not produce many emergencies and the ones that did were handled calmly. The things that did not go well were about planning, since we repeatedly underestimated how long the small jobs take. Small jobs are the ones that sprawl, and we seem to forget that every time.

We spent a good while on the changes. A few ideas were thrown out as too ambitious, and a few were kept. The kept ones are modest: write a short note whenever a rule is changed, check the queue at the same time each day, and have somebody else read any change before it goes live. None of these is surprising, but the discussion made it clear that we had all been saying we would do them without ever agreeing to.

Plans for the next stretch:

- Finish the clean-up of old entries, with a final check on what has been removed.
- Draft the status note idea from the survey, then try it with a small group.
- Review the shared calendar migration and make sure nobody has lost an event.
- Sort out the equipment log properly, including a quick count of what we actually have.

A few of us talked about whether the retrospective format itself needs refreshing. It is easy for the same four people to speak and the others to nod. One suggestion was to ask everyone to write their points on cards first and to read them out in random order. We will try that next time, and see if it brings out some quieter voices.

On the personal side, I am planning to take a couple of days off once the quarter is closed. I have mentioned it to the team and will leave a short handover note in the notebook. It is a good prompt to make sure that my own work is written down well enough for somebody else to pick up.

That is the end of this diary entry. The log goes on, and the next one will cover whatever the new quarter throws at us. Let us hope it is dull.
