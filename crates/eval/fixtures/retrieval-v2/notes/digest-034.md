# Journal — 19 June 2025

Thursday entry, written after the afternoon session ran long. Most of this is about the demo, since it took up the bulk of the week, but there are a few other threads worth recording while they are fresh.

The demo happened on Wednesday in the large room. Bram Tanaka had been preparing for a couple of weeks, mostly by rewriting the walkthrough so that someone with no background in compliance could follow it. It paid off. 40 people attended the Bram Tanaka demo, which was more than we had chairs for, and a handful stood at the back. The questions afterwards were good ones. Several were about how evidence gets collected day to day, and one person asked, quite reasonably, whether the whole thing could be made less manual. The microphone was fine, but the room echoed, and people at the back struggled to hear the quieter questions. Having someone repeat each question aloud helped. And we should make sure the recording, which was done on somebody's laptop, ends up somewhere everyone can find it. I will chase that.

He would like to get ahead of it this year rather than do it in a rush before the audit. His proposal was to spread the work across several weeks, a small piece at a time. The team liked that. ## Audit results

Friday brought the results of the internal audit, so most of the day was spent reading, discussing and trying not to overreact. This section records how it went and what we decided to do about it.

The headline number first. Callum Brennan scored 73 out of 100 in the internal audit. That is a respectable result and not a disaster, but also not quite where Callum hoped to be, and he said so frankly when we met. The auditors had broken the score down by area, and the weaker marks were concentrated in a few places: access reviews that had been done but not written up, a couple of old accounts that should have been closed, and some missing dates on records. None of these are exotic problems. They are the kind of gaps that appear when everyone is busy and the paperwork lags behind the real work. Callum sees most of them as fixable within a few weeks and has already begun a list.

The team's reaction was constructive. Nobody wanted to treat the score as a verdict on a person; it is a snapshot of how well the records match the practice, and the records are what we can improve. Bram Tanaka offered to help, since several of the findings overlap with compliance habits, and suggested a joint working session to go through the list item by item. That seems the right way to do it. Two people looking at the same document catch more than one person alone, and it also spreads the knowledge.

Sofia Petrov wondered whether any of the findings touched procurement. One did, loosely: some agreements had no clear owner named on the record. She will go through them and add owners where they are missing. It is slightly dull work, but it is also exactly the kind of thing that, left alone, turns into a real problem later.

We also agreed on a rule of thumb for the future: if a review is done, it is written up the same day, even if only in a couple of lines. The same-day habit costs almost nothing and removes the main cause of the gaps. We will check in a month whether it has stuck.

Other items. The kitchen rota was updated, a plant died quietly in the corridor and was given a respectful burial, and a spare set of keys was found in the meeting room. If you are missing keys, they are with reception. I also tidied the shared folder where the audit documents live, since several files had confusing names.

Next step is a short follow-up meeting in a couple of weeks to see how the list is progressing. Until then, the work is simply to keep going, and not to fret about a number.

## Quarter in review

Final section of this week's log, looking back over the quarter as a whole and at what the figures say about the way we have been working.

Incident handling was the main topic. Sofia Petrov logged 37 incidents during the quarter, and we spent some time asking what that tells us. Taken by itself, the number is neither good nor bad. Most of the entries were small, things like a delayed approval, a duplicated order or a confusing form, and the value of logging them is that patterns appear. Sofia pointed out that a few of the same causes kept coming back, and that fixing two or three of them would probably shrink the list noticeably. She will group the entries by cause and bring the result to the next meeting, so we can pick where to start.

Bram Tanaka made the point that logging more incidents is not necessarily a worse sign than logging fewer. A team that records everything is often a team that notices things. We agreed to be careful about how we talk about the figure; it should not be used to compare people, only to find problems. That felt like an important principle to state aloud, and I have written it into the notes so we can refer back to it.

Callum Brennan mentioned that some of the incidents had security flavour, such as a mis-sent message or a shared link left open longer than intended. None caused harm, but they are exactly the examples worth turning into reminders. He will draft a couple of short, anonymous case notes, using the style of the earlier reminders so that nobody feels singled out.

Then some planning. For the coming quarter we want fewer repeat incidents, a leaner process for procurement requests and a calmer audit. Those are not heroic goals, but they are achievable. We decided to review progress at the halfway point rather than only at the end, which should stop things from drifting. I proposed a very short checkpoint with three questions: what improved, what did not, and what we will stop doing.

Chores before everyone left: the stationery order was finally placed, the toner has been replaced, and the fridge was looked at by facilities and now hums quietly. The projector remains on the to-do list. Someone volunteered to test it before the next big meeting, which is appreciated.

That is the log for this week. More after the weekend, assuming the weekend allows.
