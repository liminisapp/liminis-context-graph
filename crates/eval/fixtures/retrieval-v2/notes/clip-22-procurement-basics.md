# A plain-language guide to purchasing for startups

Young companies tend to buy things in a hurry. A subscription is approved in a chat message, a contract is signed on the founder's laptop, and months later nobody can say what was agreed. Purchasing has a reputation for being dull, but a few light habits can spare a growing business a good deal of expense and confusion.

**Know what you are buying.** Before asking for quotes, write down what the purchase must achieve and what would count as success. Vague requirements produce vague offers, and comparing them becomes guesswork.

**Compare more than price.** The cheapest option is not always the least expensive. Consider support, the length of the commitment, how easily you can leave, and what happens to your data or work if the arrangement ends. Ask for the terms in writing, and read the renewal clause in particular, since automatic renewals are a common source of surprise.

**Decide who can say yes.** Even a tiny team benefits from a simple rule about who may approve spending and up to what level. It prevents duplicate purchases and makes it clear where to send a request.

**Keep a record.** A shared sheet listing each supplier, what is supplied, the cost, and the date it comes up for review is enough to start. Check it regularly, because the cheapest time to renegotiate or cancel is before the renewal date, not after.

**Build relationships.** Suppliers respond better to customers who are clear, prompt and fair. A short conversation about what you need often achieves more than a long email thread.

None of this requires specialist tools or a dedicated role. It requires attention, and a willingness to write things down before the pace of work makes that feel like a luxury.
