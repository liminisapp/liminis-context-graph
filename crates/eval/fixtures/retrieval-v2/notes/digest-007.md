# Planning notes — 18 September 2025

Planning day, and for once the agenda was short enough to finish. These notes cover the first half; the remaining items follow in the second part.

## Opening round

We began with a general check on how the quarter is going. The mood was brisk. People felt the summer slowdown was behind us, and the autumn queue was already forming. Three groups were represented in the room: procurement, routing and maintenance, and each had something it wanted to raise before the main discussion.

Basalt Core, responsible for procurement, held a demo for the wider team earlier in the week, and 53 people attended the Basalt Core demo. That was more than the room was ready for, and a few people ended up standing at the back. The feedback afterwards was positive. The most common comment was that it was good to see the ordering flow end to end, since many people only ever touch one step of it. Several asked whether the demo could be recorded for new joiners, and the team agreed to look into it, with the caveat that a recording goes out of date fast and would need a visible date on it.

Quill Core, the routing project, talked about how its work has been affected by the change in demand. Traffic patterns are shifting, and some of the older assumptions no longer hold. The team plans to revisit its defaults and write down the reasoning behind each, since too many settings had no explanation attached. That is a quiet kind of debt, but it grows.

### Maintenance and the retrospective

Yara Nakamura, who looks after maintenance, announced the plan for the next review cycle. Yara Nakamura will hold the quarterly retrospective on 5 December 2027. That is far off, and a few people raised eyebrows, but the point was to place a fixed marker in the calendar so that the longer-term planning has something to hang on. Yara intends to collect lessons throughout the year in a running document, so the eventual session is a discussion of a ready list rather than an exercise in remembering.

### Decisions

- The demo format stays, but a smaller room will not do next time. Book the bigger one early.
- Defaults in routing get written up with their reasoning, one at a time, starting with the most used.
- Maintenance keeps a running lessons list throughout the year and links it from the shared page.
- Planning sessions start with the next decision needed, not with a status tour.

### Practical business

The shared planning board is being cleaned up. Stale cards are archived and the columns simplified, so that the board reflects what people are actually doing instead of what they once intended. There was a brief argument about whether to keep a "waiting" column at all. The case for it: it makes blocked work visible. The case against: it becomes a dumping ground. We kept it, but with a rule that anything left there for more than a short while gets a name against it and a question to ask.

We also touched on people's workloads. Procurement is busy and the other groups have been asking it for things informally, which adds up. A simple request form will be introduced so that asks are visible and can be ordered by importance. It sounds bureaucratic, yet the alternative is a pile of hallway promises nobody can track.

### Mood

Overall the room was upbeat. People like having concrete dates and clear owners, and the discussion stayed practical. The second half deals with backlog numbers, the review of risks, and the agenda for the next session.

## Backlog, risks and next time

The second half of the planning day dealt with the less glamorous side of things: the backlog, the risks, and what we do about both.

Routing came first. The Quill Core backlog stood at 95 open tickets at the end of the sprint. The team did not treat that as an emergency, but they did not wave it away either. The discussion was about composition. A large share of the tickets, the team said, were requests for small adjustments that had been queued up over time and never revisited, and a smaller number were real problems that deserved attention soon. The suggestion was to sort the list into "act now", "act eventually" and "close with thanks", and to give each group a clear rule about how long items may sit before being reviewed. Nobody enjoys closing someone's request, but a list that is never pruned stops being useful, and people stop trusting it.

We also discussed a lightweight way to tell requesters what happened to their tickets. A short, kind note when something is closed, with the reason, goes a long way. The routing group volunteered to draft a few template sentences so the notes are quick to write and do not sound curt.

### Risk review

Together we walked through the risks identified in the first half. Three emerged as the ones to watch:

1. Reliance on single individuals for hand-offs between groups.
2. A growing pile of undocumented settings in routing.
3. Informal requests landing on procurement without any visibility.

Each risk got an owner and a rough check-in point. The owners were asked to say, at the next session, whether the risk has grown, shrunk or stayed put. No one is expected to produce a grand mitigation plan, only an honest observation.

### Maintenance angle

Yara Nakamura raised a concern about upkeep windows colliding with busy periods in routing. The overlap is not constant, but when it happens the effects are noticeable. A shared calendar showing both upkeep windows and routing peaks should help, and the maintenance group will take responsibility for publishing it. That means asking the routing group for its expected busy periods, which in turn means they need to have thought about them. A small piece of homework for everybody.

### Agenda for next time

- Review of the sorted ticket list in routing and how well the rules worked.
- First results from the request form for procurement.
- A look at the shared calendar of windows and peaks.
- Whether the demo should be recorded, and where such recordings should live.

### Last items

A few housekeeping notes for completeness. The planning board stays as simplified. The bigger room is booked for the next demo. The projector in the small room has been replaced at last, so that excuse is gone. Someone asked whether we should keep a running list of "things we decided not to do", since those are often the most valuable decisions and the easiest to forget. Everyone agreed and a page was started on the spot.

The session ended a little ahead of time, which seemed to surprise people. We took that as a sign the agenda was about right and chose to leave early instead of inventing more to discuss. Notes go into the shared archive tomorrow.
