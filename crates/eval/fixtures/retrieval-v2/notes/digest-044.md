# Journal — 24 May 2025

Saturday morning, so this is being written at home with a second coffee, looking back over a week that felt busier than its calendar suggested. I like doing the journal away from the office. It is easier to see what mattered when the building is not humming around me.

## The shape of the week

Monday was a slow start. Half the team was out, the other half was catching up on email, and the only real decision of the day was where to hold the afternoon meeting, since the usual room had been double-booked. We ended up outside on the terrace, which turned out to be a surprisingly good setting for a planning discussion. Nobody checked their phone, partly because the glare made the screens unreadable.

Tuesday and Wednesday were focused work. I spent most of both days on a document that I had been putting off for weeks, a plain description of how we handle the stock side of things from the moment something arrives to the moment it is recorded as available. It sounds simple, but when I tried to write it down, I found at least four steps where different people do it differently and none of them is wrong. That has to be resolved, but not by me alone. I will bring it to the group as a set of questions rather than as a proposal.

Thursday brought the demo, which I will describe in a moment, and Friday was mostly cleaning up after it.

## The inventory demo

Lena Moreau ran a demonstration of the revised stock-tracking process on Thursday afternoon. 86 people attended the Lena Moreau demo, which was a pleasant surprise, as the room was booked for far fewer and a few of us ended up sitting on the window sills. The demo was well structured. She began with the problem as people experience it, the half-hour spent looking for an item that should be on a shelf, then walked through what changes in the new process, then ran a live example with a deliberately awkward item to show how exceptions are handled.

I took notes during the questions, since they are the part I find most informative. People asked about:

- how returned items are recorded, and whether they go through the same steps as new arrivals;
- who is responsible when a count does not match;
- whether the process works for items that are shared between groups;
- how long it takes to learn, and whether there will be a short guide.

Lena answered each calmly, admitted where she did not yet have an answer, and promised to follow up. She has a good way of saying "I do not know yet" without it sounding like an evasion. I wish more of us would do that. The room responded well to it.

Afterwards, a few of us stayed behind and talked about which of the proposed changes are the most important. The consensus was that the exception handling is the heart of it. A stock process that works on a normal day is easy. One that still works on a bad day is the real achievement.

## Chores and housekeeping

The less glamorous part of the week included clearing the shared storage cupboard, which had become a place where objects went to be forgotten. We found cables, a broken lamp, three umbrellas, and what looked like a prototype of something nobody could remember building. Most of it was thrown out or recycled. The few items worth keeping have been labelled and put on the shelf where they belong.

The label printer, as ever, caused trouble. It does not like being asked to print more than ten labels at once and gives up quietly, which is the worst way for a machine to fail. We now have a note taped to it.

## Small decisions

We decided to hold the stock process review at the start of next month rather than the end of this one, so that people have time to read the document before they discuss it. We also decided to send the draft out as a short page with a few questions at the top rather than as a long file that nobody opens. Whether people will read it is another matter, but we have done what we can.

Another small decision, made almost by accident: the Friday lunch is now officially in the calendar so people stop asking whether it is happening. It is.

I will continue below with the second half of the journal, including the training side of things and a few notes on compliance.

## Compliance, learning and what is next

Continuing the journal after a walk to clear my head. The second half of the week had a quieter, more reflective quality, and I want to get it down before it fades.

### Compliance notes

Much of Friday went on the compliance side. Bram Tanaka has been working through the evidence we are expected to keep and has been trying, with moderate success, to get people to file it in one place rather than wherever it happened to land. His approach is refreshingly practical. Rather than send out a long policy reminder, he made a short checklist of what a compliant record looks like, and then sat with a few people while they went through their own folders against it. Several of them found gaps they had not known about, and fixed them on the spot. That is a far better outcome than a lecture.

He also raised, gently, that some of our routines depend on a specific person remembering to do something at a certain time. That is fine until the person is away. We discussed ways of making the routine visible, either as a recurring card on the board or as a line in the weekly log, so that the work does not vanish when a particular individual is out. Nobody liked the idea of adding more process, but everybody liked the idea of not being caught out, and the recurring card seems like a fair compromise.

### Learning

Learning came up in a side conversation on Thursday evening. Bram Tanaka completed 38 hours of training this term, mostly in a series of short courses on records and audit practice, and said the most valuable bit was not the content so much as the habit of setting aside time for it. He suggested that the rest of us try to block out an hour a week for similar learning, whatever topic we choose. A few people nodded, a few looked at their calendars with open dread. I think the idea has merit, provided it is protected time and not something we do when everything else is finished, since everything else is never finished.

We talked about how to make that realistic. The proposal was to keep it simple: put a recurring hour in the calendar, mark it as busy, and treat it like any other meeting. If it gets moved, it gets moved to another slot that same week, not dropped. We will try it for a couple of months and see whether anyone actually keeps to it.

### Looking back at the week

If I had to sum up the week, I would say it was about making things visible. The stock process was made visible through the demo and the document. The compliance routines were made visible through the checklist and the recurring card. Even the storage cupboard was made visible, in the sense that we finally looked inside it. There is a pleasing consistency to that.

Not everything went smoothly. We ran over time in two meetings, one demo room was too small, and a couple of requests slipped through the cracks and needed chasing. Nobody was upset about any of it. It is the sort of friction that comes with a team that is busy and mostly cheerful.

### Plans for next week

1. Circulate the stock process draft with the short list of questions at the top.
2. Schedule the review for the start of next month.
3. Try the recurring hour of protected learning time.
4. Turn the compliance checklist into a one-page handout and pin it where people will see it.
5. Fix the label printer properly, or retire it with ceremony.

That is the journal for the week. Time to go and water the plants, which have been on a strict diet of neglect since Tuesday.
