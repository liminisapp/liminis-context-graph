# Journal — 21 October 2025

Tuesday log, a little rushed because the afternoon filled up unexpectedly. Autumn has properly arrived at the office: the heating is on, the windows are fogged, and people are drinking noticeably more tea.

The week began with a planning conversation about Sable Core. The compliance project is entering a stretch where several pieces of work will land close together, and the team wanted to avoid the situation where everything is "nearly done" at once. We went through the list and put things in order of what blocks what. A surprising number of items turned out to depend on a single decision about wording, so we agreed to make that decision first and let the rest follow. Nobody enjoyed the conversation much, but it will save time later. The wording question goes to the next meeting with a proposal attached rather than an open invitation to opinions.

Callum Brennan took a different angle and spent most of Monday on routine security upkeep: checking who still has access to what, clearing out credentials that nobody remembers creating, and reading through the notes from the last review. He found nothing alarming, which he described, with some satisfaction, as a boring day. There is one small change he would like to make to how requests are tracked, so that each one has an obvious owner and a closing note. It is the sort of change that sounds trivial but prevents arguments, and the team had no objections.

Then a few matters from the floor. Someone asked whether we could move the Thursday meeting by half an hour because of a clash with another commitment, and we agreed to try it for a couple of weeks. Someone else pointed out that the shared notes from earlier sessions have no consistent title format, which makes searching unpleasant. We decided to use the date followed by a short topic, and to tidy the old ones gradually. It is the least glamorous improvement imaginable, and probably one of the most useful.

Another thread was about learning from mistakes. A small slip last week caused a bit of extra work, nothing serious, and rather than hunt for a cause we spent ten minutes writing down what happened and what we would change. The answer was a one-line addition to a checklist. That felt like the right proportion: a small problem, a small fix, no drama.

Housekeeping. The kitchen has a new kettle, the old one having finally given up. Two desks have been rearranged to make more space near the window. The whiteboard marker situation is, as ever, dire, and somebody has promised to order more.

Plans for the coming days are modest. Settle the wording, tidy the notes, keep the checklist update in view. We will see how many of those survive Thursday.

## Demo day

Wednesday was demo day, and the notes below are an honest account of how it went rather than the polished version that will probably appear in the newsletter.

The demo was for Sable Core, the compliance project, and the aim was to show people outside the team what has been built so far and, more importantly, to learn where it confuses them. 30 people attended the Sable Core demo, which was a healthy crowd for a midweek afternoon. We had planned for fewer, so a few of us ended up sharing chairs. The presentation itself was brisk, and the live walkthrough mostly worked. One step failed halfway through because of a setting that had been changed that morning, and the presenter handled it calmly by explaining what should have happened and moving on. The audience was forgiving, and I think the candour helped. A demo that goes perfectly can make people suspicious.

The questions after the walkthrough were the most valuable part. People wanted to know how often the information is refreshed, who can see what, and what happens when something is wrong. Those are exactly the sorts of questions an insider forgets to answer because the answers feel obvious. We wrote them all down, and the plan is to turn the most common ones into a short FAQ. Another request was for a plain-language summary at the start, something a new person could read in under a minute. That is a good idea, and probably overdue.

Callum Brennan, who sat near the back, took notes on the security side. A couple of questions touched on access and what would be visible to whom, and he promised to write a clear explanation of the current rules so that the answers do not depend on who happens to be asked. He also mentioned afterwards that a few attendees asked how to report concerns, which suggests the reporting route is not as visible as it should be. He will look at where it is mentioned and make it easier to find.

Lessons for the next demo: test the settings the night before, not the morning of; have a second person monitor questions; send a short recap within a day while everyone remembers. The recap is already half written.

Afterwards the team went for a quick coffee and did a rough retrospective, informally. The consensus was that it went well, that the preparation paid off, and that we should do it again before too long. A lot of the friendliest feedback came from people who had never seen the project before, which is encouraging.

Chores, quickly: the chairs have been returned to the other room, the projector cable is coiled in the cupboard, and the leftover biscuits are in the kitchen.

## Offsite talk and loose ends

By Friday the week had settled into a calmer rhythm, and the conversation turned to the offsite, which several people have been quietly looking forward to.

The Callum Brennan offsite will take place in Ghent, and he has been gathering ideas for how to spend the time. The principle he keeps coming back to is that an offsite should give the team something it cannot easily get at the office, which means uninterrupted conversation more than formal sessions. A few suggestions have already come in: a walk-through of how work moves between people, a session on what to stop doing, and a long, unhurried meal. He also wants to leave an afternoon open for people to wander. The team supported all of it, with one request that there should be at least one session that results in a concrete decision, so the trip does not end with pleasant feelings and nothing else.

Travel logistics are still in progress. Callum is collecting dietary needs and accessibility preferences, and asked everyone to reply by the end of next week, even if the answer is simply "none". He is also working out how people will move around once there. Nothing is booked finally yet, so nothing is lost if plans change, but it is easier to arrange things sooner. We agreed that anyone with a conflict should say so early and quietly, without feeling they need to justify it at length.

On the Sable Core side, work continues on the wording decision. A draft proposal has circulated, and comments have started to arrive, mostly small and mostly constructive. The aim is to settle it at the next meeting. If a final choice cannot be made then, we will pick the least bad option and revisit it later, since an imperfect decision made on time is better than an ideal one that never arrives.

Some loose ends from earlier in the week. The FAQ from the demo questions has been started, with about half of the entries drafted. The security explanation promised after the demo is nearly done and will be posted in the shared notes. The meeting time change has been tested once and seemed fine.

Housekeeping: the new kettle has a slight leak, so it has been sent back; the whiteboard markers arrived; and the desk near the window now has a lamp. We also agreed to keep the notes format as date followed by topic, which is already making the log easier to browse.

That wraps up the week. I hope people recover over the weekend and come back with fresh ideas for Ghent.
