# Team log — 10 July 2026

Friday log, written in a hurry before people leave. The week was busy but in a cheerful way, and the main topic was travel, which always generates more conversation than it deserves.

Offsites first, since several were settled this week. The Tern Core offsite will take place in Turin, which will please the people who have been lobbying for somewhere warm. The telemetry side wanted a venue with good wireless and a room big enough to put dashboards on a wall without everyone craning their necks. Turin seems to meet both needs. Separately, the Sofia Petrov offsite will take place in Valencia. That one is a procurement-focused gathering, and the idea is to combine meetings with suppliers and some time for the team to compare notes on how requests flow. Nothing is booked beyond the basic decision, so details will follow.

Routing has been the other talking point. Priya Varga has been thinking about how work gets directed between the teams, and her observation was that too many requests arrive without enough context for the receiver to act. A person reading the message has to guess what is wanted, by when, and why it matters. Her proposal is a three-line habit for any request: what you need, when you need it, and what happens if it does not arrive. It is not a form, only a convention, and it should be easy to adopt. A few people already do something like it and say it saves a round of questions.

We spent a while discussing how to make that habit stick. Conventions without reminders fade quickly. The idea was to put the three lines at the top of the shared request template so that anyone filling it in sees them every time. We will also mention it in the next newcomer session.

Smaller items from the week:

- The wiki's travel page was updated with a short checklist: passports, insurance, expense rules, and who to tell. It is much shorter than the old one.
- A reminder that anyone travelling should leave contact details with the team in case of problems.
- We cleared the backlog of small purchase requests that had been waiting for sign-off. Most were approved quickly once someone actually looked at them.
- The weekly summary was trimmed again. It is down to decisions and requests.

A longer conversation was about the balance of in-person and remote work around these offsites. Some people value the time together enormously; others find travel draining and would prefer to join remotely. We agreed to make attendance flexible where possible, and to make sure remote participants are properly included, with a camera, a microphone, and a person assigned to watch the chat. It is easy to promise inclusion and forget it in practice, so the assignment of a watcher is a concrete step.

On procurement matters, the usual chores continued. Contract renewals are being collected into a single list so nothing sneaks up unnoticed. Whoever updates the list should add the owner's name and a rough date for any action, even if the date is a guess. A guess with a date is far more useful than a note saying "soon".

Telemetry had a quiet week. A few dashboards were tidied, and the team agreed to retire several views that nobody had opened in a long time. Retiring things is underrated; each view you keep has to be maintained, and unused ones silently rot.

That is the first half of the log. The second half covers the remaining offsite and a few notes on planning for the next quarter.

## Second half: one more offsite and quarter planning

Continuing the Friday log. This half starts with the last offsite decision and moves on to planning.

The Priya Varga offsite will take place in Gdansk. The routing group has been arguing amiably about where to go for weeks, and Gdansk finally won on the strength of its practicality: easy to reach, plenty of places to eat, and a good venue for working sessions. The idea is to use the time to walk through how requests move between teams, with real examples, and to agree a few simple rules that everyone can live with. As with the others, only the place is fixed so far. The agenda is a blank page, and the first job is to decide who actually needs to be in the room for which sessions.

That brought us back to the request habit from the first half. Several people said they would like to try the three-line convention before the offsite, so they can bring real experience of whether it works. That seems sensible. A trial of a few weeks, with notes on what helped and what did not, would give the routing discussion something solid to chew on instead of opinions.

On to quarter planning. The group looked at what is likely to compete for attention over the next stretch. A few themes stood out:

- Procurement work will keep growing as more contracts come up for review. Sofia Petrov and the team need breathing room to handle that without dropping other work.
- Telemetry work on Tern Core wants to clean up its alerting, which has grown noisy over time.
- Routing needs time for the request habit to bed in, along with a simple way to see whether it is helping.
- Everyone wants fewer meetings. Nobody agrees on which ones to drop, which is a classic problem.

We settled on a modest rule for planning: for each team, pick no more than two main goals, and write each in a sentence that a person outside the team could understand. Anything beyond that goes on a "maybe" list. The point is to make trade-offs visible early, instead of discovering them in week nine. There was some scepticism about two being too few, but the counterargument that three or four goals usually means none of them gets finished was persuasive.

Other business:

1. Finance asked for a rough view of travel costs as soon as possible. A shared sheet is being started, with columns for who, where, when and approximate cost. Estimates are fine at this stage.
2. A newcomer asked how to find out who is responsible for what. The team page is the answer, but it needs updating, so that is a chore for next week.
3. The shared calendar will show offsite dates as soon as they are fixed, so people can plan around them.
4. The suggestion box was emptied and read. Most suggestions were about the kitchen, which tells you something about priorities.

Finally, a thought for next time. These logs are most useful when they record the reasoning behind decisions as well as the decisions themselves. I have tried to do that here, but I suspect I have been too brief about why Gdansk beat the alternatives. Whoever remembers the debate, please add a line. Otherwise in six months we will wonder why we chose it, and the only record will be that we did.

That is the end of this log. Have a good weekend, and travel safely wherever you are heading.
