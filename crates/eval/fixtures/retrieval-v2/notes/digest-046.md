# Weekly digest — 25 June 2026

Thursday digest. The week was warm, the office was sticky, and the fan by the window finally earned its keep. Here is what happened, in roughly the order it happened.

## Monday and Tuesday

Monday opened with a planning huddle that overran, mostly because we tried to settle three questions at once and ended up settling none. Lesson noted: one question per meeting, even if that means more meetings. We did agree on a rough order of work for the week and who would shadow whom on the trickier items.

Tuesday was the day of the long email thread about routing. Quill Core, the project responsible for routing, had been getting odd reports from people who said messages were taking the scenic route. Nothing alarming, but enough that the team wanted to look at it properly before it became a pattern. Someone in the team suggested keeping a simple log of every odd report for a few days so we could look for common ground instead of guessing. That is now a shared note, and people have started adding to it without being asked, which is encouraging.

Lumen Core, which covers security, sent round a short reminder about password hygiene and the shared accounts we are meant to be retiring. It was polite and a little weary, as these reminders always are. The team agreed to set aside an afternoon next week to actually do the retiring rather than keep promising to.

## Chores and small decisions

- The kitchen rota has been updated. Everyone has one slot per month, and swaps are allowed if you arrange them yourselves.
- The old shared drive folder is being archived. If you still use anything in it, say so by Friday.
- A decision on the standing desk: we are buying one, to be shared, and it lives by the window. First user gets to pick the height and everyone else has to live with it.
- We will stop using the long form for expense notes and switch to a one-line version unless a receipt is unusual.

## A thought on pace

There is a recurring feeling in the team that we start many things and finish a few. It surfaced again on Tuesday, and no one argued with it. The idea floated is a visible "doing" column of no more than a handful of items, so that starting something new means finishing or pausing something old. It might be too neat, but it costs nothing to try for a fortnight.

I also want to say thanks to whoever fixed the broken link in the welcome pack without making a fuss. It had been annoying everyone for weeks and went unmentioned until it quietly disappeared.

More tomorrow, once the heat breaks and we can think properly.

## Midweek and a bit of reflection

Wednesday was heads-down for most people, so there is less to say than usual. A few threads continued from earlier in the week and one new one popped up.

The routing log is growing. Entries are mostly short, a line or two each, and a pattern is starting to show: the odd behaviour tends to bunch up around busy periods rather than appearing at random. That gives the Quill Core team something concrete to chase, and someone is going to sit down with the log and draw a rough picture of when things go sideways. No conclusions yet, and nobody should treat the hunch as fact until the picture exists.

On the security side, Lumen Core asked for a quiet word with anyone still using a personal device for work email. This is not a crackdown, just a headcount so the team knows what it is dealing with. A short, anonymous form will go round so people can answer honestly without feeling watched.

### Meeting hygiene

We had a mini post-mortem on Monday's overrun huddle. The conclusions were small and sensible:

- Send the agenda the day before, even if it is only three lines.
- Name one person to keep time and give them permission to be annoying about it.
- If a topic needs more than ten minutes, schedule it separately.

Nobody thinks this will solve everything, but it should stop us repeating the same mistake every other Monday.

### Odds and ends

The coffee machine has been descaled, and the difference is noticeable. Please do not tell the person who did it that it was overdue; they know. The recycling situation is also improving, with the new labelled bins making a visible difference.

We had a short conversation about holiday cover. Summer is coming, and the team wants a simple shared calendar that shows who is away and who is covering what. A volunteer is drafting a one-page guide so handovers do not depend on remembering who said what in a corridor.

### What I would like to see next

A calmer end to the week. The list for Thursday and Friday is manageable, and the biggest risk is people agreeing to extra tasks out of politeness. If you cannot take something on, say so early, and say it kindly. I would sooner hear "not this week" on Wednesday than discover on Friday that something quietly slipped.

One more bit of tidying: the notes in this digest are going into the shared archive each Friday, so if you write something here that you want to find later, give it a clear heading. Future you will be grateful.

## Retros and routines

Today's entry leans toward process, because a few of us got into a good conversation about how we review our own work.

Quill Core held its quarterly retrospective on 12 April 2026, and the notes from it have been sitting in the shared folder since. I reread them this week and was struck by how much of it was about small friction rather than big failures. People wanted clearer hand-offs, shorter meetings, and fewer surprise requests that arrive on a Friday afternoon. Several of those themes have come back up this week, which suggests the retrospective named real problems, but we have not made as much progress on them as we hoped. We decided to schedule a short check-in against the retro notes rather than wait for the next full session.

The practical outcome: one person will pull out the three actions that mattered most and report whether each is done, in progress, or abandoned. Abandoned is an acceptable answer as long as someone says so.

### Dull but useful

- Keep a running list of requests that arrive late in the week. If the list gets long, we will talk about it at the next stand-up instead of absorbing it silently.
- Agree a default for "who answers first" on shared channels, so questions do not sit unanswered because everyone assumes someone else has it.
- Block an hour every other week for housekeeping: tidying documents, closing old tickets, deleting stale branches.

### Security corner

Lumen Core's access review is still in progress. The security team is being methodical, which is exactly what you want, but it does mean a few people are waiting for confirmations. Please be patient and avoid chasing individually; a single message to the shared channel is enough, and they will get to it.

### Informal chat

At lunch there was a long debate about whether the office should adopt a quiet hour. Opinions were split roughly down the middle. Some people find open conversation helpful and others find it exhausting. A trial period was proposed, starting with an hour after lunch, with a clear sign on the door of the meeting room so people know when it is safe to talk. We will try it and see.

### Closing note

I finished the day by clearing my own backlog of small tasks, and it felt oddly good. Highly recommend. Tomorrow is for the access review follow-ups and the first look at the routing picture.

## Thursday evening, wrapping up

Last section of this week's digest, written with a cup of tea going cold beside me.

The headline from today is the access review. It turned out better than feared. Lumen Core, the security project, completed 49 hours of training this term, and that effort shows: the team found issues quickly and explained them clearly, without turning the discussion into a lecture. People noticed. Several colleagues said it was the first time a security walkthrough felt useful instead of a box-ticking exercise, and a couple asked if the same format could be reused for other topics.

On routing, the picture from the log was drawn this afternoon. It confirmed the hunch about busy periods, though it also showed a second pattern nobody had expected, which will need a closer look. The Quill Core team will keep logging for another week before deciding whether to change anything. The wise move is to wait for more data than to jump on a half-explanation.
