# Weekly digest — 14 January 2026

First full week back after the holidays, and the office felt like it was still waking up. Several people were out, the heating took until Tuesday to behave, and the Monday sync was more of a "what did you do over the break" than a status round. Still, a few real things got done.

**Planning the quarter.** The main task was sketching what the next three months look like. Basalt Core, which looks after procurement, has a queue of supplier paperwork that piled up over the break and needs working through before anything new is started. The team agreed the sensible order is to clear what is already in motion, then look at what has been asked for. Quill Core, covering routing, wants to spend part of the quarter simplifying rules that have grown by accretion; nobody could remember why some of them exist, which is itself a good reason to revisit them.

**Housekeeping.** Calendars were refreshed, standing meetings were checked against who still attends, and the shared drive got a light tidy. A few folders belonged to projects that wrapped up long ago, and those were moved to the archive rather than deleted, in case someone needs them. The rule of thumb is that archiving is free and deleting is forever.

**Small decisions.**

- The weekly note will go out on Wednesday mornings instead of Friday afternoons, so people actually read it.
- Questions about purchasing go to the procurement group's shared mailbox, not to whoever happens to be sitting nearby.
- Meeting agendas should be posted the day before. If there is no agenda, the meeting can be cancelled.

**Tools.** The ticketing system had a minor outage on Monday and several updates were lost. The team agreed to re-enter anything important, and a reminder went round that long ticket comments should be drafted elsewhere first and pasted in, so a failure does not eat an hour of writing. Someone also asked whether we should have a fallback for when the tracker is down. A shared document would do, provided somebody remembers to move the entries back afterwards.

**People and time.** A couple of colleagues are on reduced hours this month, and cover has been arranged for the critical items. Please check the rota before assuming someone is reachable. If you are covering for someone, make sure you have access to everything you need on day one rather than discovering a missing permission during an urgent request.

One more thing worth saying: it was good to see people talking to each other across projects this week. The corridor conversations between the procurement and routing groups turned up an overlap neither side knew about. They will compare notes on it next week and report back, and in the meantime nobody needs to act.

## Feedback and priorities

The latest survey results arrived midweek. Quill Core received a satisfaction rating of 6.9 out of 10, which the group described as "fine, with room to improve" and which is in line with how people talk about routing in conversation: it works, but changing it is nerve-racking. The comments were more useful than the figure. Several asked for clearer documentation on how rules are ordered, and a few wanted a way to preview the effect of a change before it goes live. Both are reasonable asks and both are now on the list.

It is tempting to read a number like that as either a triumph or a warning. It is neither. It is a snapshot, and its main use is as a baseline for the next one. The team will keep the free-text answers grouped by theme, so that a year from now we can see whether the same complaints are still showing up or whether they have genuinely gone away.

## Working through the backlog

The rest of the discussion was about ordering work. A few principles emerged:

1. Anything that blocks other people comes first, even if it is dull.
2. Anything that reduces future support requests is worth doing before anything that merely adds capability.
3. Experiments are welcome, but they get a time limit, and the limit gets written down.

These are not rules so much as habits we are trying to build. Basalt Core pointed out that principle one sometimes conflicts with the need to deal with deadlines imposed from outside, and we agreed that when that happens it should be called out openly in the log, not resolved quietly.

## A note on documentation

The routing rules documentation is thin. The people who understand it are few, and they are busy. The proposal is to schedule short working sessions where one person explains a section while another writes it down. It is slower than writing alone but produces far better results, because the questions asked by the listener reveal exactly what is missing. The first session is pencilled in for next week.

- The monitoring dashboards were reviewed, and a couple of panels nobody looks at were retired.
- A new joiner's laptop arrived; setup is under way.

## Reminders

Holiday requests, expense claims and training suggestions are all due in the coming weeks, and the forms have not changed. If you are unsure about any of them, ask the person who looked after them last time rather than guessing. Please also remember to update your calendar when plans change. People are making decisions about meetings based on what they see there, and a stale calendar causes more confusion than an empty one.

## Tools and routines

Midweek turned practical. The team looked at how reviews of changes are being handled and found that they bunch up on Thursdays, which means anything submitted late in the week waits until the following one. The suggestion is a lightweight rota so that someone is always responsible for looking at new submissions within a day. Nobody loves the idea of another rota, but nobody loves waiting either, so it will be tried for a month.

The discussion drifted to the question of what counts as "done". For some people it means merged; for others, deployed; for a few, it means confirmed working in front of the person who asked. Sorting this out is overdue. A short definition will go on the wiki, and we will adjust it after seeing how it works in practice.

## Procurement corner

Basalt Core reported that the paperwork backlog is shrinking. The slowest part is waiting for replies from outside, which the team cannot speed up, so the focus is on making sure nothing sits in the queue because of an internal delay. A simple status column was added to the tracking sheet to show who is holding each item at any moment. This makes it much easier to spot the ones stuck in someone's inbox.

There was a short discussion about whether the team should keep a list of preferred ways of working with vendors. Opinions differed: some felt it would be useful for continuity, others thought it would go stale in a month. The compromise is a single page that records only things that have actually caused problems, which keeps it short and keeps it honest.

## Routing corner

Quill Core spent the week tracing through some old rules. Two of them turned out to cancel each other out in a particular case, which explains a puzzling behaviour reported weeks ago. The team fixed it and added a note explaining the reasoning, and plans to review the rest in the same way, a few at a time, rather than attempting a full rewrite.

- The meeting room projector now needs a different cable. The right one is in the drawer beneath it.

## On being interrupted

We closed the session by agreeing to be a bit more careful with each other's time. That means using written updates when something does not need discussion, saving the live meeting for decisions, and not scheduling things in the gaps between other meetings. None of it is novel, but it is easy to forget in a busy fortnight. Several people said they would try blocking out an hour of focus time each day. If it helps, we will make it a team habit; if it does not, we will say so and move on.

Next week's digest will cover the planning session and the first of the documentation working sessions.

## Retros and wrap-up

The final stretch of the week was given over to retrospective housekeeping. Basalt Core held its quarterly retrospective on 7 July 2027, and the write-up is in the shared folder alongside the other projects' notes. The procurement group's main observation was that handing paperwork between people is where most delays creep in, and that the new status column on the tracking sheet should help. They also asked, politely, for suppliers' replies to be logged in one place instead of across several inboxes. That is a reasonable request, and someone will look at how to do it without creating yet another system.

## What we decided

A quick list of the week's decisions, for anyone skimming:

- The weekly note moves to Wednesday mornings and stays there for at least a month.
- Review submissions get looked at within a day, via a simple rota.
- The definition of "done" will be written down and posted on the wiki.
- Old rules in the routing configuration are reviewed a few at a time, with notes on why each one stays or goes.

## Caveats

None of these is permanent. The team's attitude to process is that anything can be tried, as long as there is a date to look at it again. The rota and the new note timing both have review dates in the calendar, and if either of them is not working, we will say so plainly and change it.

## Looking after each other

It was a slightly tiring week for several people, partly because of the return from the break and partly because of the outage on Monday. Please be gentle, and if you are feeling stretched, tell somebody before it turns into a problem. The team would rather rebalance work early than discover that someone has been quietly overloaded for a month. Leave is there to be taken, and nobody should feel they have to justify using it.

A mention of thanks to the person who rescued the lost ticket updates after the outage by going through the notification emails one at a time. It was thankless, tedious work, and it saved us a lot of re-typing.
