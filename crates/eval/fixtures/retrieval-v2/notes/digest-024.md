# Weekly digest — 27 June 2025

Short week, long Friday. Writing this up from the back table while the kettle argues with itself, so apologies for the sprawl.

**Monday stand-up.** We spent most of it re-sorting the board. Half the cards had drifted into the wrong column over the last fortnight, and nobody could say with confidence which ones were actually blocked. We agreed on a simple rule: if a card has not moved in five working days, the owner either comments on why or archives it. Someone in finance had also asked for a tidier trail on purchase requests, which fed into the same conversation, so we tagged those cards with a plain label to make them easy to find later.

**Offsite planning.** The big item of the week was settling where the Basalt Core offsite will take place. After a fair amount of going back and forth about travel time, cost and who has to cross a border, the group landed on Tampere. Nobody objected, which in this team counts as enthusiastic agreement. The next step is working out a rough agenda and checking that the people who manage the procurement side can get away for the full stretch. I volunteered to draft a first pass of the schedule, with plenty of slack for the things that always overrun.

**Inventory corner.** Lena Moreau finished her counting pass on the shared storage shelves and flagged a handful of items that exist on the sheet but not in reality, plus a couple that exist in reality but not on the sheet. She is also working through her learning plan alongside the day job, and she mentioned that she has now completed 18 hours of training this term. She says the module on stock reconciliation was the most useful, mostly because it gave her words for problems she had already been quietly fixing by hand. We should probably ask her to run a short walkthrough for the rest of us when she has a free hour.

**Chores.** The fridge in the kitchen has been cleared out, and the recycling rota has been updated so that it actually reflects who is in the office on which days. The label printer is working again after somebody discovered the roll had been loaded upside down. Please leave it alone now. If it jams, tell the team channel instead of prodding it.

**Analytics demo.** On Wednesday afternoon Noor Okafor ran a demo of the new reporting views. 14 people attended, which was a good turnout given that it clashed with a couple of other calls. Questions were mostly about how often the numbers refresh and whether the filters can be saved per person. Noor took notes and promised a short follow-up message with answers, plus a recording for anyone who missed it. A couple of people asked whether the views could eventually replace the weekly spreadsheet export; the answer was a cautious maybe, pending a month or so of everyone using both.

**Small decisions.**
- We will keep the Friday digest in this one log rather than splitting it by topic, since the cross-references are the whole point.
- Meeting notes go in the log the same day, even if they are only three lines.
- Nobody schedules anything over the lunch hour on Thursdays, which was already custom but is now written down.

That is the first half of the week sorted. More below on the remaining threads, the open questions and what carries over into next week.

## Open threads

Picking up from the first half. These are the things that did not close this week and probably will not close next week either, but they are worth keeping in view.

The shared calendar is still a mess. Recurring meetings created by people who have since changed roles keep appearing with no obvious owner. We went through them one by one on Thursday and deleted about half. For the rest, whoever last attended is now the nominal owner, which is not elegant but at least means someone will notice when the invite goes stale. The Basalt Core planning meeting was one of the survivors, and its invite now lists a single organiser instead of three.

On procurement paperwork, there was a short and slightly tetchy exchange about who signs off on small purchases. The informal answer has always been "whoever is nearest", which works until it does not. We are not going to write a policy for this, since nobody wants one, but we did agree that anything above the petty-cash comfort zone gets a one-line note in the log before the money moves. That gives us a trail without adding a form.

Inventory follow-ups from Lena Moreau's count are mostly simple. Two boxes of cables turned out to be sitting under a desk in the quiet room. A monitor that was marked as spare is in fact someone's second screen and was quietly reclaimed. The more interesting finding was that our naming for storage locations is inconsistent, so the same shelf appears under three labels depending on who wrote the entry. She proposed a short naming convention and we will review it as a group before anything gets renamed. Renaming things in the middle of a count is how you lose a week.

Analytics requests that came out of the demo have been gathered in one place. Noor Okafor sorted them into three rough piles: things that are quick, things that need a conversation, and things that are really a different project wearing a disguise. The first pile is already scheduled. The second needs a slot in next week's sync. The third gets politely parked, with a note so nobody feels ignored.

A few smaller observations from around the room:

- The new desk arrangement has people sitting closer than they used to, and the noise during calls has gone up. Headphones are encouraged, and the quiet room is for actual quiet, not for taking a call that could have happened at your desk.
- Someone suggested a monthly clear-out day for the shared drive. General approval, no volunteers yet.
- The onboarding checklist for newcomers is out of date in at least four places. We will fix it the next time a new person starts, since that is when the errors will be most visible.

None of this is urgent, and that is sort of the point of having a weekly log. Small things get noticed, written down and nudged forward before they turn into big things.

Questions for next week: who owns the calendar clean-up long term, whether the storage naming convention should be applied retroactively, and whether the reporting views need an owner of their own or can stay with the person who built them.

## Thursday and Friday

Thursday started with a fire drill that turned out to be a test, which nobody had been told about, so the first twenty minutes of the day were spent standing in the car park making small talk. On the plus side, we now know where everyone parks.

The main meeting of the day was the planning session for the coming month. The agenda was deliberately short. First, we looked at what is genuinely in flight versus what only appears on the board because nobody removed it. Second, we picked the two or three things that should not slip. For procurement, the priority is getting the supplier comparison finished so that the Basalt Core team can make a call before the offsite. For inventory, the priority is closing out the reconciliation Lena Moreau has been chipping away at. For analytics, it is making the new views dependable enough that people stop keeping private spreadsheets alongside them. Noor Okafor was fairly blunt that dependability matters more than adding anything new for now.

We also revisited the question of working hours around the holiday period. The consensus was relaxed: people can flex as long as someone is reachable each day, and the on-call arrangements stay as they are. A shared document will hold the rough availability so that nobody has to ask in the channel every other day. It is worth saying that the point of that document is to reduce questions, not to police anyone's time.

Friday was quieter. A few of us used the morning to tidy the wiki, which had accumulated duplicates of pages with slightly different titles. Merging them was oddly satisfying. We kept the better-written version of each and left a short redirect note on the other, so old links still lead somewhere sensible.

In the afternoon there was a casual retrospective of the week, nothing formal. The things people said went well: the demo, the shelf count, the calendar clean-up. The things people said went less well: too many meetings on Tuesday, a long email thread that should have been a five-minute call, and the label printer saga. We agreed to try a no-meeting Tuesday afternoon for a few weeks and see whether it makes any difference.

Some housekeeping items from the end of the day:

- The spare keys have been returned to the drawer. Please keep them there.
- The plant by the window is being watered by a rota that nobody has admitted to joining, but it is alive, so the system works.
- Someone should check the first-aid kit before the end of the month.

One more note on tone. Several people said this week that the log has become more useful as it has become less formal. The earlier entries read like minutes, and nobody went back to them. The recent ones read like a diary of the team, and people actually search them. Let us keep it that way.

## Wrap-up and next week

Closing out the log for the week with a short list of carry-overs and a few thoughts.

**Carry-over items.**
1. Draft the offsite agenda and circulate it to anyone on the procurement side who needs to plan travel. I will do this early next week, and comments are welcome in whatever form is easiest.
2. Finish the supplier comparison. This is the one with an actual deadline, so it gets protected time on Monday and Tuesday.
3. Review the proposed storage naming convention as a group. Fifteen minutes at the start of the sync should be enough, provided nobody wants to relitigate shelf numbering.
4. Send out the follow-up answers and recording from the analytics demo, if that has not already happened by the time you read this.
5. Try the no-meeting Tuesday afternoon, and report back on whether it helped.

**Things we learned.** Boards drift unless somebody cares for them. Small rules, like the five-day comment-or-archive one, work better than big clean-up projects. Demos are worth doing even when the audience is small, because the questions reveal what people actually want. And counting things physically is humbling: the sheet is never right, and the gap tells you more about your process than about your stock.

**A word on workload.** A couple of people mentioned feeling stretched this week, partly from the number of short meetings and partly from the context switching between planning and chores. Nobody is in trouble, but it is worth noting. If your plate is full, say so in the channel before it overflows, and the rest of the team will see what can be moved. This is much easier to do on a Monday than on a Friday evening.

**Thanks.** Thank you to everyone who stayed for the retrospective, to whoever fixed the label printer, and to whoever quietly emptied the dishwasher without being asked. The small acts keep the place running.

**Looking ahead.** Next week should be calmer, with fewer standing meetings and more time for the work that does not fit into a calendar slot. The sync on Wednesday will focus on the offsite agenda and the supplier comparison. Thursday's lunch hour remains sacred. Anyone who has not yet added their availability for the holiday period should do that before the weekend, so the shared document is useful when people start looking at it.

If anything in this digest is wrong or missing, please edit it directly rather than posting a correction in the channel. That is the entire reason it lives in a shared log. And if you are reading this months from now, trying to remember why we chose one thing over another, I hope there is enough here to jog your memory. If not, add a line, so the next person has a better trail than you did.

That is it for this week. Good weekend, everyone.
