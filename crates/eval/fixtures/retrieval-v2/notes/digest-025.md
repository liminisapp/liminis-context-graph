# Review notes — 8 June 2026

Review notes for the week, written on Monday while the memory of the last few days is still reasonably sharp. As usual, these are a mixture of meetings, chores and the small calls that never get a proper document.

The big conversation of the week was about how we handle requests that arrive from outside the team. Support is the obvious front door, and Juniper Core has been absorbing a steady stream of questions that are not really support questions at all: people asking where to find something, who owns a thing, or whether a process has changed. The group's suggestion is to build a short page that answers the ten most common ones, with pointers to the right owner, so that a human is only needed for the rest. Nobody thought this was glamorous, but everyone agreed it would save time. The idea is to start by reading through the last few weeks of requests and tallying what comes up repeatedly, rather than guessing.

On maintenance, Nimbus Core mentioned that the routine upkeep of older systems keeps being postponed because new work always seems to take priority. This is a classic trap. The upkeep is invisible until something breaks, and then it becomes urgent and expensive. We talked about setting aside a fixed slice of each sprint for it and treating that slice as untouchable. A couple of people were nervous that it would be eroded the first time a deadline loomed, so the compromise is that any raid on the maintenance slice has to be noted in the log with a reason. Social pressure, mild but real.

Analytics, with Helix Core, had a quieter week. They are mostly working on tidying the definitions behind their reports, because two people had produced different numbers for what should have been the same measure. The cause was a subtle difference in how each had filtered the data. They are writing the definitions down in one place and asking anyone who builds a report to point at them. Another case of the wiki earning its keep.

Chores that came up:

- Review the list of shared accounts and remove those that nobody has used recently.
- Update the contact page, which still lists a couple of people who have moved on.
- Check that the weekly backup report is actually being read by somebody.
- Replace the batteries in the meeting-room clock, which has been wrong for ages and has misled at least one person into thinking they were late.

A note on tone: the discussion about requests got a little heated, not because anyone disagreed on substance, but because everyone had a story about being asked the same thing for the tenth time. We took a short break and came back calmer. Worth remembering that frustration is usually a signal that something should be automated, written down or removed.

Next week we will read through the request tallies and see whether the short answers page has legs.

## Sprint numbers and the maintenance slice

Picking up the thread from earlier in the week, now that the sprint has closed and the board is something I can read without squinting.

The maintenance conversation had a follow-up. The Nimbus Core backlog stood at 76 open tickets at the end of the sprint. Looked at one way, that is a healthy amount of upkeep waiting to be done; looked at another, it is evidence that the old habit of postponing it has caught up with us. The group's view is that it is a bit of both. A fair number of the tickets are small and could be closed in an afternoon each, while a smaller set are real pieces of work that need planning. The suggestion was to sort the lot into those two groups, knock off the small ones in a focused burst, and then give the larger ones a proper place in the next plan, with owners. Nobody wants a backlog that only ever grows.

We spent a little time on why backlogs of this kind build up. The honest answer is that upkeep tickets rarely have a deadline, so they lose every comparison with something that does. One idea was to give each of them a soft review date, so that they cannot sit unexamined forever. Another was to make the upkeep slice visible on the board in its own lane, so that its progress is as easy to see as feature work. Both are cheap to try.

Smaller items from the review:

- Support, via Juniper Core, would like a clearer route for passing a problem to the maintenance group without the details being lost in the handoff. A short form with a few required fields was proposed. I think people will tolerate it if it really is short.
- Analytics, via Helix Core, confirmed that the shared definitions page now exists and has been linked from the report templates. Early feedback is positive.
- The shared accounts list was reviewed and a handful of dormant accounts were switched off. If anyone finds themselves locked out of something unexpectedly, that is probably why. Shout and it will be restored.

There was a brief but useful exchange about how we report progress in these reviews. Several people pointed out that we spend more time listing numbers than talking about what they mean. A backlog figure by itself says little; what matters is the direction and what is driving it. We agreed that next time each group will bring one sentence on the trend, not just the count.

On the logistics side, the meeting room will be unavailable for part of next week because of building work, so the review may move to the larger space downstairs. Check the invite when it arrives. I will also try to remember to tell the person who always forgets which floor that is.

Closing note: the upkeep burst is scheduled for the middle of next week. If you have small tickets you have been meaning to close, now is a good time to find them.

## Training, travel and wrap-up

The last part of the review covered people-focused topics: training, travel and general wellbeing. These tend to get squeezed to the end of the agenda, so I made a point of giving them room.

Training first. Juniper Core completed 19 hours of training this term, mostly on handling difficult conversations and on the tooling that supports the front-line work. Feedback from the people who attended was positive, with the practical sessions rated above the lecture-style ones. A recurring remark was that the training felt more valuable when it was tied to a real case from the previous week. The group would like to build on that by having people bring an example along next time. There was also a note that sessions are easier to attend when they are in the middle of the day instead of at the tail end, when everybody is tired. We will try to bear that in mind when booking.

On travel and group events, the analytics team has settled on a place for its offsite. The Helix Core offsite will take place in Leeds. The conversation was mostly about logistics rather than content, since the programme is still being shaped. People would like a balance between working sessions and time to simply talk, since the latter is usually what the group misses most when everyone is busy. A few suggestions for the agenda came up, including a session on the shared definitions and a longer look at what the group should stop doing. I would add that a fair share of the benefit of an offsite is that it gets people out of their usual chairs.

Wellbeing, briefly. People are working hard and some are visibly worn down. We discussed making sure that time off is actually taken and not just approved. A reminder was also issued that nobody should feel obliged to reply to messages outside working hours, however keen they are. A short list of quiet-time norms might help, and I offered to draft one for comment.

Housekeeping to finish:

- The contact page has been brought up to date, and the people who had moved on are gone from it.
- The batteries in the meeting-room clock have been replaced, and it has been confirmed that the time is now right.
- Next review will be held in the larger room if the building work is still going on.

The Nimbus Core maintenance burst is on the calendar for next week, and a couple of people from other groups have offered to help close small tickets. That is a good sign. I think we are coming round to the idea that upkeep is a shared responsibility instead of something that falls on whoever has the least to do.

The log is open for corrections, as always. If I have misrepresented anything, fix it, and add a note about what you changed. Thanks to everyone for a constructive session.
