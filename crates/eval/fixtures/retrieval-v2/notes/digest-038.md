# Operations diary — 26 July 2025

Saturday catch-up, written from home with the window open and the neighbour's lawnmower doing its best to ruin my concentration. Several things from the past week need recording before I forget them, so here goes.

## What happened this week

The week started with the usual Monday triage. We went through the pile of small requests that had built up over the weekend, sorted them by who could actually do something about them, and dropped about half into a "not this month" pile. That sounds harsh, but it is the only way to keep the real work moving. The people who raised the dropped items were told directly, which I think matters more than the decision itself.

On Tuesday the team held a longer session on how we welcome new colleagues. Aiko Osei, who is responsible for onboarding, shared the results of the latest feedback round: she received a satisfaction rating of 7.7 out of 10 in the survey. She was pleased but not complacent. The comments were what interested her most, and they were more useful than the number. People liked the personal check-ins and found the paperwork heavy. Several asked for a clearer sense of what the first month should look like, and one person wrote that they would have liked to meet their neighbours on the team earlier. Aiko will turn those into a short list of changes and bring it to the next meeting.

We also spent time talking about how to make the check-ins easier to sustain. If one person carries them all, they become a bottleneck, so the idea is to spread them across the team with a simple script of questions. Nobody wants the check-ins to feel like interviews. The script stays deliberately short and friendly, and people may skip questions that feel awkward.

### Small decisions

- The feedback form stays anonymous. A suggestion to add names was politely declined.
- We keep a single place for onboarding material and stop scattering it across folders.
- The Wednesday lunch gathering is now official, which means someone has to organise it. Volunteers rotate.
- Printing is discouraged unless a document must be signed.

### Chores

The storage room finally got swept. It was an archaeological dig: cables from three generations of laptops, a box of conference badges, and a surprising number of single gloves. Most of it went to recycling. The rest went into labelled boxes, and the labels are legible, which is a novelty.

The shared calendar had a few stale entries from before the summer. They were cleared, so if you notice something missing that you actually needed, say so and it will go back in. Also, a gentle reminder that holiday dates should go in the calendar as soon as they are booked, not the night before leaving.

### Mood check

Morale seems healthy. People are tired in the pleasant way that follows a productive stretch, not the grim way that follows a crisis. A few mentioned looking forward to time off, and the team is working out a cover plan so nobody returns to a mountain of mail. I intend to write the cover plan down in the next entry so it is findable.

That is the first half of the notes; the second half covers what is coming in the weeks ahead and a couple of items that need follow-up.

## Plans and follow-ups

Picking up where the first half stopped, with the forward-looking items and a few threads that are still open.

### Holiday cover

The cover plan turned out simpler than feared. For every absence, one named person picks up urgent items and one named person is the point of contact for anything that can wait. Both go in the shared calendar next to the absence, so nobody has to guess. We tested it against a pretend scenario in the meeting, and it held up. The weak spot is the handover itself: people leave a day too late to write things down. The rule going forward is that the handover note is due the working day before the last day in the office, not on it.

### Procurement thread

Basalt Core, the project that looks after procurement, is working through a stack of approvals, and there was a conversation about whether the approval path could be shortened. Right now a request can pass through several hands, and each hand adds a little delay. The team does not want to remove the checks, only the pauses between them. One proposal was to give routine, low-risk items a fast lane with a single sign-off, while anything unusual still gets the full route. It sounds reasonable and nobody raised a serious objection, though several asked for a definition of "routine" that does not collapse the first time someone is in a hurry.

Another small idea: a weekly ten-minute slot dedicated to clearing the approvals queue, with everyone who might sign something present. It is dull but efficient, and it might be the best use of ten minutes the team has found in months.

### Onboarding follow-up

From the earlier feedback discussion, Aiko Osei will draft the shorter first-month plan and share it for comment before the end of next week. She asked for reviewers who joined recently, since they remember what was confusing. Two volunteers stepped forward immediately, which suggests the topic matters to them. The plan will include a gentler first day, with fewer meetings and more time to get set up.

### Things to watch

- Equipment lead times, which have been unpredictable lately.
- The shared inbox, which fills faster than anyone empties it.
- Meeting load in the run-up to the holidays, when everyone suddenly wants a quick sync.

### Notes on tone

I would like to record that the last few team conversations have been notably cooperative. When someone disagreed, they offered an alternative instead of just objecting. That is a habit worth protecting, and I will say so when the group meets next.

### Small practical items

The spare chairs are going to a neighbouring office that asked for them. The broken desk lamp has been replaced. A request was made for a bigger bin in the kitchen, and I am leaning toward yes, since the current one overflows by Wednesday every week. The whiteboard wipes have been restocked, and the new markers actually work.

That covers the middle of the log. The final part picks up the end-of-sprint numbers and the closing notes for the week.

## End of sprint

The sprint closed on Friday, and this section covers the numbers, the mood and the tidy-up.

The Basalt Core backlog stood at 31 open tickets at the end of the sprint. People had different reactions. Some thought it looked heavy, others pointed out that a good chunk of the tickets were small and could be knocked down in an afternoon of focused effort. The more useful discussion was about the shape of the list instead of its length: a few old items had been sitting there for so long that nobody remembered why they existed. We agreed to go through the oldest ones and either close them with a short explanation or give them an owner and a rough date. Leaving them to age quietly helps no one.

The team also talked about how tickets are written. A recurring problem is a title that says what is wrong but not what "done" looks like. A simple habit might fix it: finish every ticket description with a single line that begins "Done when". It costs a few seconds to write and spares the person picking it up a lot of guessing.

### Review notes

- The fast lane for routine approvals will be trialled for the next sprint, with a short review at the end.
- The weekly approvals slot starts this coming week.
- Old tickets get triaged in a single sitting, with the whole team present, instead of trickling through over days.

### Onboarding corner

Aiko Osei reported that the first draft of the shorter first-month plan is nearly ready. She has taken the earlier comments seriously and trimmed a lot of what used to be required reading. The remaining question is how to handle people who join mid-sprint, when everyone is busy and there is little capacity for hand-holding. A buddy system was suggested, in which each newcomer is paired with a colleague who is not on the busiest part of the sprint. It seems sensible, though it relies on people actually having the slack.

### Housekeeping

The storage boxes are labelled and shelved. The kitchen bin is bigger. A new batch of notebooks arrived and has been left near the printer for anyone who needs one. The old badges were thrown out after a final look to check nobody wanted them as keepsakes.

### Closing thoughts

It was a tidy sprint without much drama. The numbers are not perfect, but the team knows where the weak spots are and has a plan for each. I will count that as a decent result.
