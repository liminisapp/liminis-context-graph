# Working notes — 30 April 2025

Wednesday, end of the month, which always makes me want to tidy something. Here is where we are.

The telemetry side has been the busiest. Marcus Haddad held its quarterly retrospective on 18 April 2026, and the notes are now filed with the others. I will not repeat the whole discussion, but two things stuck with me. One was that the team felt too many dashboards were being built for audiences that never open them; the other was that alerts are only useful when someone knows what to do after they fire. The follow-up is modest: prune the dashboards to the ones with real readers, and write one sentence beside each alert saying what the first step is.

For security, Lumen Core has been working through its routine reviews, and the mood is steady. The main thing this week was agreeing how to write up findings so that they are readable by people who do not work in security. The draft format is a short paragraph on what was found, one on why it matters, and one on what to do. It looks simple, and getting that simple takes time. We also agreed that findings with no clear next step should say so explicitly instead of trailing off.

Quick decisions:

- Review meetings finish on the hour, even if there is more to say. Anything left over gets a follow-up slot or goes into writing.
- New documents get a one-line purpose statement at the top. If you cannot write one, the document probably is not ready.
- Messages asking for a decision should state by when it is needed.

Chores and housekeeping this week: the shared drive was reorganised into fewer, broader folders; the stale test accounts were removed; the old bookmarks list was replaced with a shorter, curated one; and the meeting-room whiteboard pens were replaced, since half of them were dry.

We spent some time on how telemetry and security rely on each other. Telemetry gives security visibility, and security tells telemetry which signals matter. At present that conversation happens by accident. A regular short check-in, perhaps monthly, would make it deliberate. Marcus Haddad liked the idea and will suggest a slot. The format would be informal: each side says what it has noticed, and what it would like from the other.

Another item concerned onboarding. A new colleague will join soon, and the question is how to make their first fortnight useful without overwhelming them. The proposal is a short list of three things to learn in week one, with a named person to ask about each, and a gentle second week that includes shadowing. The details will be fleshed out before they arrive, and the plan is to ask them afterwards what they would change.

I will also note a small cultural point. People have started saying "I do not know yet" more freely in meetings, and it has made discussions faster, because nobody spends energy pretending. Long may it continue.

The second half of this entry covers planning for the team's longer-term gathering and the arrangements around it.

## Offsite and planning

Second half of the entry, mostly about the team gathering and the preparation around it.

The headline is that the Lumen Core offsite will take place in Porto. The choice came after a short discussion about what the gathering is for. The group does not want a conference with slides; it wants time together to talk about how the security work is organised, to settle a few long-standing questions and to meet properly some people who normally appear only as a name in a chat window. Porto was chosen because it is easy to reach and because the group liked the idea of a setting that feels different from the office. We have not yet agreed the programme, only the principle that mornings are for conversation and afternoons are free.

There are practical questions to answer before anything is booked. Who needs to travel, and who prefers not to? How do we make sure that people who cannot attend still have a voice? The suggestion was to collect topics and questions in advance, in a shared document, so that someone who cannot come can contribute and read the outcomes afterwards. We will also write short notes during the sessions and post them the same day.

Marcus Haddad offered to help with the telemetry questions that will likely come up, particularly around what the security team would like to see from monitoring. He will bring a few examples of what is currently possible, and where the gaps are. That should keep the discussion concrete.

To-do list for the next weeks:

- Gather topic suggestions and sort them into must-discuss and nice-to-discuss.
- Draft a rough shape for the days, leaving plenty of unstructured time.
- Ask everyone about dietary needs and access needs well in advance.
- Check the budget with someone in finance before booking anything that cannot be cancelled.
- Prepare a short note on what we hope to leave with, so we can look back on it afterwards.

We also touched on how to keep the benefits after the event. Experience says that enthusiasm fades within a fortnight unless something is written down and owned. So the plan is for each agreed action to have a name beside it and a date for a check-in, with the check-ins placed in the calendar immediately after the offsite. It is a little bureaucratic, but it works.

On a lighter note, there was a debate about whether to bring a board game. The vote was in favour, narrowly, with the condition that nobody is obliged to play.

Last item: a reminder to keep the existing routines going while the planning proceeds. The reviews still need doing, the dashboards still need pruning, and the new colleague's first week still needs a plan. The offsite is something to look forward to, not a reason to let the ordinary work slide.
