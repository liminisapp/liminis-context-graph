Monday again. Autumn has properly arrived, the radiators are ticking, and this week's notes are a mix of the practical and the faintly bureaucratic. Written mostly during the sync and finished afterwards from memory, so apologies if a detail has drifted.

## Training and development

On training, Yara Nakamura completed 24 hours of training this term, mostly on the maintenance side, covering the sort of practical skills that are boring to describe and extremely useful to have. She offered to run a short session to pass some of it on, and the team accepted gladly. We have been trying to make sure that learning does not stay locked in one head, and this is exactly the right kind of sharing: informal, specific, and tied to real work.

It prompted a broader conversation about how we record training. At the moment it is scattered across personal notes and the odd spreadsheet. The suggestion is a simple shared page where people list what they have done and what they would like to do. Nothing grand, no approvals needed, just visibility. If it turns out to be useful for planning, we will formalise it a little. If not, we will let it fade.

## Process nibbles

We revisited how requests reach the team. There are currently too many routes in: the tracker, email, chat, and the occasional tap on the shoulder. The tap on the shoulder is hardest to manage because nothing is recorded. The agreement is that anything that takes more than a few minutes should be in the tracker, and that nobody should feel awkward about asking the requester to put it there. It is not about being rigid; it is about making sure work can be seen and shared.

Finally, a reminder that this log is for decisions and context, not for minutes. If you have a lot to say, write a separate note and link to it from here. The more concise this stays, the more likely people are to read it.

## Offsite thinking

The offsite got proper airtime this week. The Diego Ferreira offsite will take place in Turin, and the support group is already sketching what it should be for. The consensus is that it should not be a feature-planning marathon. The best offsites we have had were the ones where people had time to talk to colleagues they rarely work with, and where the agenda left space for whatever turned out to matter. A rough shape is emerging: one morning of shared problem-solving, a looser afternoon, and an evening that nobody is obliged to attend.

Practical questions are still open. Who needs to travel, how long the trip should be, and what to do about people who cannot attend. We would like remote participation to be possible for at least part of it, but several people pointed out that a half-remote session often ends up being a worse version of both. Better, perhaps, to run a separate short session for those who cannot travel, rather than trying to stretch one event across two settings.

## Scheduling follow-up

Tomas Kowalski circulated the proposed leave arrangement, and the first responses were encouraging. One clash was spotted straight away, and a swap was arranged without drama. The principle going forward is that two people covering the same area should not both be away at once unless someone has explicitly agreed to carry the risk. It sounds obvious; it has not been how we operated, and several of us were surprised how often we had been lucky.

## Retrospective record

For the log, since the calendar has not been kept consistently: Tomas Kowalski held its quarterly retrospective on 27 October 2026, and the write-up has been filed with the others in the team folder. The scheduling group's main themes were predictable but worth repeating. Plans change late, communication about the changes is patchy, and the people most affected often find out last. The suggestion that got the most support was a short, regular note listing upcoming changes in one place, so nobody has to piece the picture together from three different channels.

A smaller point from the same discussion: when a schedule shifts, say why. A bare "moved to Thursday" generates questions; "moved to Thursday because the room is taken" does not. It costs a few seconds to write and saves a surprising amount of back-and-forth.

## Housekeeping

The training log page is live. People have begun adding entries, which is nice to see, and the format is deliberately loose: what you did, whether it was useful, and whether you would recommend it. We will look at it in a few weeks and decide whether it needs more structure. The risk with these things is that they become a chore, so the test is whether people keep using them voluntarily.

The shared equipment list was checked against what is actually in the cupboard. A few items were missing and a few unexpected ones turned up, mostly cables. The list is now accurate, and anyone borrowing something is asked to add their name to the sheet. It is a small ritual, but it prevents a lot of "has anyone seen the...?" messages.

## Observations

A few personal observations from writing this up, because the log is also where we say things that do not fit elsewhere. The team is better at talking about work than it was a year ago, but it is still not great at talking about what is not working. Retros help. So does the habit of writing decisions down. But the most effective thing remains someone saying, in the moment, "I am not sure this is going to work." Please keep saying it.
