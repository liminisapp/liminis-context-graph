# Sync notes — 27 June 2026

Saturday-adjacent Friday, so attendance was thin and the sync was shorter than usual. I am writing it up while it is fresh, mostly from scribbles on the back of the printed agenda.

## Billing side

Cedar Core, the billing group, kicked things off with a rundown of the month-end routine. The dull-but-essential part is the reconciliation of invoices against what was actually delivered, and this month it went more smoothly than the last. A few mismatches turned up, as they always do, and each was traced to either a late entry or a duplicated line. Nothing was disputed. The group wants to build a short checklist of the most common mismatch causes so the person doing the reconciliation does not have to rediscover them every time. There was agreement that a checklist is a good idea, along with a quiet admission that nobody has yet written it.

We also talked about how billing questions from other groups are handled. At the moment they arrive by whatever route the asker prefers: direct message, email, a comment on a card, or a casual word in the corridor. That makes it hard to know what is outstanding. The proposal is a single shared thread, with one line per question and a name next to it, so that anyone can see what is open. It is not a ticket system, and nobody wants one. It is a list.

On the tooling side, someone asked whether the monthly report format could be simplified. Currently it has several sections that almost nobody reads. The suggestion was to keep the totals and the exceptions, and move everything else into an appendix that is generated but not circulated. Seemed sensible, and the group will try it for one cycle.

## Onboarding side

Fable Core, which looks after onboarding, had a bigger agenda, as there are several newcomers expected over the summer. The main theme was making the first week less of a scramble. Right now, a new person's first days involve a lot of waiting for access, hunting for documents and being told to ask someone who turns out to be on leave. The group walked through the first-week experience step by step and marked the places where people tend to get stuck. The top three were access requests, finding the right channels, and knowing who to ask about what.

An idea that got traction was a one-page "who to ask" sheet, kept short and reviewed each quarter. It would not list everything, just the ten or so questions newcomers most often have, with a name or a role next to each. The point is to reduce the number of times someone has to feel awkward about asking. We also agreed to prepare accounts and equipment before the first day rather than on it, which sounds obvious but has not always happened.

A small but welcome decision: each newcomer will be paired with a buddy who is not their direct manager, someone they can ask the silly questions without worrying about how it looks.

## Feedback and audit

The middle of the sync was given over to numbers, which always makes the room go a little quiet.

First, the feedback survey. Cedar Core received a satisfaction rating of 8.4 out of 10 from the groups that rely on billing. That is a solid result, and the written comments backed it up: people said invoices are clear, questions get answered promptly and the month-end process is predictable. The criticisms were mild and mostly about how long it takes to get a correction through once an error has been found. The group took that on board and will look at whether the correction path can be shortened. Someone joked that the best feedback is the kind where the complaint is about speed rather than accuracy, and I think that is right.

Second, the internal audit. Fable Core scored 72 out of 100 in the internal audit. Reactions around the table were mixed. Some felt it was a fair snapshot of a group that is still putting its processes in order; others thought it undersold the practical work being done, since several of the marks were lost on missing paperwork rather than missing practice. The honest assessment, offered by the group itself, was that both views are true. The work is largely happening, but the documentation lags behind it. The audit notes listed a handful of gaps, such as access records not being kept in one place and the onboarding steps not being written down in a consistent format. None of them are dramatic, and all of them are fixable.

We agreed to treat the audit as a to-do list rather than a verdict. The approach is to pick the gaps that are cheapest to close first, get a few quick wins on the board, and then deal with the bigger ones. It is worth saying that the tone of the conversation was constructive. Nobody was defensive, and there was no sense of blame. The people who run the audits want the results to be useful, and the people being audited want to improve.

A few related thoughts from the room:

- Numbers like these are easiest to live with when they are compared over time. One reading tells you where you are; two readings tell you where you are going.
- The survey and the audit measure different things and should not be set against each other. A group can be well liked and still have gaps in its paperwork, and vice versa.
- If either of them changes direction sharply, it is worth asking what happened that quarter.

## Closing items

The tail end of the sync covered the odds and ends that never fit anywhere else, so here they are, roughly in the order they came up.

**Summer cover.** Holidays are starting to cluster in July, which means we need to be a bit more deliberate about who covers what. The billing group is the most exposed, since month-end falls in a week when two people are away. The plan is to write down the month-end steps in enough detail that someone from outside the group could follow them with a bit of help. That doubles as the checklist already on the list, so there is a happy overlap. For onboarding, the concern is the opposite: a cluster of newcomers arriving while experienced people are away. The buddy pairing should help, but we agreed that each newcomer also gets a named fallback contact for the first fortnight.

**Shared tools.** There was a short grumble about the number of different places where information lives. Cards, threads, documents, wiki pages, and the occasional photo of a whiteboard. Nobody has the energy for a big consolidation project, so the compromise was a lightweight rule: if it matters, it ends up in the wiki with a link from wherever the conversation happened. That way, the original discussion can stay messy, and the durable part has one home.

**Communication norms.** A reminder that questions to either group should say what is needed and by when. A message that just says "quick question" followed by silence for ten minutes is hard to answer. The better version states the question in the message, so that the reader can reply without a back-and-forth.

**Small wins this week.**
- The first-week sheet for newcomers has a draft, and two people have already commented on it.
- A long-standing billing query from a few weeks ago was finally closed.
- The shared thread for billing questions is live, and has three entries already.
- The old projector is gone and the replacement works on the first try, which is more than can be said for most things in this building.

**What I will do next.** I will tidy these notes into the wiki, send the actions to the owners, and put a short reminder on the calendar for the follow-up. If I have forgotten anything, tell me. The log is cheaper to fix than it is to ignore.

**Mood.** Good, on balance. The week had a few awkward moments, including the audit discussion, but people dealt with them professionally and with some humour. The audit in particular could have gone sideways and did not, which is a credit to the people involved.
