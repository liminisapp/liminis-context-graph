# Operations diary — 24 October 2025

A longer entry than usual, because the audit round finished and there is a lot to digest. I will try to keep it readable rather than exhaustive.

First, the audit. The scoring is out for everything we put forward, and the overall picture is decent. Nimbus Core scored 95 out of 100 in the internal audit, which is the best result we have seen from any maintenance effort. The reviewers singled out the way the team records what it does and why, with small notes made at the time rather than reconstructed later. That is a habit worth copying elsewhere, and several people have already asked how it is done. The honest answer is that it is dull and disciplined, with no tool or trick behind it. Someone from the team will run a short session on the routine for anyone who wants it.

On the scheduling side, Tomas Kowalski scored 69 out of 100 in the internal audit. He took it with good humour and was clear-eyed about it afterwards. Most of the lost marks were about sign-offs: plans were changed, and the approval trail did not always follow. The scheduling work itself was judged sound. He has already drafted a short routine so that any change to the calendar carries a line saying who agreed it and when. He also pointed out that the audit measures the paper trail as much as the work, which is true, but also fair, because the paper trail is what lets anyone else understand what happened.

A discussion followed about how to treat results like these. Our view, repeated from earlier rounds, is that the score is a prompt for a conversation and not a ranking. We do not want teams competing over marks, and we especially do not want people avoiding hard work in order to look tidy. The place for the numbers is the improvement list, where each low item is turned into a specific habit. A high score should lead to sharing what works, and a lower one to asking for help.

Moving on to the week's practical matters:

- The shared workspace was reorganised. Folders now follow the structure of the work rather than the structure of the old org chart, and a redirect note sits in each retired location. Expect to be annoyed for a few days, and then to forget it ever looked different.
- The monthly access check was done, and there was nothing unusual. A couple of stale permissions were removed, and the owners were told.
- A vendor raised a question about how we use their service, and someone in finance replied with the details. We are waiting for a response, and the team has no action in the meantime.
- The test builds are faster since the overlapping jobs were rescheduled. People noticed, which is the point.

There was also time for the kind of conversation that does not fit an agenda. A few of us talked about how the quarter has felt: busy, a bit scattered, but with a visible trail of small fixes. Several people said they find the diary more useful than they expected, partly because it makes it easy to see what we decided and why. A suggestion was made to add a standing line at the end of each entry listing decisions only, so someone scanning the log in future can find them quickly. We will try it, and if it feels like homework, we will drop it.

The remaining part of this entry covers the billing work and the plan for the coming weeks. For now, the action list from this section is small: share the maintenance recording routine, adopt the sign-off line for scheduling changes, and add the decisions line to future entries. Owners are noted on the planning page, and I will check progress at the next sync.

## Billing, backlog and the weeks ahead

Picking up with billing, which was the other main thread of the week.

The Cedar Core backlog stood at 41 open tickets at the end of the sprint. It is a short list by our usual standards, and the team is pleased, though no one is taking it as permanent. Most of the open items are waiting for information from outside, and a couple are small enough to close this week. The more interesting observation was about shape rather than size: the tickets are now mostly genuine questions rather than stale leftovers, which suggests the earlier cleanup worked. The plan is to keep the list short by reading it every Monday and closing whatever has been answered.

There was a useful conversation about what to do when billing and other efforts disagree about timing. Cedar Core tends to need information at the end of a cycle, while others produce it at the start of the next. The workable answer was to agree a cut-off and stick to it, with a short written exception route for genuine emergencies. The cut-off will be written on the planning page so nobody has to guess. It is not a perfect solution, but it removes a lot of the friction we saw last quarter.

Tomas Kowalski came back with the sign-off routine for scheduling changes and ran through an example. The line reads naturally and takes seconds to add, and the team agreed to start using it straight away. Nimbus Core's habit of keeping small contemporaneous notes was suggested as a model, and a short session is booked for anyone curious about the approach.

A few more practical points:

- The workspace reorganisation had one casualty, a shared template that ended up in the wrong place. It has been found and restored.
- We went through the calendar for the coming weeks and noticed that two busy periods overlap. Nothing can be moved, so we will plan the cover in advance instead of improvising.
- The decisions line was added to the end of this diary, and the first attempt follows below. It was easier to write than expected.
- One person asked about training opportunities, and we agreed to gather requests at the next sync and see what is possible.

Looking ahead, the next few weeks are about consolidating rather than starting things. The audit has given us a list of habits to adopt, and the best use of the time is to make them routine before adding anything new. There is also the matter of preparing for the end-of-year period, when people are away and handovers matter more than usual. The handover template will get a real workout, and we should revisit it once the first few have been written.

On mood: people sound steady, if a little tired, and the quiet end of the week was welcome. A couple of colleagues said they would like more time for focused work and fewer small interruptions. That is a hard thing to promise, but we can at least avoid scheduling anything on Friday afternoons unless it is necessary.

Decisions this entry: adopt the sign-off line for scheduling changes, keep the Monday read-through of the billing list, agree a cut-off for information at the end of each cycle, and drop any Friday afternoon meetings that are not essential. Everything else carries forward.
