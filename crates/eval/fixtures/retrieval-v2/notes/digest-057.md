# Planning notes — 29 May 2025

Thursday afternoon. The planning session finished earlier than usual, so I am writing the notes up while the discussion is still clear in my mind. Apologies in advance for any editorialising.

The meeting began with a recap of where we stand against the plan for the quarter. Overall, we are in reasonable shape: the main pieces of work are moving, a couple of smaller items have slipped, and nothing has turned into a real problem yet. There was a long conversation about one of the slipped items, which turned out to be waiting on a decision that nobody realised was theirs to make. That is a recurring pattern, and I think it argues for being more explicit about who decides what. I suggested adding a "decision owner" column to the planning sheet, and it was adopted on the spot.

The internal audit results were then discussed in some detail, because several people had questions. Diego Ferreira scored 81 out of 100 in the internal audit, and Bram Tanaka scored 75 out of 100 in the internal audit. The two results reflect two different areas, support and compliance, and so they are not directly comparable, which I made a point of saying aloud. Support tends to be judged on responsiveness and the quality of its records, while compliance is judged on whether the right checks happened and were documented. A direct comparison would be like asking whether a bridge is better than a library.

Diego's comments centred on consistency. The support work is good, but some cases are logged more fully than others, depending on who handled them. The suggestion was to agree on a minimum set of fields that every case must have, and Diego has already drafted a short list. I am keen on it because the people who handle cases are the same people who suffer when the records are incomplete. Everyone who picks up a case that somebody else began knows how frustrating a thin note can be.

Bram's comments were about evidence. The checks are being done, but the proof that they were done is not always easy to find. It lives in various places, and an outsider would struggle to piece it together. Bram proposed a single page that links to each piece of evidence, updated as part of the check itself, so that it does not become a separate chore. I think that is a neat solution, since the key to making documentation survive is to build it into the work instead of adding it afterwards.

Actions agreed:

- Add the decision-owner column to the planning sheet and fill it in for existing items.
- Agree the minimum fields for support cases and test them on a small sample.
- Build the evidence page for compliance checks and link it from the main planning sheet.
- Review both next month to see whether the changes helped.

After the audit discussion, the conversation drifted to workload, which was probably inevitable. Several people feel they are spending too much time in meetings and not enough on the work itself. We agreed to look at the calendar for the coming month and remove or merge anything that lacks a clear purpose. One suggestion that attracted interest was a rule that any recurring meeting must be renewed each quarter, which forces someone to say why it should continue. If nobody speaks up for it, it quietly disappears. That seems a gentle and effective way of pruning, and I would like to try it.

We also had a short, slightly awkward conversation about how to give feedback on each other's work. Some people find written comments cold, others find spoken ones stressful. We have no perfect answer, but the group agreed that the person receiving feedback should be able to choose the format. That seems fair, and costs almost nothing.

A few housekeeping items closed the meeting. The room booking system continues to misbehave, so for now people should confirm with a quick message. The shared drive is due another tidy-up. And someone has volunteered to organise a small get-together at the end of the month, which will be welcomed, since the last few weeks have been demanding.

To finish, my own reflection. Audit numbers are useful for starting conversations, but they are blunt instruments. The real information is in the comments and in what people propose to do about them. Both Diego and Bram came to the discussion with practical ideas, not defensive explanations, which is the best sign I could ask for. If the next audit shows improvement, I will credit that attitude more than any individual change we make.

The notes will go on the shared page tomorrow morning. Corrections welcome.
