# Building a hiring pipeline without a recruiter

_Clipped from the web. Source: example.org/building-a-hiring-pipeline-without-a-recruiter._

Small companies often reach the point of needing new people before they can justify a dedicated recruiter. The work does not disappear; it lands on whoever has a spare hour, usually the person with the least time. Managers who have done this describe a few practices that keep the process humane and reasonably fast.

**Write the role down first.** Before any advert goes out, the hiring manager should be able to describe the job in a paragraph: what the person will do in their first months, who they will work with, and what success looks like. Vague roles attract vague applications. Several managers said that the paragraph also exposed disagreements inside the team that were better settled before candidates arrived.

**Keep the process short and visible.** Candidates lose interest when they hear nothing for a week. A simple shared tracker, with one line per candidate and a clear next step, prevents most of the silent delays. Where possible, the same two or three people should handle every stage, so that feedback is consistent and candidates meet familiar faces.

**Use the same questions for everyone.** Without a recruiter's training, interviews drift toward conversation and instinct. A short list of structured questions, each linked to something the role really requires, makes comparisons fairer. Interviewers record notes immediately afterwards, before discussing with colleagues, to avoid being swayed by the loudest opinion.

**Reply to everyone.** This was the habit people mentioned most warmly. A brief, honest message to unsuccessful candidates costs little and builds goodwill that outlasts the vacancy. Several managers noted that people they had declined later applied again, or recommended friends.

None of this replaces the skill of a professional recruiter, and growth eventually makes one worthwhile. But a clear role, a visible process, consistent questions and courteous replies will take a small team a long way.
