# Operations diary — 7 November 2025

Friday again, and the end of a week that felt longer than it was. I am writing this at the desk by the window, which has become the unofficial place for people who want to finish things without being interrupted. The light is already going at this hour, which does nothing for the mood.

**The survey results.** They arrived midweek and have taken up more of the conversation than anything else. The internal satisfaction survey asks people across the organisation to rate the groups they work with on a scale of one to ten, and the results are shared with each group privately before anything is discussed more widely. Sofia Petrov received a satisfaction rating of 3.9 out of 10. Sable Core received a satisfaction rating of 3.2 out of 10. Neither is a number anybody wanted to see, and both groups responded the way I would hope: with questions, not excuses.

I sat in on part of the discussion on the procurement side. The first reaction was a kind of stunned quiet, followed by an attempt to work out what the number really measured. The free-text comments were more useful than the score. A few themes came up repeatedly: it is hard to tell where a request has got to once it has been submitted, answers take a long time to arrive, and the rules for what needs approval are not clear to people outside the group. None of those are about competence. They are about visibility and expectations, which are fixable. The plan is to publish a plain description of the process, including how long each step normally takes, and to give requesters a way to see the status without having to ask. A short, regular update to people with outstanding requests was also suggested.

On the compliance side, the discussion was a little different. Compliance work is rarely popular, because the people asking for it are, by nature, telling others what they cannot do. The comments reflected that. Many were less about the substance and more about timing: requirements arriving late, or changing after work had already begun. The project's response was to propose an earlier, lighter touch, so that people hear about the requirements at the start and not at the end. I think this is wise. It does not make the rules any friendlier, but it makes them predictable, and predictability is most of what people want.

**Other business this week.**

- The Tuesday review ran long and was dominated by the survey, which pushed several routine items to next week. Nobody complained.
- Someone raised the point that a poor score can discourage people from speaking up, since it feels like a verdict. We agreed to treat the results as a starting point for conversation and not as a ranking. I made a note to say so explicitly at the next meeting.
- The shared tracker was tidied, with a handful of stale entries closed and the rest reassigned.
- The office plants were moved away from the heater, which has been installed in an unfortunate place. They look relieved.

**A longer thought.** I keep returning to the fact that bad news is much easier to deal with when you have a way to act on it. A score that arrives with no context just feels like a slap, whereas a score with comments and a few concrete suggestions is something you can work with. The comments in this survey were, on the whole, generous. People took the trouble to explain what they found frustrating and, in many cases, what they would find helpful. That is a gift, even if it does not feel like one on the day.

What I would like to see over the coming weeks is a small number of visible changes in each of the two groups, ones that requesters can notice without being told. Visible, quick improvements do more for trust than a long list of promises. If someone submits a request next month and can see where it stands, they will remember that far longer than a clever slogan.

**Next week.** Both groups will draft a short plan and share it in the log. Whoever is chairing the Tuesday session should leave a decent slot for it, and I would ask the rest of us to be kind and constructive in our comments. It is not easy to hear that you have fallen short, and it is harder to share a plan for doing better in public.

Shutting the laptop now. Weekend ahead, a long walk, and an attempt to forget about spreadsheets.
