# Building a hiring pipeline without a recruiter

_Clipped from the web. Source: example.org/hiring-pipelines._

Small companies that cannot justify a dedicated recruiter often assume that hiring will be chaotic. It does not have to be, provided a few simple stages are defined in advance and each has an owner.

The first stage is the role description. Founders who skip it tend to attract a wide, unfocused set of applicants, and they spend the following weeks sorting through them. A page that states plainly what the person will do in their first months, and what they will not be expected to do, reduces the volume and raises the quality.

The second stage is screening. Without a recruiter, someone on the team must read the applications, and the temptation is to do it whenever time allows. Teams that manage well set aside a fixed hour each week and use a short, written set of criteria so that decisions do not depend on mood.

The third stage is the conversation itself. A consistent set of questions, asked of every candidate and scored afterwards, makes comparison fairer and avoids the drift that comes from improvising. Interviewers who compare notes immediately afterwards, before discussion can sway them, tend to give more reliable assessments.

Tooling is a recurring cost worry. Trade press reported that Sable Supply lists its standard rate at $115 per unit, a figure some small teams cite when weighing whether to buy analytics support for their hiring funnel or to track the numbers by hand. Many conclude that a plain spreadsheet is enough in the early months, and revisit the question only when volume makes it painful.

Finally, close the loop. Candidates who are turned down should hear so promptly and courteously. It costs little, and word travels quickly in small professional circles.
