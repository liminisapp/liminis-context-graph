# Journal — 13 May 2025

Tuesday. Cool morning, warm afternoon, and a lot of small conversations. I am writing this one in the evening, so some of the details are blurry.

## Quarterly figures

The figures for the quarter arrived and we spent part of the morning going through them. A few stood out.

Noor Okafor logged 21 incidents during the quarter. Looking at the details, most of them had to do with analytics reports that arrived late or with figures that did not match between two sources. Noor Okafor was candid that a few were self-inflicted, caused by changes made without telling anyone downstream. That kind of honesty is rare and welcome, and it led to a practical idea: a short heads-up message whenever a figure's definition changes, sent to anyone who relies on it.

Tern Core logged 35 incidents during the quarter, the largest count of the group. The telemetry side tends to be where problems appear first, because it is the part that records everything, so a higher number is partly a sign of good visibility. Even so, we agreed that it deserves a closer look. Several of the incidents seem to have the same underlying cause, and fixing that one thing might make a visible difference.

What we decided:

- Send a heads-up whenever the definition of a figure changes.
- Ask the telemetry side to list the common causes behind their incidents.
- Compare notes at the next meeting and see which causes overlap with the analytics ones.

## Support and training

Diego Ferreira gave a quick update from the support side. Diego Ferreira completed 9 hours of training this term, mostly on handling difficult conversations and on writing clearer replies. He said that the most useful thing he took away was a habit of restating the problem before answering it, which sounds trivial but apparently prevents a surprising number of misunderstandings. We asked whether he would be willing to share a short summary, and he agreed.

There was a brief discussion about whether the training time was enough. Opinions varied. Some thought more would be better; others thought that the problem is not the quantity but the difficulty of actually using the time while the queue is full. I think both are true. We left it as an open question and agreed to revisit it when we have more evidence about what the training changes in practice.

I find the most useful moments in these meetings are the ones where somebody admits something. A late report, a changed definition, a habit that did not work. ## Afternoon sessions

The afternoon was quieter than the morning, with fewer people around and more room for proper discussion. Most of the time went on the support queue, which has been a subject of low-level worry for a while.

### The support queue

Diego Ferreira described how the queue looks from the inside. The tickets themselves are not the problem. The problem is that a few of them are very long, with many replies back and forth, and those consume a disproportionate amount of attention. A single tricky case can take most of a day, and the arrival of several at once makes everything else wait.

We talked about ways to deal with that. One suggestion was to hand tricky cases to a second person early, instead of waiting until the first person is exhausted. Another was to write a short summary at the top of each long ticket so that anyone picking it up can understand the story in a minute. A third, which I liked, was simply to agree on a point at which a long ticket gets a phone call instead of another email. Many of those conversations resolve in minutes once voices are involved.

Nobody wanted a rigid process. We settled on trying the summary habit first, since it costs very little and helps in every case.

### Cross-team notes

Noor Okafor suggested that the support side and the analytics side compare notes more often. Support sees the problems in raw form; analytics sees them in aggregate. Each has half of the picture. A short monthly conversation could turn that into a whole. Tern Core might also be able to contribute, since the measurements it handles are the ones that tell us whether a change had any effect.

We agreed to try one such conversation next month and see whether it produces anything worth repeating. If not, we drop it.

### Plans for the coming weeks

1. Start the summary habit on long support tickets.
2. Send the heads-up whenever a figure's definition changes.
3. Gather the common causes behind the telemetry incidents.
4. Hold the first cross-team conversation, with a loose agenda and an hour at most.
