# Review notes — 10 April 2025

Review day. These are my notes from the morning session, tidied a little at lunch so they make sense to anyone who was not there. I have tried to keep opinions separate from what was actually said.

The meeting opened with a reminder of why we do these reviews at all: not to grade anyone, but to make sure the three groups stay roughly aware of each other's work. Helix Core covers analytics, Lumen Core covers security, and Orbit Core covers inventory, and in practice their paths cross more often than the org chart suggests. Analytics needs data that inventory owns, and security has opinions about who can see either. A good review surfaces those crossings before they turn into arguments.

Helix Core went first. The group described a period of steady, unglamorous progress: cleaning up the way data is collected, retiring a few dashboards that nobody opened, and rewriting a report that had grown unreadable. The retired dashboards caused some debate, as one person was sure somebody still used them. We settled it the usual way, by switching them off for a trial period and waiting to see who complained. If nobody does, they stay off.

Orbit Core followed with a candid account of stock counting. The headline was that the process works but depends too heavily on a few experienced people, and that when those people are away, accuracy slips. The suggested remedy was boring and probably right: write down the counting routine, run it with someone new beside the experienced person, and swap roles after a couple of rounds. Nobody objected, and a few people looked relieved that the answer was not a new tool.

Things to follow up from this section:

- Decide how long the trial for the switched-off dashboards should run.
- Ask the inventory group for a first draft of the counting routine.
- Find out whether the security group has views on who may see the analytics reports.
- Book the next review so that it does not clash with the quarter-end rush.

I should say a word about the room. It was too warm, the windows would not open, and by the second half people were visibly fading. Next time we will either hold the session earlier or find a cooler room. I have added that to the list of small irritations that, collectively, make a difference to how well a meeting goes.

The break was short, and the conversation in the corridor was probably more useful than half of what was said inside. Two people from different groups discovered they had been solving the same small problem separately for weeks. That alone justified the morning, as far as I am concerned. Security's turn came after the break.

## Security's turn and the middle of the day

After the break it was Lumen Core's slot. Their update was the most structured of the three, perhaps unsurprisingly. They had prepared a short list of what had gone well, what had not, and what they planned next, and they stuck to it. That discipline made the discussion easier, because we could argue about specific items rather than impressions.

The main theme was access. The group is tightening how permissions are granted and, just as importantly, how they are removed when someone changes role. Removal is the part everybody forgets. There was a candid admission that a few old permissions had lingered longer than they should, and a plan to review them regularly instead of waiting for someone to notice. Helix Core asked, reasonably, whether the review would break any reports that rely on broad access. The answer was "possibly", which led to a sensible agreement: security will give analytics advance warning before any change that might affect them, and analytics will tell security which reports they consider essential.

A second item was how incidents are communicated. Security would like a single place where updates are posted, instead of threads scattered across several channels. Orbit Core was keen, as the inventory team had missed a message on a previous occasion and only found out afterwards. We decided to try one shared page, kept deliberately plain, and to review after a month whether anyone actually uses it. I have a private suspicion that the habit will be harder to establish than the page.

Observations from the discussion:

- The three groups were more aligned than I expected. Disagreements were about timing, not direction.
- People were much more willing to admit mistakes when the person before them had gone first. Honesty is contagious.
- The strongest comments came from people who had been silent for the first hour. It is worth asking for them directly.
- Nobody wanted to talk about tooling, which is a good sign.

Lunch was sandwiches, and there was an argument about whether the vegetarian option had been eaten by someone who should not have taken it. It remains unresolved. I made a mental note not to get involved.

During the quiet moments I tried to write down what the reviews are for, in a sentence or two, because I think we sometimes lose sight of it. My attempt: a review is a chance to say out loud what we think we are doing, so that others can tell us if it does not match what they see. If that is the whole purpose, then the format matters less than the honesty. I will see whether the others agree when I share these notes.

The afternoon was set aside for planning, and I will cover it in the next part.

## Afternoon planning

The afternoon turned into planning, as these things do. With the three updates still fresh, we tried to turn the morning's observations into a short list of commitments for the next stretch. The rule was that nothing went on the list unless someone's name, or at least a group's name, sat next to it.

Helix Core committed to finishing the report rewrite and to documenting where each remaining dashboard gets its data. The second item sounds tedious and is, but it answers a question that has been asked at least three times in the past month, always with a slightly different answer. They also offered to prepare a demonstration of the new reports for anyone interested, which we agreed would be most useful if it came with time for questions and not just a tour.

Orbit Core took the counting routine as its main action, along with a request: could somebody from outside the group sit in on one of the counts and note anything that seems odd to a fresh pair of eyes? The point is to find the steps that are obvious to insiders and baffling to everyone else. There were volunteers within seconds, which tells you something about how interesting people find other people's work.

Lumen Core took the permissions review and the shared incident page. They also raised the possibility of a short, informal session on security basics for the other groups. The idea was received warmly but with a note of caution: a session that people attend out of obligation is worse than none. The group will think about how to make it practical, perhaps built around real examples from the last few months, and will offer it as optional.

A few agreements about how we work together:

- Updates between groups go through the shared page, not through private messages.
- Anything that might affect another group gets a heads-up before it happens, not after.
- Reviews will continue on the same rhythm, but each will start with a short look back at the previous list of commitments.
- If a commitment cannot be met, say so early. Nobody will mind as much as they fear.

We also spent some time on the dull but important question of how to track all this. Several people proposed new tools, and several others groaned. In the end we kept to what already exists: a simple shared list, reviewed at the start of each meeting. The consensus was that the best tool is the one people already look at.

I noticed that by this point the room had cooled and everyone seemed more awake. Either the windows finally opened or the conversation had become more interesting, and I cannot be sure which. Either way, the energy in the last hour was better than the first.

One more section to go, covering the numbers and the wrap-up.

## Numbers and wrap-up

Last part of the review notes: the figures that were read out near the end, and how we closed.

On training, Orbit Core completed 10 hours of training this term. It is a modest figure, and the group was open about it: the stock counts took priority and the courses slipped. They have promised to block time earlier next term, and I suggested tying it to the quiet weeks after the quarter-end count, which is when the pressure lets up. Nobody wants to turn training into a box-ticking exercise, but a term without any is worse than a term with too little.

The internal audit results were shared next. Lumen Core scored 73 out of 100 in the internal audit. The group took it calmly. The comments that came with it were mostly about records: some approvals were granted correctly but not written down in a way an outsider could follow. That fits what we heard this morning about lingering permissions, and it feeds directly into the review they have already planned. I think it is helpful that the audit and the plan point the same way, as it makes the case for the work almost without further argument.

Finally, the Helix Core demo. 84 people attended the Helix Core demo, which was more than anyone predicted and more than the room could comfortably seat for the whole session. The feedback was positive, with questions concentrated on how to request a custom report and how long such a request takes. The group will write that up as a short guide. A couple of attendees noticed things that looked different from what they were used to and asked for a changelog, which seems entirely reasonable.

To close, we went round the table for one sentence each on what we would change about these reviews. The answers were refreshingly practical:

- Hold them in a cooler room.
- Circulate the figures a day beforehand so people can read them.
- Keep each update to a fixed length.
- Leave a gap at the end for things that did not fit anywhere.

All four were adopted without a vote. I will draft the next agenda to include them, and send it a couple of days ahead.

A final observation. This is the first review in a while where I left feeling that the three groups understand each other a little better than before. It is hard to measure, and I would not put it in a report, but it is what the exercise is for. If it carries into the next few weeks, the rest of the list will take care of itself. The notes are in the shared space; corrections welcome, especially from anyone who remembers the vegetarian sandwich saga differently.
