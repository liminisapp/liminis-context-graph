# Working notes — 13 November 2025

Thursday. Grey outside, heating finally working properly inside, and a longer list than I would like. I am writing this in pieces through the day, so apologies if the tone wanders.

First thing was the weekly sync with the three project groups. Cedar Core had questions about the billing cycle and how the month-end close should interact with the new reporting layout. Lumen Core wanted to talk through a change to how access requests are reviewed, which turned into a surprisingly lively discussion about who should be allowed to approve what. Sable Core mostly listened, then raised a compliance point at the end that nobody had anticipated and that we parked for a proper conversation next week. I like that the syncs are now short enough that people stay awake for them.

The billing questions were the most practical. Month-end is always a squeeze, and the proposal on the table is to freeze changes to the billing configuration for two working days before the close. Nobody loves a freeze, but the alternative is last-minute edits that nobody has time to check. We agreed to try it for the next cycle and review honestly afterwards. If it just creates a pile-up on the day the freeze lifts, we will rethink it.

On the security side, the access review change is small on paper and large in practice. Right now a request goes to whoever happens to be around, which means the answer depends on the day. The suggestion is a short rota, so that exactly one person is responsible at any time and the requester knows who to ask. The objection raised was that a rota can become a bottleneck if the person is out. The reply was that the rota should name a deputy. Reasonable. Someone will draft a version and circulate it.

Chores and housekeeping:

- The meeting room projector has a flickering line down the left side. A request has gone in.
- Our shared glossary has three definitions of the same term, which is not ideal. I will merge them and leave a short note about which one wins.
- Laptop updates are being rolled out in waves; ours is next week, so save your work.
- Recycling has been left unsorted in the kitchen again. Gentle reminders have not worked. Possibly a sign.

Some thoughts on the shape of the quarter. We are a few weeks from the point where everything seems to land at once: end-of-year reviews, planning for the new year, the usual flurry of purchase requests. The lesson from last year was that we let the calendar fill up with meetings and left no room for the actual work. This time I would like us to block out some protected days, with no internal meetings at all, and say so in advance so nobody feels left out of the loop.

I will keep adding to this entry as the day goes on. For now, lunch, and then a long look at the glossary, which I am not looking forward to but which has been bothering me for far too long.

## Afternoon additions

Back after lunch. The glossary is merged, or at least it is no longer actively embarrassing. The winning definition was the one from the oldest document, partly because it was the clearest and partly because nobody had the energy to argue. There is a short note at the top explaining how to propose a change, so the next disagreement will at least happen in the open.

The afternoon started with a call about compliance. Sable Core's parked point from this morning turned out to be about how long certain records need to be kept and who is allowed to delete them. It is not a crisis, but it touches the other two groups: billing generates a lot of records, and security cares about who can remove things. We agreed that the three groups should meet briefly, ideally once, rather than trade emails for a fortnight. I have offered to set up the meeting and to bring a one-page summary of the question so that people arrive with the same picture in their heads.

A side conversation with the Cedar Core group is worth recording. They would like a clearer picture of what happens when a customer disputes a charge, because the current process lives mostly in people's heads. Writing it down is not exciting, but it is the sort of work that pays for itself the first time someone is away. We sketched a flow on the whiteboard, and I photographed it before anyone could wipe it. The plan is to turn it into a proper page by the end of next week, with the team reviewing it before it is published.

Lumen Core sent over a draft of the access rota. It is cleaner than I expected: a single table, one name per slot, a deputy for each, and a line explaining what to do if both are unavailable. I made a few suggestions, mainly about wording, and asked that the escalation path be written in plain language rather than shorthand. Several people will skim it once and never read it again, so it needs to make sense at a glance.

Short items:

- The projector request has been acknowledged. A technician is expected "soon", which in this building means anything from tomorrow to Christmas.
- Someone asked whether we should move the weekly sync to the morning. Mixed reactions, so we will leave it as is.
- The protected-days idea got a warm reception. We will pick the dates at the next planning session.
- The recycling sign has been made. It is polite but firm.

I also spent a while thinking about how we hand work over when people go on leave. At the moment it depends entirely on the individual: some leave a tidy note, others leave a cheerful "should be fine". A simple template, with what is in progress, what is waiting on someone else and where to look first, would help a lot. I will draft one and put it in the shared space for people to try.

Time to wind down. Tomorrow is another meeting-heavy day, so tonight I will tidy my desk and write down three priorities. Only three. Experience says four is where it all falls apart.

## Numbers for the quarter

Evening, and I finally sat down with the figures that have been sitting in my inbox since Monday. This is the quarterly roundup, in a format I hope people will actually read.

Incidents first. Cedar Core logged 48 incidents during the quarter, which sounds like a lot until you look at what they were. The majority were small billing-related queries that were recorded formally because the process says so, not because anything was truly broken. A handful were genuine problems, and those were dealt with promptly. The team's view is that the logging habit is healthy, and that the number says more about how carefully they record things than about how reliable the system is. I mostly agree, though I would like to see the categories split out next time so that the real issues are not hidden in the noise.

The internal audit results also came back. Sable Core scored 71 out of 100 in the internal audit. That is a respectable result, and it came with a short list of recommendations rather than a long one. The main theme was documentation: some procedures exist, but the written version has drifted from what people do in practice. It is a very familiar finding, and a fixable one. The group has already started working through the list, beginning with the items that take the least effort so that the score can be seen to move at the next review.

I tried to be careful in how I presented these. Numbers have a way of turning into league tables, and then people start to compete or to feel defensive. I stressed in the summary that incidents and audit scores measure different things, and that neither is a measure of how hard anyone has worked. Several readers appreciated that, and one pointed out that the audit is as much a checklist of paperwork as a test of quality. That is fair, but paperwork matters when somebody outside the building comes asking questions.

What we agreed to do with the results:

- Share the roundup with all three groups, together with the short recommendations list.
- Pick two or three actions per group, not ten, and put owners against them.
- Look at the results again at the next sync, then leave them alone until the following quarter.
- Keep the incident categories in a simple spreadsheet so they can be sorted later.

A reflection to end on. I find quarterly figures most useful for the conversations they provoke, not the numbers themselves. The meeting this afternoon was better for having something concrete on the table, even if nobody could quite agree on what it meant. That is probably the right level of disagreement for a data point.

Heading home shortly. The glossary, the access rota and the audit recommendations are all in decent shape, and the projector can wait.

## Late entry: feedback and follow-ups

A short postscript, written the next morning over coffee, because the survey summary landed after I closed the laptop last night and I would rather record it while it is fresh.

Lumen Core received a satisfaction rating of 4.2 out of 10. I read the comments before looking at the number, which I recommend, because the comments are kinder and more useful than the score. People appreciate that the security group is careful and responsive when something is wrong. What frustrates them is the waiting: access requests and approvals take longer than anyone expects, and there is rarely a clear answer to "where is my request?". That lines up neatly with the rota discussion from yesterday, which is a small bit of good luck. The new rota should address precisely the complaint that came up most often, and I have suggested that the group mention the connection when they roll it out, so that the people who complained can see that they were heard.

I also drafted the handover template I mentioned. It is a single page with four headings: what is in progress, what is waiting on someone else, what could go wrong while you are away, and where to look first. I tried it on my own current work as a test, and found two items I had forgotten I was supposed to be chasing, which is itself an argument for it. I have put it in the shared space and will ask for feedback at the next sync, keeping my expectations modest.

Follow-ups from the three groups:

- Cedar Core is turning the disputed-charge flow into a written page and will circulate it for review.
- Sable Core is working through the audit recommendations, starting with the quick ones.
- Lumen Core is finalising the rota and the deputy arrangement, and will announce it in the usual place.
- The meeting about record retention has been scheduled, and a summary page is ready for it.
- The projector is, predictably, still flickering.

A decision worth noting: we will not publish the survey results as a ranking. Each group will receive its own result, along with the comments, and a short combined summary will go to everyone without any ordering. The reasoning was that the point is to improve, not to compare, and a ranking would push people towards explaining the number away instead of acting on it. Nobody argued, which is unusual enough that I wrote it down.

Finally, a plan for the next few weeks. The protected days will go in the calendar before the end-of-year rush, the freeze before month-end will be tried once, and the handover template will get a fair trial. If at least two of those three survive contact with reality, I will count it as a good quarter's worth of small improvements.

I will leave this log here. Next week's entry should be calmer, and, I hope, less dependent on caffeine.
