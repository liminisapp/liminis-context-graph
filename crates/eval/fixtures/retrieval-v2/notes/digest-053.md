# Review notes — 4 July 2025

Review day on a hot Friday, with the windows propped open and a fan pointed at whoever looked most flustered. I volunteered to take the notes, so here they are, lightly edited for sense.

## How the review went

The format was simple: each group spent a few minutes on what went well, what did not, and what it wants to change. We kept it informal, with no slides, since the last round of slides had been so dense that nobody remembered them by teatime.

Support was up first. Juniper Core, the project responsible for support, talked about how requests have been arriving in clumps instead of a steady trickle. Mondays and the days after any release are the heaviest, and the team has tried to plan around that without much luck. The most useful suggestion from the room was to ask the release group to post a short heads-up before changes go out, which gives support a chance to prepare answers. The team also wants a shared set of answers to the most common questions, kept in one place and updated whenever the real answer changes.

Next, scheduling. Tomas Kowalski, who covers scheduling, described the headache of fitting everyone's calendars together around the holiday season. The problem is not the number of requests but their timing: everybody wants the same weeks. His proposal is to ask for holiday preferences earlier and to use a simple first-come rule only as a tie-breaker, after a fairness check. People liked the idea in principle and asked for the rule to be written down so that it does not become folklore.

### What people appreciated

- Quick replies on the shared channel, especially when someone stepped in outside their usual area.
- The tidy-up of the old documents, which has made searching far less painful.
- The decision to cut the Tuesday meeting short and finish with a concrete action every time.

### What people want changed

- Fewer last-minute requests on Friday afternoons.
- A clearer path for escalating something that is stuck.
- Some way to see who is covering what when colleagues are away.

### Small decisions

We will draft a one-page escalation guide that says who to ask, in what order, and how long to wait before asking the next person. A volunteer from each group will review it. We will also test the "heads-up before release" habit for a month and see whether support's workload feels more predictable.

### Odds and ends

The fan is staying until the heat breaks. The cold drinks fridge was cleaned out, and it is a relief to report that nothing alarming was found. A reminder that the shared notes folder should have clear titles, so that what we write today can be found in the autumn.

The rest of these notes cover the plans for the next few months and a couple of announcements.

## Plans and announcements

Second half of the review notes, covering what comes next and a few things people wanted on the record.

First, the team gathering. The Juniper Core offsite will take place in Turin. There was a cheerful reaction, followed quickly by practical questions about travel, timing and who looks after the shared tasks while people are away. The support group promised to share a proposed outline for the days, with a mix of planning and unstructured time, because the last offsite showed that the hallway conversations were as valuable as the agenda. The cover arrangement for the support queue is the item most in need of care, and it will be worked out well in advance so nobody comes back to chaos.

Tomas Kowalski ran a demo of the new scheduling approach for the wider group, and 31 people attended the Tomas Kowalski demo. He walked through a sample month, showing how conflicts are flagged and how swaps get requested. Questions afterwards were mostly about edge cases, such as part-time colleagues and people who switch days. He took notes and will come back with answers. Several people said the visual layout made the whole thing far easier to follow than the earlier spreadsheet, which tended to look like a wall of cells.

### Decisions

- The escalation guide goes into the shared folder next week, reviewed by one volunteer per group.
- Holiday preferences get collected earlier this year, with a clear deadline and a reminder.
- Support's shared answers page will have a named editor, so it is not everyone's job and therefore nobody's.
- The release heads-up trial begins immediately.

### Open questions

How do we handle swaps requested at very short notice? The scheduling approach covers normal cases well, but an emergency swap is another matter. We parked it with the understanding that whoever has the most experience of such swaps will write up how they were handled in practice, which may inform a rule.

Is the offsite the right moment to revisit the cover arrangements for the whole team? Several people thought yes. It may make sense to use part of one day to walk through the arrangements together and spot gaps. Nobody has to decide now.

### Last notes

A thank-you to everyone who helped clear the old documents; the difference is striking. The fan will be retired once the weather turns. A note on the whiteboard asks people not to erase the planning sketch until the photograph has been taken, and someone should photograph it.

That is the end of these notes. I will tidy the formatting tomorrow and add them to the archive with the rest of the month's reviews.
