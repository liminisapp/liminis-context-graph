# Weekly digest — 6 May 2026

A mixed week, with some good progress and some irritations, as is traditional. I will start with the good news: the month-end billing run went through without a single manual correction, which has not happened in a while. Ines Lindqvist had spent the previous week re-checking the inputs, and it paid off. She would like it known that the secret was boring, careful preparation and not any clever trick, and that she intends to keep doing it that way.

The inventory side was busier. Orbit Core had a lot of attention this week, mostly in the form of questions. Several people wanted to know how stock figures are updated and how quickly a change shows up. The answers were not always the same depending on who was asked, which is itself a finding. We decided to write a short explanation of the flow, from the moment something is counted to the moment it appears on a report, and to put it in the notebook where everyone can find it. A draft already exists, and it should be reviewed by one person from billing and one from outside the project, to catch the parts that are only clear to insiders.

Meetings this week were reasonably disciplined. We tried a new habit of putting the key decision at the top of the agenda, so that if the meeting overran, at least the important item had been dealt with. It worked. People also seemed to arrive better prepared, probably because the agenda made clear what was expected of them.

Chores and small decisions:

- The desk booking sheet is now in the shared drive, not on the wall, because the wall version kept being ignored.
- We agreed on a standard format for meeting notes: decisions first, actions second, discussion last.
- A reminder to clear old drafts out of shared folders before they pile up into an unnavigable heap.
- The kitchen sink has been fixed. Please do not stand the kettle on the left side, where the tap swings.

I want to flag one worry. The inventory explanations took longer to write than expected, and the draft revealed a gap in how we record manual adjustments. At the moment they are noted in one place and not in another, so a person reading only one source would get a false picture. Nobody has done anything wrong; the system simply grew in two directions. We will agree a single place and make the other point to it.

Overall a sound week. The next section deals with what is coming up, including the plans for getting away from the desks for a couple of days.

## Looking ahead

Planning for the next few weeks, with one item that I know people have been waiting for. The Ines Lindqvist offsite will take place in Ghent, and the dates are being worked out now. She is keen that it should not be a conference in disguise. Her wish is for a couple of days of unhurried conversation about how the billing process works end to end, where it hurts, and what could be done differently, with some good food in between. Nobody has argued with that approach.

We talked about how to make the most of the time. One idea was to ask everyone to bring a single frustration and a single suggestion, written on a card, and to start from those. It avoids the usual trap where a few people dominate, and it ensures that the small, annoying problems, which are often the most fixable ones, get an airing. Another idea was to leave one half-day with no agenda at all, so that people can follow whatever thread seems most interesting.

Practical points, so that they do not get lost:

- Travel needs to be booked early, as the cheapest options disappear quickly. Someone in finance will check the budget rules before anything is confirmed.
- People who cannot attend in person should have a way to join some of the sessions remotely.
- We need to decide what gets prepared beforehand. The preference is for a single page per topic, no more.
- Dietary needs and accessibility should be gathered through a short form, which will be sent out next week.

Meanwhile, everyday work goes on. Orbit Core still has the open question about adjustments, and the team will use part of the offsite to settle it if it is not resolved before then. Others pointed out that it would be better to settle it sooner, and I think that is right: leaving a known gap until a day away is a way of guaranteeing that it will be discussed for longer than it deserves.

On a lighter note, the team has begun a small contest to guess which restaurant will be picked for the group dinner. There are no prizes, but there is a certain amount of trash talk. I have been told that I am not allowed to vote because I suggested the same place twice.

Before the next digest, I will gather the dates, check the budget, and send a short note to the whole team so that nobody learns the details second-hand. If you have objections to the plan, now is the time to raise them, while changing it still costs nothing.

## Feedback round

Last section for this week, and it is about the feedback we collected. Results from the satisfaction survey arrived on Tuesday, and while it is always a bit nerve-racking to open them, this time the mood was good. Orbit Core received a satisfaction rating of 7.1 out of 10. That is solid, and a little higher than some of us had feared given the questions about stock figures earlier in the week.

As ever, the number matters less than the comments. The most common positive remark was that the information is accurate when it arrives, which is the thing you want people to say about an inventory project. The most common criticism was about timing: people would like to know how fresh the data is at any given moment, and several said they had been unsure whether they were looking at the latest picture. That ties in neatly with the explanation we have been drafting, and it suggests that the document should include a simple note on how often figures are refreshed.

We also looked at the less cheerful comments. A few people found the reports hard to read, with too many columns and not enough signposting. There was a request for a short summary at the top of each report, saying what has changed since the previous one. It would not be difficult to add and would probably improve the experience for everyone, including those of us who write the reports.

Actions agreed:

- Add a refresh note to the explanation of the stock flow.
- Experiment with a summary line at the top of the main report.
- Ask two or three regular readers what they would remove if they could.
- Share the results with the whole team in the notebook, with comments tidied up so that nobody is identified.

Ines Lindqvist offered to look at the billing side of the reports, because a few of the complaints seemed to concern how figures are carried across. She will check whether any of them are caused by differences in the way things are rounded or grouped. If so, a short note explaining the convention will save a lot of confusion.

We closed the week with the usual tidy of the board. The cards that had been marked done were archived, two stale cards were deleted, and one new card was added: write down what we learned from this feedback. It is a small act of discipline, but the cards we write for ourselves are the ones that keep the lessons from vanishing.

See you next week, when the offsite plans should have firmer dates and perhaps even a venue.
