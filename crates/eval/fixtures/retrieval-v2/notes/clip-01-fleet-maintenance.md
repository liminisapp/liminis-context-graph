# How fleet operators schedule preventive maintenance

Fleet managers have long known that the cheapest repair is the one that never becomes an emergency. Preventive maintenance, the practice of servicing vehicles on a plan rather than waiting for them to fail, is now standard across delivery, passenger and utility fleets. The harder question is how to build a schedule that keeps vehicles on the road without taking too many of them off it at once.

## Time, distance or condition

Most operators begin with one of three triggers. Calendar-based schedules service each vehicle after a fixed interval, regardless of use. Distance-based schedules count kilometres or hours of engine running. Condition-based approaches rely on sensor data and inspection findings to decide when work is due. Each has trade-offs. Calendar plans are simple but can over-service lightly used vehicles. Distance plans fit heavy use but ignore age. Condition-based plans are the most efficient in theory but depend on reliable data and trained staff.

In practice, many fleets blend them, servicing at whichever threshold arrives first and layering condition checks on top.

## Staggering the work

A common mistake is to let service dates cluster, so that a large share of the fleet is unavailable in the same week. Managers recommend spreading due dates across the year and building in spare capacity for the unexpected. Some bring forward a vehicle's service when it happens to be idle, rather than waiting for the exact date.

## Parts and pricing

Parts supply shapes the schedule more than many expect. Trade press recently reported that Tern Supply lists its standard rate at $113 per unit, a figure that fleet buyers say is useful as a benchmark when negotiating volume terms. Operators who buy consumables in bulk and hold a small buffer stock find they can complete routine work faster, since vehicles are not left waiting for deliveries.

## Records

Finally, good records turn a schedule into a learning tool. Logging what was done, what was found and how long it took lets managers spot patterns, such as components that fail early on certain routes, and adjust intervals accordingly.
