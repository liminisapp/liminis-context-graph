# Planning notes — 11 September 2025

Thursday planning session, written up the same evening so that nothing is lost. The meeting ran a little long, partly because of a tangent about naming conventions that I will mention below, and partly because there was genuinely a lot to cover. These notes are organised by theme rather than in the order we talked, since the conversation wandered.

## Where things stand

Let me start with the picture as I understand it. Billing is in reasonable shape. The routine that matches payments to invoices is running without much attention, the exception queue is short, and the people who rely on it have stopped asking whether it is working. Onboarding, which Aiko Osei looks after, is the area under the most pressure, with new people arriving faster than the supporting material can keep up. Everyone agreed that the two are linked: the better the onboarding, the fewer billing questions come from people who did not know the process.

## Demo reflections

The recent demo was well received. 84 people attended the Cedar Core demo, which is the largest turnout we have had for anything of this kind, and the room (plus the overflow link) was properly full. The format worked: a short explanation of what the billing project does, a live walkthrough of a realistic case, and then questions. The questions were the most valuable part. People asked about edge cases we had not considered, and a few of them revealed misunderstandings that we can now address in the documentation.

Some themes from the feedback, as recorded:

- People wanted clearer explanations of why certain fields are required.
- Several asked for a way to see the status of a submission without having to ask someone.
- A few suggested a shorter version for people who only need the basics.
- One person, memorably, asked whether the system could be less polite when it rejects something. The answer was that it could be more specific, which is not quite the same thing.

The team will turn these into tickets over the coming days. Not everything will be done soon, but it is better to have the requests written down in one place than to rely on memory.

## Backlog

The backlog discussion was more sobering. The Aiko Osei backlog stood at 163 open tickets at the end of the sprint. Nobody was surprised, but seeing it written down concentrated minds. A large share of the tickets are small, and some are probably out of date. Others are genuinely waiting for effort that the team does not currently have. The decision was to run a grooming session next week, with the aim of closing or merging whatever can be, and of sorting the rest into a rough order.

It is important not to treat the figure as a performance metric. The people working through the queue have been doing so steadily, and the volume reflects how many new people the team has been supporting, not how well it is doing. If anything, the figure suggests that onboarding is popular and that the demand for help is real. What it does tell us is that we need a better way of handling repeated questions, so that the same issue is not raised afresh by every new person.

## Ideas for easing the load

A few suggestions came up, none yet agreed.

1. Turn the most common questions into short written answers, and link to them from the welcome pages.
2. Ask a few experienced people to act as buddies, so that new starters have somewhere to go before they raise a ticket.
3. Use the demo recording as part of the induction, so the live demo does not have to be repeated.
4. Set aside a fixed hour each week for open questions, so that small issues are dealt with in one go.

The discussion on the fourth point was lively. Some felt that it would encourage people to wait for the hour instead of asking when they are stuck; others thought it would reduce interruptions. We will try it and see.

## Housekeeping and naming

The tangent about naming conventions deserves a brief record. A few people have noticed that files and tickets are named inconsistently, which makes searching harder than it should be. The proposal is a light convention: start with the area, then a short description, and avoid abbreviations that only the author understands. There was no appetite for enforcing it, and the consensus was to adopt it going forward without renaming everything that already exists.

Other bits:

- Room bookings for next week's sessions are confirmed.
- The welcome emails have been updated with correct links.
- The shared drive will get a tidy before the end of the month, with notice before anything is moved.

## Closing thoughts

A fair summary of the session: the team is busy, a little stretched, but in good spirits. The demo turnout was encouraging, and the backlog is a problem we understand rather than one we are surprised by. The next steps are clear enough: grooming, ticketing the demo feedback, and getting the buddy arrangement going. If you have an opinion on any of that, the planning page is where to put it.
