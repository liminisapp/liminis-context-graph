# Team log — 24 September 2025

Wednesday again, and the log is overdue. Pulling together what happened since the last entry, in roughly the order it happened.

Monday was dominated by the support queue. A backlog of older tickets had been quietly growing, and the person covering support finally ran out of patience with it. Diego Ferreira spent most of the morning going through the oldest items, closing the ones that had been solved weeks ago without anyone updating the ticket and writing short replies on the rest. It was not glamorous work but the queue is visibly shorter, and the tone of incoming messages has softened now that people are getting answers faster. We agreed this kind of sweep should happen on a schedule rather than whenever somebody snaps.

On Tuesday there was the demo. The format was a walk through the support tooling: how a ticket arrives, how it gets triaged, and how a reply goes out. In total, 49 people attended the Diego Ferreira demo, which was more than the room could comfortably hold, so some stood at the back. Questions were practical. Several people wanted to know how to flag a ticket as urgent without going through the whole form, and a couple asked whether customers can see the status of what they have submitted. The honest answer to the second was "not yet", and that went on the list of things to look at.

A few notes on what worked and what did not:

- Starting with a real, anonymised example kept people engaged far better than a diagram would have.
- The microphone cut out twice. Someone will check the batteries before the next one.
- People appreciated that we showed an awkward case where the tooling does not behave nicely. Several said it made the rest more believable.
- We ran about ten minutes long because the questions were good, which seemed a fair trade.

The other main thread of the week was housekeeping around onboarding. Aiko Osei has been rewriting the first-week guide for newcomers, and a draft went out for comment on Tuesday afternoon. The old guide was a long, linear document, and the new one is organised by what you are trying to do: get access, find the right channel, ask a question, report a problem. Early feedback is positive, though two people pointed out that the access section assumes you already know who to ask. That is exactly the sort of gap a fresh pair of eyes catches, so we have asked a newer colleague to read it cold and mark every place they got stuck.

Smaller items from the week:

1. The shared calendar was cleaned up, with a handful of dead recurring meetings removed.
2. A reminder went around that expense receipts should be attached when the claim is submitted, not afterwards.
3. The weekly summary was shortened. Decisions and requests only; everything else moves to the channel.
4. The meeting room projector has been replaced. It works. Please be kind to it.

We also had a conversation about how to handle requests that arrive in the wrong place. A message sent to the wrong person or wrong channel often stalls because nobody wants to be rude and redirect it. The agreement was that redirecting is polite, not rude, as long as you say where it should go and offer to move it yourself. A small habit, but a lot of friction hides there.

Looking ahead, the rest of the week is mostly about finishing the onboarding guide and preparing for a second demo. Notes on that in the next section.

## Thursday and the second demo

Picking up the thread. The second demo happened later in the week, and since it followed so closely on the first it is worth recording what we learned from the pairing.

This one was about onboarding, and 34 people attended the Aiko Osei demo. It was a smaller room by choice, since the audience was mostly people who either recently joined or are about to start mentoring someone who has. Aiko walked through the new first-week guide section by section, pausing wherever the draft had changed from the old version. The most useful moment came when a newcomer in the audience said, out loud, that a particular step had confused them, and the group worked out a clearer wording on the spot. That sort of live repair is much better than a round of written comments that nobody reads.

Feedback, roughly grouped:

- Positive: the task-based layout, the short length of each section, and the decision to put the contact list on the very first page.
- Neutral: some people felt the screenshots will go stale quickly and wondered who will maintain them. A fair worry. We decided to use fewer screenshots and more plain descriptions of what to look for.
- Constructive: the access section still assumes prior knowledge. The cold reader who was asked to try it confirmed this and marked three places where they stalled. Those are being fixed.

After the demo, a short debrief with the two presenters. Both agreed that demos work best when the presenter lets the audience steer. Preparing a script is fine, but the real value is in the questions, and the best sessions were the ones where the script was abandoned halfway through. We discussed whether to formalise this, perhaps by always reserving half the time for questions, and decided to try it for the next few.

Other business from the day:

1. A decision was made about how to handle duplicate tickets. When two tickets describe the same problem, merge them and leave a note in both so the people who filed them know what happened. Previously duplicates were just closed, which left submitters wondering whether they had been ignored.
2. Diego Ferreira raised the idea of a short glossary for support terms, since newcomers keep asking what certain labels mean. Everyone agreed it was a good idea, and the first draft will be tiny, a dozen terms at most, with more added as questions come up.
3. The question of who covers support during holidays came up. There is a rough rota, but it is informal. We will write it down so it survives people forgetting.

There was also a lighter moment. Someone found an old version of the onboarding guide that told newcomers to email an address that no longer exists. It had apparently been the official advice for a very long time. The page has been corrected and, for amusement, the old version has been archived with a note on top reading "do not follow".

On process, we noticed that our two main chores, support sweeps and guide upkeep, have the same weakness: they depend on one person remembering. The proposal is to put both on the shared calendar as recurring items with a named owner and a named backup. It is not exciting, but it is the type of change that prevents next month's backlog.

I will finish the section with a reminder to anyone reading: if you spot something in the guide that is wrong, fix it directly and say what you changed in the history note. No need to ask permission.

## Friday close-out

The week is ending quietly, which is a pleasant change. This section covers the leftover items and a few reflections before the weekend.

The first thing to record is the state of the support queue. After Monday's sweep and a bit of steady upkeep, it is at a level where nobody is embarrassed by it. We agreed on a simple rule: any ticket with no reply for a few working days gets looked at by whoever is on duty, regardless of who owns it. The goal is not to remove ownership but to prevent tickets from being quietly orphaned. Diego Ferreira has volunteered to review how it goes after a couple of weeks and report back whether the rule is too blunt.

On onboarding, the first-week guide has been merged and announced. Aiko Osei sent a short message pointing people at it and asking for corrections. By the end of the day two small ones had come in, a typo and a broken link, both fixed within minutes. That is the kind of response I hoped for, because it shows people are actually reading it and feel free to edit.

We talked briefly about metrics. Someone asked whether we should track how long new joiners take to feel productive. It is an appealing idea, but the group was cautious. Any number we pick will be a rough proxy, and rough proxies tend to get gamed. The suggestion that won out was to ask newcomers a plain question at the end of their first month: what would you change about your first week? Written answers, no scale. We can read them and judge for ourselves.

Chores and bits:

- Archived the old planning folder. Nothing lost; it is just out of the way.
- Rotated the shared credentials for the support tooling, which had been overdue. Everyone was told through the usual route and nobody reported a problem.
- Updated the team page so the responsibilities listed match who actually does what, including support and onboarding.
- Cleared out the stale items in the "someday" list. About half were obsolete, and a few were quietly done already.

On a more reflective note, this was a week where small, unglamorous maintenance paid off. Nothing dramatic shipped, but the queue is calmer, the guide is better, and two demos got people talking to each other. It is easy to undervalue that kind of week while it is happening. A log is a good place to note that something worked even if it did not feel exciting.

One concern I will flag so it is not forgotten: both the support sweep and the guide upkeep are assigned to named people, but there is still no written backup for either. If one of those people is away, the work stalls. This was raised earlier in the week and agreed as a fix, yet it has not actually been done. I have put it at the top of the list for Monday.

Next entry should cover how the cold-reader feedback shaped the second draft of the guide, whether the duplicate-ticket habit has stuck, and who has agreed to be the backup for each chore. Have a good weekend, everyone.
