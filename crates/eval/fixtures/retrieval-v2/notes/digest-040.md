# Working notes — 29 March 2026

A slow Sunday evening, which is when I do my best thinking about the week ahead and my worst thinking about almost everything else. Notes below are a mix of what happened and what I intend to do about it.

Let me begin with the calendar, since it is the thing most likely to bite us. Cedar Core held its quarterly retrospective on 13 October 2027, and the team wanted that recorded in the planning sheet so that future quarters line up with it. I know that is a long way off from where we are sitting, but the group asked for the date to be fixed early and the planning sheet is where such things live. I have added it, with a note explaining how the cadence is meant to work, so that whoever inherits the sheet does not have to guess. It is a small thing, but dates that are written down tend to survive; dates that are remembered tend not to.

On the people side, Noor Okafor completed 21 hours of training this term, mostly in analytics, and has been trying out a couple of the techniques on real work. The most visible result is a tidier weekly summary, which is shorter than the old one and, as far as I can tell, answers more of the questions people used to ask afterwards. Noor has offered to share what was learned at a short session. I told Noor to take the credit for it, and that the session could be as informal as a lunchtime chat. I would rather people hear a few practical tips than sit through a presentation.

Other business from the week:

- The planning meeting on Tuesday ran well over time, because a debate about naming conventions turned into a debate about everything. We agreed to take naming offline and decide by comment on a shared page.
- The new starter checklist was tried for the first time, and it works, though two of the steps are in the wrong order. Fixed.
- A request came in for a quieter place to take calls. We have a spare corner that could be curtained off, and I will look into prices without promising anything.
- The weekly summary email now goes out on Thursdays instead of Fridays, since nobody reads email on Fridays.

A note about morale. People are in decent spirits, though there is a mild tiredness that I think comes from the long stretch between holidays. We cannot do much about that, but we can avoid piling on new initiatives. I have asked each group to name one thing they would stop doing if they could. The answers so far are revealing: mostly reports that nobody reads and meetings with no decisions. I would like to act on at least two of them.

The week ahead is lighter than usual, which is a gift. I plan to use the spare time to catch up on documentation, to read through the pile of notes from the past month, and to resist the temptation to schedule anything on Wednesday afternoon. A free afternoon is more valuable than it looks.

## Midweek notes

Wednesday, and the free afternoon did, in fact, remain free. I spent it reading, which felt almost illicit. A few things have emerged since the weekend.

The naming-convention debate moved to the shared page, and it is going better in writing than it did in the room. Written comments are calmer and more specific. Two clear camps have formed: one wants names that describe what a thing does, the other wants names that describe where it lives. Reading both sets of arguments, I think a hybrid will win out, and I have suggested that the people most invested draft a short proposal together. If they can agree on a page, the rest of us can live with it. If they cannot, I will make a call, which nobody will enjoy.

Noor Okafor shared a few practical tips from the recent training over lunch, as promised, and it turned out to be exactly the right format. About a dozen people drifted over with their sandwiches and stayed. The tips were small but useful: how to frame a question before starting an analysis, how to check that a chart is saying what you think it says, and how to write a summary for someone who has five minutes. I came away with a note to myself about the third one in particular, because I tend to write for people who have an hour. Noor has put the slides in the shared space, though I suspect the conversation was more valuable than the slides.

Cedar Core had a short planning session, mainly to talk about the billing calendar and how it connects to the retrospective date we recorded. They wanted to make sure the retrospective does not land on top of another busy period, and the sheet now shows both side by side. It is a minor adjustment, but it avoids the sort of clash that is irritating to discover late. The group also asked whether future retrospectives could include a short section on what they decided to stop doing, which I think is a lovely idea and have offered to help them build a template.

Small things worth noting:

- The call corner has been measured. A curtain would cost less than I feared, but the acoustics are the real problem, and a curtain will not fix them.
- The Thursday summary email is now scheduled, and a test copy went out to a few people without incident.
- A broken chair was reported, then fixed, then reported again. The cycle continues.
- Someone brought in biscuits. Productivity rose briefly.

One concern I want to put on record. We are asking a lot of people to document things, to write proposals, to share what they have learned, and to attend sessions. Each request is reasonable, but together they add up. I would like us to be honest about what we are asking and to protect time for it, rather than assuming it will happen in the margins. If we do not, the documentation will be the first casualty and we will be back where we started.

Tomorrow looks calm. I will use it to start on the template for the retrospective and to chase the naming proposal.

## End of week

Friday afternoon and the building has the pleasantly empty feel it gets just before the weekend. A good time to close out the week's log.

The naming proposal arrived this morning. It is a page and a half, which is about right, and it makes a reasonable case for the hybrid approach. A few people have already added comments, mostly small edits, and nobody has objected to the main idea. I suspect the quiet agreement is partly relief that the argument is nearly over. I will give it until Monday for final remarks, then mark it as agreed and ask someone to apply it to the shared pages over the following fortnight. It will be dull work, but dull and consistent beats clever and confusing.

The retrospective template for Cedar Core is in rough shape. It has four prompts: what went well, what we would do differently, what we would stop doing, and what we are unsure about. The last one was added at the group's suggestion, and I think it is the most interesting, because it invites people to admit uncertainty without it sounding like a failure. I sent the draft round for comments and will tidy it next week. I also suggested that whoever runs the session send the prompts out in advance, so that people can think before they arrive and the loudest voices do not set the tone.

Noor Okafor mentioned that the tidier weekly summary format is now being used by two other groups, and asked whether it would be worth writing down the reasoning behind it. I said yes, as long as it stays short. A format without its rationale tends to get copied badly, with the useful bits dropped and the decorative bits kept. The rationale can sit at the bottom of the same page, for anyone who wants it.

Housekeeping:

- The curtain idea for the call corner has been shelved. We will try putting a sign up and see whether people respect it.
- The chair has been replaced with a sturdier one. Let us hope.
- The Thursday summary email went out on time this week, and a couple of people actually replied.
- Biscuit supplies are low. This is not an emergency, but it is being monitored.

A small, slightly sentimental reflection. When I started keeping these notes, I thought they would be a record of decisions. They have turned out to be more of a record of the texture of work: what we argued about, what we got tired of, what made us laugh. The decisions are in other places, and the notes fill in the human side. I think that is worth keeping, even if no one reads them for a year.

Plans for next week: close the naming proposal, polish the template, look at the stop-doing list, and take another free afternoon if the calendar allows. I will report back on Sunday, when my thinking is at its best.
