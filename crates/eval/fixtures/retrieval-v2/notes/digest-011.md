# Weekly digest — 14 September 2025

Sunday catch-up, assembled from the week's chat threads and scribbled sync notes. It was a decent week overall, with fewer fires than usual, which gave people room to think about the longer term.

## Learning and development

Training was the main theme. The term is well underway, so we took stock of how much people have managed so far. Basalt Core completed 44 hours of training this term, and Sable Core completed 50 hours of training this term. Both groups are happy with how it went, though both said the same thing: the hours were found by ringfencing time in advance, not by hoping gaps would appear. That is a lesson worth passing on to anyone still struggling to fit learning in. If it is not in the calendar, it does not happen.

The procurement side spent its hours mostly on contract handling and supplier communication, the sort of knowledge that is hard to pick up from documents alone. Compliance went deeper on the regulatory side, including some material that was new to most of the team. Both said they would share what they learned, and short write-ups are expected on the wiki soon.

## Scheduling

Tomas Kowalski reported on the calendar for the coming weeks. The pattern is a familiar one: crowded in the middle, quieter at the edges. The suggestion is to move a couple of flexible items to the quieter days, which should relieve some pressure without requiring anyone to cancel anything. He asks that people confirm their availability by midweek so that clashes can be spotted early.

A small but welcome change: the scheduling page now shows who is covering for whom, so you no longer need to ask around to find out. If your arrangements change, please update it, because it is only useful while it is accurate.

## Notes on process

We briefly talked about how to run the weekly sync more efficiently. Too much of the time goes on updates that could have been written. The proposal is to ask for written updates in advance, and use the meeting only for questions and decisions. Several people said this would suit them, particularly those who find it hard to speak up in a larger group. We will try it for a few weeks and see.

There was also a request to keep a running list of "things we decided not to do", since those are often forgotten and rediscovered later. A short page on the wiki should do. It is surprisingly useful to know that an idea was considered and rejected, and why.

Next week looks busy but manageable. The main events are a planning session, the first written-update sync, and the descaling.

## Feedback, and a few loose ends

The satisfaction survey for scheduling came back, and Tomas Kowalski received a satisfaction rating of 7.5 out of 10. That is a solid result for a job that tends to attract complaints when it goes wrong and silence when it goes right. The comments were largely about communication: people like the outcome of the scheduling but would like earlier notice when something shifts. That fits with what we have seen in the calendar, and the new coverage page should help. Tomas is going to follow up with a couple of the respondents to understand what "earlier" means in practice, since it differs a good deal from one person to the next.

It is worth noting the usual caution about numbers. A single survey is a snapshot, and a good score is a reason to keep doing what is working, not a reason to relax. We will look at the next round before reading anything into small movements.

## Compliance and procurement notes

Sable Core has been looking at how records are kept, with a view to making them easier to find during a review. The group's idea is a simple index: one table listing what exists, where it is, and who is responsible for it. Not a new system, just a map. Basalt Core likes the idea and would like to borrow it for supplier paperwork, which has the same problem of things living in too many places.

A short conversation followed about how the two groups can share more. They overlap more than people realise, since purchasing decisions often have compliance implications and vice versa. A monthly half-hour conversation was suggested, with no agenda beyond "what are you working on that might affect us?". It is cheap and probably worthwhile.

## Reflections

A recurring pattern in this log is that the useful changes are small and specific. This week's examples: a coverage page, an index of records, a written-update sync. None of them required approval or a budget, and each addresses something people had been grumbling about for months. It is a reasonable argument for acting on irritations as soon as they are named, not saving them up for a big review.

On a lighter note, the descaling went ahead, the coffee is back, and morale has recovered. A small thing, but not a trivial one.

That is it for this week. See everyone on Monday, and please remember to send your written updates before the sync.
