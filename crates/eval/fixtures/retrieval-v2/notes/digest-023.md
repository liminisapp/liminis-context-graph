# Sync notes — 28 September 2025

Sunday evening, writing up the sync from Friday while I still remember most of it. It was one of those meetings that felt unremarkable at the time and, read back, contains quite a few decisions.

We began with the sprint numbers, because that is how we always begin. The Helix Core backlog stood at 214 open tickets at the end of the sprint. A few people winced, but the discussion was calmer than usual. Much of the backlog is long-lived: requests that were reasonable when they were raised and have since lost their urgency, or duplicates that nobody merged. Someone proposed a regular "backlog gardening" slot, perhaps a short block once a fortnight, where we look at the oldest items and either close them, rewrite them or schedule them. The team liked the idea. It does not reduce the work, but it makes the list honest, and an honest list is easier to plan from. Whoever owns the board will set it up.

Bram Tanaka covered the compliance side. In his words, he logged 40 incidents during the quarter, which is a number he wanted to put in context. Most were minor items, a missing signature here or an outdated record there, and the reason the count is relatively high is that he has been recording things he would earlier have simply fixed and forgotten. The recording has made patterns visible: the same few causes show up repeatedly. He wants to deal with those first, and asked for help from anyone who touches the processes involved. A couple of people offered, and a short working session will be held in the coming days.

We also talked about how to avoid using these numbers as scores. Everyone seemed to agree that a backlog count or an incident tally tells you something about the system, not about a person. It would be easy to slip into comparing, and the effort to resist that should be deliberate. I suggested adding a line to the top of the sprint summary reminding readers what the numbers are for, and nobody argued.

Planning for the next sprint was lighter. The team intends to pick fewer items and finish them properly, rather than start many and carry them forward. That is a familiar aspiration, but this time we agreed to say no to new requests unless they are urgent, and to keep a visible list of what was declined. The list is useful for explaining ourselves later.

A few smaller things before we closed. The meeting room booking tool has been misbehaving and needs resetting. The team wiki has some pages with broken links, which Bram will repair when time allows. And the next sync will start ten minutes later to accommodate someone's commute.

There was a brief mention of a possible lunch next month, which met with general approval and no actual planning. It will happen when it happens.

That is the sync. Corrections welcome, as ever, and apologies for the length.

## Training and the week ahead

Second half of the sync notes, covering training, upkeep and the coming week, written the following morning after I had slept on it.

Training took more time than I expected. Helix Core completed 7 hours of training this term, and the question was whether that is enough. The group responsible for analytics has been busy with the sprint, and learning is the thing that usually gets squeezed first. Seven hours is not nothing, but it falls short of what people hoped for at the start of the term. Instead of being pessimistic, we asked what was most useful in the time that was spent. The answer was a session on reading data more carefully, which several people said changed how they look at charts. We decided to repeat that session for anyone who missed it and to schedule it early next term rather than leaving it to chance.

Bram Tanaka made a related point from the compliance side: records of training are part of what gets checked, so it helps to write down what was done, when, and by whom, even when the course itself was short. He offered a simple one-line format for the log and said he would be happy to check entries if people send them his way. It costs a minute per entry and spares a headache later, which is a good trade.

On the week ahead, the plan is to pick up the backlog gardening slot and run the first session. We will start with the oldest items and give ourselves a time limit, so it does not swallow the afternoon. If it works, the slot becomes regular. If it does not, we will say so and try something else. We also agreed to set aside some time for Bram's working session on recurring incident causes, which will be done as a small group instead of the whole team.

Upkeep. The wiki links are being repaired in order of how often the pages get visited, which seemed sensible. A new person is expected to join the shared channel soon, so we should check that the onboarding notes are current. I will read through them this week and mark anything out of date. The shared drive has the usual clutter, and we agreed to leave it for now.

One thing worth recording as a decision: the team will not take on new standing meetings without dropping an existing one. The calendar is full enough already, and a simple one-in, one-out rule is easier to follow than a long argument each time. Nobody objected, and a couple of people looked visibly relieved.

I will close the notes here. The next sync is in two weeks, and by then the gardening slot should have produced some results, whether good or instructive.
